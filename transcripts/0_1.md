# 0.1 Right-Triangle Trigonometry

## How tall is the tree
**On screen:** A tree in a park. A surveyor's sighting tube stands 20 m from its base, aimed at the top; the line of sight and the ground make a 35° angle. A right triangle is outlined: ground 20 m, height h, angle 35°.
> You can't run a tape measure up a tree. But you can walk twenty meters away and measure the angle up to the top: thirty-five degrees. [pause]
> That makes a right triangle: the ground, the tree, and your line of sight. You know one side and one angle. [pause]
> Trigonometry turns that one side and one angle into the height of the tree. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Lesson 1 · Right-Triangle Trigonometry." Trig Review · a free module.

## Naming the sides
**On screen:** A right triangle with the right angle marked by a small square. The side across from it lights up: "hypotenuse (longest side)". An angle θ is marked at the left corner. The leg across from θ lights up "opposite"; the leg touching θ lights up "adjacent". Then θ moves to the top corner, and the two leg labels swap.
> Every right triangle has one ninety degree angle. The side across from it is the longest side, and it's called the hypotenuse. [pause]
> Now pick one of the other two angles and call it theta. The leg across the triangle from theta is the opposite side. The leg that touches theta is the adjacent side. [pause]
> Opposite and adjacent depend on which angle you pick. Move theta to the other corner and the two legs trade names. The hypotenuse never changes. [long pause]

## Same angle, same ratios
**On screen:** Three right triangles with a 35° angle, nested at the same corner: legs 2 and 1.4, 3 and 2.1, 4 and 2.8. Beside them a table: adjacent, opposite, opposite ÷ adjacent, with 0.7 in every row.
> Here are three right triangles of different sizes, all with a thirty-five degree angle. They have the same shape. Only the size changes. [pause]
> Divide the opposite side by the adjacent side in each one. One point four over two, two point one over three, two point eight over four. Every one comes out to about zero point seven. [pause]
> So the ratio depends only on the angle, not on how big the triangle is. That's why these ratios get names. [long pause]

## SOH CAH TOA
**On screen:** A right triangle with θ, opposite, adjacent and hypotenuse labeled. The three ratios appear one at a time: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent. Then "SOH CAH TOA" in a box.
> The three ratios are called sine, cosine, and tangent. [pause]
> Sine of theta is the opposite side over the hypotenuse. Cosine of theta is the adjacent side over the hypotenuse. Tangent of theta is the opposite side over the adjacent side. [pause]
> The usual memory aid is SOH CAH TOA: sine, opposite over hypotenuse. Cosine, adjacent over hypotenuse. Tangent, opposite over adjacent. [long pause]

## Ratios from sides
**On screen:** The problem: "A right triangle has legs 8 and 15. θ is the angle opposite the side of length 8. Find sin θ, cos θ, and tan θ." The triangle with θ marked. Board: hypotenuse² = 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289; hypotenuse = 17; sin θ = 8/17; cos θ = 15/17; tan θ = 8/15.
> A right triangle has legs eight and fifteen, and theta is the angle opposite the side of length eight. Find sine, cosine, and tangent of theta. [pause]
> First the hypotenuse. Its square is eight squared plus fifteen squared: sixty-four plus two twenty-five, which is two eighty-nine. [pause]
> So the hypotenuse is seventeen. [pause]
> Sine is opposite over hypotenuse: eight seventeenths.
> Cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse: fifteen seventeenths. [pause]
> Tangent is opposite over adjacent: eight fifteenths. [long pause]

## The reciprocal ratios
**On screen:** The three ratios from before, each with its flip written under it: csc θ = hypotenuse/opposite = 1/sin θ (cosecant), sec θ = hypotenuse/adjacent = 1/cos θ (secant), cot θ = adjacent/opposite = 1/tan θ (cotangent). A memory tip: "each pair has exactly one 'co': sine with cosecant, cosine with secant." Then the 8-15-17 triangle with csc θ = 17/8, sec θ = 17/15, cot θ = 15/8.
> Flip each ratio upside down and you get three more. Cosecant is the hypotenuse over the opposite side, so it's one over sine. [pause]
> Secant is the hypotenuse over the adjacent side, one over cosine. Cotangent is adjacent over opposite, one over tangent. [pause]
> The names pair up oddly, but each pair has exactly one "co" in it. Sine goes with cosecant, and cosine goes with secant. [pause]
> In the triangle with sides eight, fifteen, and seventeen, cosecant is seventeen eighths, secant is seventeen fifteenths, and cotangent is fifteen eighths. [long pause]

## Finding a side
**On screen:** The problem: "From 20 m away, the angle up to the top of a tree is 35°. How tall is the tree?" The tree picture from the opening. Board: h is opposite 35°, 20 m is adjacent; tan 35° = h/20; h = 20 tan 35°; ≈ 20(0.7002) (degree mode); ≈ 14.0 m.
> From twenty meters away, the angle up to the top of a tree is thirty-five degrees. How tall is the tree? [pause]
> Name the sides from the thirty-five degree angle. The height h is opposite it, and the twenty meters along the ground is adjacent. [pause]
> Opposite and adjacent go together in tangent. So tangent of thirty-five degrees equals h over twenty. [pause]
> Multiply both sides by twenty: h is twenty times tangent of thirty-five degrees. [pause]
> Your calculator has to be in degree mode for this. Tangent of thirty-five degrees is about zero point seven zero zero two. [pause]
> So the tree is about fourteen point zero meters tall. [long pause]

## Working back to the angle
**On screen:** "tan 35° ≈ 0.7002" with an arrow back: "tan⁻¹(0.7002) ≈ 35°". The three inverse keys: sin⁻¹, cos⁻¹, tan⁻¹, "ratio in, angle out". Then a person looking up at a rooftop, with the horizontal and the line of sight: "angle of elevation". The same picture from the roof looking down: "angle of depression".
> Sometimes you know the sides and want the angle. Then you run a ratio backward. [pause]
> Tangent takes thirty-five degrees to about zero point seven zero zero two. The inverse tangent key, written tan to the negative one, goes the other way: give it zero point seven zero zero two and it gives back thirty-five degrees. [pause]
> Sine and cosine have inverse keys too. Each one takes a ratio in and gives an angle out. The last lesson of this module covers them in detail. [pause]
> Word problems often name the angle between the horizontal and a line of sight. Looking up, it's the angle of elevation. Looking down, it's the angle of depression. [long pause]

## Finding an angle
**On screen:** The problem: "Priya stands 50 m from the base of a building that is 30 m tall. Find the angle of elevation from where she stands to the top of the building." Priya, the building, and the right triangle. Board: 30 m is opposite θ, 50 m is adjacent; tan θ = 30/50 = 0.6; θ = tan⁻¹(0.6); ≈ 31.0°.
> Priya stands fifty meters from the base of a building that is thirty meters tall. Find the angle of elevation from where she stands to the top of the building. [pause]
> The thirty meter height is opposite the angle, and the fifty meters along the ground is adjacent. [pause]
> So tangent of theta is thirty over fifty, which is zero point six. [pause]
> Now run it backward: theta is the inverse tangent of zero point six. [pause]
> In degree mode, that's about thirty-one point zero degrees. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "sin θ = opp/hyp, cos θ = adj/hyp, tan θ = opp/adj". "csc θ = hyp/opp, sec θ = hyp/adj, cot θ = adj/opp". "Find a side: the ratio that links the side you know to the side you want." "Find an angle: an inverse key on the ratio."
> In a right triangle, sine is opposite over hypotenuse, cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse, and tangent is opposite over adjacent. Cosecant, secant, and cotangent are those ratios flipped. To find a side, use the ratio that links the side you know to the side you want, then solve. To find an angle, use an inverse key on the ratio. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: The ladder
**On screen:** "Marisol leans a 6 m ladder against a wall. It makes a 70° angle with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?" A ladder against a brick wall. Board: the ladder is the hypotenuse, h is opposite 70°; sin 70° = h/6; h = 6 sin 70°; ≈ 6(0.9397); ≈ 5.64 m.
> Marisol leans a six meter ladder against a wall. It makes a seventy degree angle with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach? [pause]
> The ladder is the hypotenuse, and the height h is opposite the seventy degree angle. Opposite over hypotenuse is sine. [pause]
> So sine of seventy degrees equals h over six. Multiply by six: h is six sine seventy degrees.
> Sine of seventy degrees is about zero point nine three nine seven. [pause]
> Six times that is about five point six four meters. [long pause]

## Example 2: The kite string
**On screen:** "Jordan's kite is 30 m above the ground. They hold the string at ground level, and it makes a 40° angle with the ground. How long is the string?" Jordan, the kite, the string as the hypotenuse. Board: 30 m is opposite 40°, the string L is the hypotenuse; sin 40° = 30/L; L sin 40° = 30; L = 30/sin 40°; ≈ 30/0.6428; ≈ 46.7 m.
> Jordan's kite is thirty meters above the ground. They hold the string at ground level, and it makes a forty degree angle with the ground. How long is the string? [pause]
> The thirty meters is opposite the forty degree angle, and the string is the hypotenuse. So sine of forty degrees equals thirty over L. [pause]
> This time the unknown is on the bottom of the fraction. Multiply both sides by L first: L sine forty degrees equals thirty. [pause]
> Then divide both sides by sine of forty degrees: L is thirty over sine forty degrees. [pause]
> Sine of forty degrees is about zero point six four two eight, so L is about forty-six point seven meters. [pause]
> That's longer than thirty, as it should be. The hypotenuse is always the longest side. [long pause]

## Example 3: A skateboard ramp
**On screen:** "Deshawn builds a skateboard ramp. Its sloped surface is 4 m long, and its high end is 1.2 m off the ground. What angle does the ramp make with the ground?" The ramp drawn as a wedge. Board: 1.2 m is opposite θ, 4 m is the hypotenuse; sin θ = 1.2/4 = 0.3; θ = sin⁻¹(0.3); ≈ 17.5°.
> Deshawn builds a skateboard ramp. Its sloped surface is four meters long, and its high end is one point two meters off the ground. What angle does the ramp make with the ground? [pause]
> The one point two meter height is opposite the angle, and the four meter surface is the hypotenuse. [pause]
> Opposite over hypotenuse is sine: sine of theta is one point two over four, which is zero point three.
> Run it backward with inverse sine: theta is sine inverse of zero point three. [pause]
> In degree mode, that's about seventeen point five degrees. [long pause]

## Example 4: Solving a whole triangle
**On screen:** "A right triangle has hypotenuse 12 and an acute angle of 25°. Find the other acute angle and the lengths of both legs." The triangle, with a the leg opposite 25° and b the leg adjacent. Board: other angle = 90° − 25° = 65°; sin 25° = a/12, a = 12 sin 25° ≈ 5.07; cos 25° = b/12, b = 12 cos 25° ≈ 10.88.
> A right triangle has hypotenuse twelve and an acute angle of twenty-five degrees. Find the other acute angle and the lengths of both legs. [pause]
> The three angles of a triangle add to a hundred eighty degrees, and the right angle uses ninety. So the two acute angles add to ninety, and the other one is ninety minus twenty-five: sixty-five degrees. [pause]
> Call the leg opposite twenty-five degrees a. Opposite over hypotenuse is sine: sine of twenty-five degrees is a over twelve, so a is twelve sine twenty-five degrees, about five point zero seven.
> Call the adjacent leg b. Adjacent over hypotenuse is cosine: cosine of twenty-five degrees is b over twelve, so b is twelve cosine twenty-five degrees, about ten point eight eight. [pause]
> A quick check: five point zero seven squared plus ten point eight eight squared is about one forty-four, which is twelve squared. [long pause]
