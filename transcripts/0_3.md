# 0.3 The Six Trigonometric Functions

## How tall is the tree
**On screen:** A tree in a park. A surveyor stands 20 m from its base with a sighting tube aimed at the top; the line of sight and the ground make a 35° angle. A right triangle is outlined: ground 20 m, height h, angle 35°.
> You can't run a tape measure up a tree. But you can walk twenty meters away and measure the angle up to the top: thirty-five degrees. [pause]
> That's a right triangle with one side and one angle known. The angle fixes the shape, and the shape fixes the ratio of the height to the ground distance. [pause]
> The trig functions are those ratios. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 0.3 · The Six Trigonometric Functions." Unit 0 · Trig Review.

## From the circle to any triangle
**On screen:** The unit circle's reference triangle at angle θ: hypotenuse 1, legs cos θ and sin θ. It scales up smoothly by a factor r: hypotenuse r, legs r cos θ and r sin θ. Then the ratios: sin θ = opposite/hypotenuse, cos θ = adjacent/hypotenuse, tan θ = opposite/adjacent, and the mnemonic "SOH CAH TOA".
> On the unit circle, the triangle at angle theta has hypotenuse one, a horizontal leg of cosine theta, and a vertical leg of sine theta. [pause]
> Scale that triangle up by any factor r. The angle stays the same, the hypotenuse becomes r, and the legs become r cosine theta and r sine theta. [pause]
> So in any right triangle, sine is the opposite side over the hypotenuse. Cosine is the adjacent side over the hypotenuse. Tangent is opposite over adjacent. [pause]
> The usual memory aid is SOH CAH TOA: sine, opposite over hypotenuse; cosine, adjacent over hypotenuse; tangent, opposite over adjacent. [long pause]

## Ratios from sides
**On screen:** The problem: "A right triangle has legs 8 and 15. θ is the angle opposite the side of length 8. Find sin θ, cos θ, and tan θ." The triangle with θ marked. Board: hypotenuse² = 8² + 15² = 64 + 225 = 289; hypotenuse = 17; sin θ = 8/17; cos θ = 15/17; tan θ = 8/15.
> A right triangle has legs eight and fifteen, and theta is the angle opposite the side of length eight. Find sine, cosine, and tangent of theta. [pause]
> First the hypotenuse. Its square is eight squared plus fifteen squared: sixty-four plus two twenty-five, which is two eighty-nine. [pause]
> So the hypotenuse is seventeen. [pause]
> Sine is opposite over hypotenuse: eight seventeenths.
> Cosine is adjacent over hypotenuse: fifteen seventeenths. [pause]
> Tangent is opposite over adjacent: eight fifteenths. [long pause]

## Any angle, any point
**On screen:** Axes. A point (x, y) on the terminal side of θ, at distance r = √(x² + y²) from the origin, in quadrant II. Formulas: sin θ = y/r, cos θ = x/r, tan θ = y/x. The point (−3, 4) with r = 5 as a quick case: sin θ = 4/5, cos θ = −3/5.
> For angles past ninety degrees, use a point instead of a triangle. Pick any point x comma y on the terminal side, and call its distance from the origin r. [pause]
> Then sine theta is y over r, cosine theta is x over r, and tangent theta is y over x. The signs of x and y take care of the quadrant. [pause]
> For the point negative three comma four, r is five, so sine is four fifths and cosine is negative three fifths. [long pause]

## The reciprocal functions
**On screen:** Three new names appear under their partners: csc θ = 1/sin θ (cosecant), sec θ = 1/cos θ (secant), cot θ = 1/tan θ = cos θ/sin θ (cotangent). A memory tip: "each pair has exactly one 'co': sine with cosecant, cosine with secant."
> Three more functions are the reciprocals of these. Cosecant is one over sine. Secant is one over cosine. Cotangent is one over tangent, which is cosine over sine. [pause]
> The names pair up oddly, but each pair has exactly one "co" in it. Sine goes with cosecant, and cosine goes with secant. [pause]
> In a right triangle that makes cosecant the hypotenuse over the opposite side, secant the hypotenuse over the adjacent side, and cotangent adjacent over opposite. [long pause]

## Where they are undefined
**On screen:** A unit circle. The points at π/2 and 3π/2 glow (cos θ = 0): "tan θ and sec θ undefined". Then the points at 0 and π glow (sin θ = 0): "cot θ and csc θ undefined".
> A fraction is undefined when its denominator is zero. Tangent and secant both divide by cosine, so they're undefined wherever cosine is zero: pi over two, three pi over two, and so on. [pause]
> Cotangent and cosecant divide by sine, so they're undefined wherever sine is zero: zero, pi, two pi. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "SOH CAH TOA". "csc θ = 1/sin θ, sec θ = 1/cos θ, cot θ = 1/tan θ." "Point (x, y), distance r: sin θ = y/r, cos θ = x/r, tan θ = y/x." "tan, sec undefined where cos θ = 0; cot, csc where sin θ = 0."
> In a right triangle: sine is opposite over hypotenuse, cosine adjacent over hypotenuse, tangent opposite over adjacent. Cosecant, secant and cotangent are their reciprocals, and each pair has one "co". For any angle, use a point: sine is y over r, cosine is x over r. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Reciprocals at landmark angles
**On screen:** "Find sec(5π/4) and csc(5π/6)." Board: cos(5π/4) = −√2/2; sec(5π/4) = 1/(−√2/2) = −2/√2; = −√2; sin(5π/6) = ½; csc(5π/6) = 2.
> Find the secant of five pi over four and the cosecant of five pi over six. [pause]
> Secant is one over cosine. Five pi over four has reference angle pi over four in the third quadrant, so its cosine is negative root two over two. [pause]
> One over that is negative two over root two. Two over root two is root two, so the secant is negative root two.
> Cosecant is one over sine. Sine of five pi over six is one half. [pause]
> So the cosecant is two. [long pause]

## Example 2: The tree
**On screen:** The tree picture again: ground 20 m, angle 35°, height h. Board: tan 35° = h/20; h = 20 tan 35°; ≈ 20(0.7002); ≈ 14.0 m. A note: "calculator in degree mode".
> From twenty meters away, the angle up to the top of the tree is thirty-five degrees. How tall is the tree? [pause]
> The height is opposite the angle and the twenty meters is adjacent, so use tangent: tangent of thirty-five degrees equals h over twenty. [pause]
> Multiply both sides by twenty: h is twenty tangent thirty-five degrees.
> On a calculator in degree mode, tangent of thirty-five degrees is about point seven zero zero two. [pause]
> So the tree is about fourteen point zero meters tall. [long pause]

## Example 3: The ladder
**On screen:** "A 6 m ladder leans against a wall, making a 70° angle with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach?" A ladder against a brick wall. Board: sin 70° = h/6; h = 6 sin 70°; ≈ 6(0.9397); ≈ 5.64 m.
> A six meter ladder leans against a wall, making a seventy degree angle with the ground. How high up the wall does it reach? [pause]
> The ladder is the hypotenuse, and the height is opposite the angle. Opposite over hypotenuse is sine: sine of seventy degrees equals h over six. [pause]
> So h is six sine seventy degrees.
> Sine of seventy degrees is about point nine three nine seven. [pause]
> Six times that is about five point six four meters. [long pause]

## Example 4: From tangent to the rest
**On screen:** "tan θ = 2 and θ is in quadrant III. Find sin θ and sec θ." Axes with the point (−1, −2) on the terminal side. Board: tan θ = y/x = −2/−1; point (−1, −2); r = √(1 + 4) = √5; sin θ = y/r = −2/√5 = −2√5/5; sec θ = r/x = √5/(−1) = −√5.
> The tangent of theta is two, and theta is in the third quadrant. Find sine theta and secant theta. [pause]
> Tangent is y over x. In the third quadrant both are negative, so write two as negative two over negative one. That puts the point negative one comma negative two on the terminal side. [pause]
> Its distance from the origin is the square root of one plus four: root five.
> Sine is y over r: negative two over root five. Rationalized, that's negative two root five over five. [pause]
> Secant is one over cosine, so it's r over x: root five over negative one, which is negative root five. [long pause]
