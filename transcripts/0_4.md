# 0.4 The Six Trigonometric Functions

## A point off the unit circle
**On screen:** Axes with the unit circle and a larger circle of radius 5. A ray from the origin into quadrant II passes through the unit circle and then through the point (−3, 4) on the big circle. The point (−3, 4) slides along the ray down to the unit circle, its coordinates shrinking to (−3/5, 4/5), with "÷ 5" beside the arrow.
> On the unit circle, the coordinates of a point are the cosine and sine of its angle. But most points aren't on the unit circle. [pause]
> The point negative three comma four is five units from the origin. Divide both coordinates by five and it slides along the same ray onto the unit circle, at negative three fifths comma four fifths. [pause]
> So this angle has cosine negative three fifths and sine four fifths. Any point on the terminal side gives the trig functions, once you know its distance from the origin. [long pause]

## Any angle, any point
**On screen:** Axes. A point (x, y) on the terminal side of θ in quadrant II, at distance r from the origin; "r = √(x² + y²)". The point divided by r lands on the unit circle at (x/r, y/r). Formulas: sin θ = y/r, cos θ = x/r, tan θ = y/x. Then a quadrant I point with its triangle: y labeled "opposite", x "adjacent", r "hypotenuse".
> Pick any point x comma y on the terminal side of theta, and call its distance from the origin r. By the Pythagorean theorem, r is the square root of x squared plus y squared. [pause]
> Divide both coordinates by r and the point lands on the unit circle. So cosine theta is x over r, and sine theta is y over r. Tangent, sine over cosine, is y over x. [pause]
> In the first quadrant this is SOH CAH TOA again: y is the opposite side, x is the adjacent side, and r is the hypotenuse. In the other quadrants, the signs of x and y give the signs. [long pause]

## A point on the terminal side
**On screen:** The problem: "The point (−5, 12) is on the terminal side of θ. Find sin θ, cos θ, and tan θ." Axes with the point and the ray. Board: r = √(25 + 144) = √169 = 13; sin θ = y/r = 12/13; cos θ = x/r = −5/13; tan θ = y/x = 12/(−5) = −12/5.
> The point negative five comma twelve is on the terminal side of theta. Find sine, cosine, and tangent of theta. [pause]
> First the distance r: the square root of twenty-five plus a hundred forty-four, which is root one sixty-nine, thirteen. [pause]
> Sine is y over r: twelve thirteenths. [pause]
> Cosine is x over r: negative five thirteenths. [pause]
> Tangent is y over x: twelve over negative five, negative twelve fifths. [pause]
> The point is in the second quadrant, so sine is positive and cosine is negative, just as the signs came out. [long pause]

## The reciprocal functions
**On screen:** Three names under their partners: csc θ = 1/sin θ = r/y (cosecant), sec θ = 1/cos θ = r/x (secant), cot θ = 1/tan θ = cos θ/sin θ = x/y (cotangent). For (−5, 12): csc θ = 13/12, sec θ = −13/5, cot θ = −5/12. Then "cos(π/3) = ½, so sec(π/3) = 2".
> In lesson one, cosecant, secant, and cotangent were the triangle ratios flipped over. The same definitions work for every angle. Cosecant is one over sine, secant is one over cosine, and cotangent is one over tangent, which is cosine over sine. [pause]
> With a point and its distance r, that makes cosecant r over y, secant r over x, and cotangent x over y. [pause]
> For the point negative five comma twelve, cosecant is thirteen twelfths, secant is negative thirteen fifths, and cotangent is negative five twelfths. [pause]
> At a landmark angle, flip the unit circle value. Cosine of pi over three is one half, so secant of pi over three is two. [long pause]

## Where they are undefined
**On screen:** A unit circle. The points at π/2 and 3π/2 glow (cos θ = 0): "tan θ and sec θ undefined". Then the points at 0 and π glow (sin θ = 0): "cot θ and csc θ undefined".
> A fraction is undefined when its denominator is zero. Tangent and secant both divide by cosine, so they're undefined wherever cosine is zero: pi over two, three pi over two, and so on. [pause]
> Cotangent and cosecant divide by sine, so they're undefined wherever sine is zero: zero, pi, two pi. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Point (x, y), distance r = √(x² + y²): sin θ = y/r, cos θ = x/r, tan θ = y/x." "csc θ = 1/sin θ = r/y, sec θ = 1/cos θ = r/x, cot θ = 1/tan θ = x/y." "tan, sec undefined where cos θ = 0; cot, csc where sin θ = 0."
> For any angle, take a point x comma y on its terminal side and its distance r. Sine is y over r, cosine is x over r, and tangent is y over x. Cosecant, secant, and cotangent are their reciprocals. Tangent and secant are undefined where cosine is zero, and cotangent and cosecant where sine is zero. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Reciprocals at landmark angles
**On screen:** "Find sec(5π/4) and csc(5π/6)." Board: cos(5π/4) = −√2/2; sec(5π/4) = 1/(−√2/2) = −2/√2; = −√2; sin(5π/6) = ½; csc(5π/6) = 2.
> Find the secant of five pi over four and the cosecant of five pi over six. [pause]
> Secant is one over cosine. Five pi over four has reference angle pi over four in the third quadrant, so its cosine is negative root two over two. [pause]
> One over that is negative two over root two. Two over root two is root two, so the secant is negative root two.
> Cosecant is one over sine. Sine of five pi over six is one half. [pause]
> So the cosecant is two. [long pause]

## Example 2: A point in quadrant IV
**On screen:** "The point (4, −3) is on the terminal side of θ. Find csc θ, sec θ, and cot θ." Axes with the point. Board: r = √(16 + 9) = 5; csc θ = r/y = 5/(−3) = −5/3; sec θ = r/x = 5/4; cot θ = x/y = 4/(−3) = −4/3.
> The point four comma negative three is on the terminal side of theta. Find cosecant, secant, and cotangent of theta. [pause]
> The distance r is the square root of sixteen plus nine: five. [pause]
> Cosecant is r over y: five over negative three, negative five thirds.
> Secant is r over x: five fourths. [pause]
> Cotangent is x over y: four over negative three, negative four thirds. [long pause]

## Example 3: From tangent to the rest
**On screen:** "tan θ = 2 and θ is in quadrant III. Find sin θ and sec θ." Axes with the point (−1, −2) on the terminal side. Board: tan θ = y/x = −2/−1; point (−1, −2); r = √(1 + 4) = √5; sin θ = y/r = −2/√5 = −2√5/5; sec θ = r/x = √5/(−1) = −√5.
> The tangent of theta is two, and theta is in the third quadrant. Find sine theta and secant theta. [pause]
> Tangent is y over x. In the third quadrant both are negative, so write two as negative two over negative one. That puts the point negative one comma negative two on the terminal side. [pause]
> Its distance from the origin is the square root of one plus four: root five.
> Sine is y over r: negative two over root five. Rationalized, that's negative two root five over five. [pause]
> Secant is one over cosine, so it's r over x: root five over negative one, which is negative root five. [long pause]

## Example 4: From cosine to the rest
**On screen:** "cos θ = −5/13 and θ is in quadrant II. Find tan θ and csc θ." Axes with the point (−5, 12). Board: cos θ = x/r = −5/13; point (−5, y) with r = 13; y² = 169 − 25 = 144; y = 12 (quadrant II: y > 0); tan θ = y/x = −12/5; csc θ = r/y = 13/12.
> The cosine of theta is negative five thirteenths, and theta is in the second quadrant. Find tangent theta and cosecant theta. [pause]
> Cosine is x over r, so take x equal to negative five and r equal to thirteen. [pause]
> Then y squared is thirteen squared minus five squared: a hundred sixty-nine minus twenty-five, a hundred forty-four.
> So y is plus or minus twelve. In the second quadrant y is positive, so y is twelve. [pause]
> Tangent is y over x: negative twelve fifths. Cosecant is r over y: thirteen twelfths. [long pause]
