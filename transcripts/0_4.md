# 0.4 Graphs of Sine, Cosine, and Tangent

## Unrolling the circle
**On screen:** A unit circle on the left, axes on the right. A point travels around the circle; a horizontal dashed line carries its height across to the graph, where a dot at (θ, sin θ) traces a wave as θ runs from 0 to 2π. Then the point keeps going and the wave repeats.
> Send a point around the unit circle and keep track of its height. Plot the height against the angle, and a wave comes out. [pause]
> One trip around the circle draws one full wave. Keep going and the same wave repeats forever. [pause]
> That repeating is why sine and cosine describe anything that cycles: tides, sound, seasons, a Ferris wheel. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 0.4 · Graphs of Sine, Cosine, and Tangent." Unit 0 · Trig Review.

## The graph of sine
**On screen:** y = sin x on [−2π, 2π] with π-labeled ticks. Labels appear: "period 2π" with a bracket over one wave; "range: −1 ≤ y ≤ 1"; zeros at 0, ±π, ±2π marked; the maximum (π/2, 1) and minimum (3π/2, −1) marked.
> Here's y equals sine x, with x in radians. It starts at zero, rises to one at pi over two, comes back to zero at pi, drops to negative one at three pi over two, and returns to zero at two pi. [pause]
> Then it repeats. The length of one full cycle is called the period, and sine's period is two pi. [pause]
> The outputs never go above one or below negative one. And sine is zero at every multiple of pi. [long pause]

## The graph of cosine
**On screen:** y = cos x drawn on the same axes, in a second color. It starts at (0, 1). A copy of the sine graph slides left by π/2 and lands exactly on the cosine graph: "cos x = sin(x + π/2)".
> Cosine starts at one, because the point at angle zero is one comma zero. It falls to zero at pi over two, reaches negative one at pi, and climbs back to one at two pi. [pause]
> It's the same wave as sine, slid a quarter period to the left. Same period, two pi, and same range. [long pause]

## Amplitude and period
**On screen:** y = sin x in gray, then y = 3 sin x (taller: "amplitude 3"), then y = sin 2x (squeezed: "period π"). A formula box: "y = A sin(Bx): amplitude |A|, period 2π/|B|".
> Multiplying the whole function by a number stretches it up and down. Three sine x swings between negative three and three. Half the distance from the top to the bottom is called the amplitude, so its amplitude is three. [pause]
> Multiplying x inside squeezes it sideways. Sine of two x runs through its cycle twice as fast, so it finishes one wave by x equals pi. [pause]
> In general, A sine B x has amplitude the absolute value of A and period two pi over the absolute value of B. The same goes for cosine. [long pause]

## Shifts
**On screen:** y = sin x slides right by π/4 ("x − C: shift right C") and then up by 2 ("+ D: midline y = D", the dashed midline drawn). Formula: "y = A sin(B(x − C)) + D".
> Adding a constant D outside lifts the whole wave, so it swings around the line y equals D. That line is called the midline. [pause]
> Replacing x with x minus C slides the wave C units to the right. [pause]
> So the general form is A sine of B times x minus C, plus D. Amplitude from A, period from B, shift from C, midline from D. [long pause]

## Describing a cosine graph
**On screen:** The problem: "For y = 3 cos(2x) + 1, find the amplitude, period, midline, maximum, and minimum." Board: amplitude |3| = 3; period 2π/2 = π; midline y = 1; maximum 1 + 3 = 4; minimum 1 − 3 = −2. The graph on [0, 2π] with the midline dashed and the max and min levels marked.
> For y equals three cosine of two x, plus one, find the amplitude, the period, the midline, and the maximum and minimum values. [try it]
> The amplitude is the absolute value of A: three. [pause]
> The period is two pi over B: two pi over two, which is pi. [pause]
> The midline is y equals D: y equals one.
> The wave reaches three above the midline: one plus three, a maximum of four. [pause]
> And three below: one minus three, a minimum of negative two. [long pause]

## The graph of tangent
**On screen:** y = tan x on (−3π/2, 3π/2): branches rising through the origin and through ±π, with dashed vertical asymptotes at ±π/2 and ±3π/2. Labels: "period π", "asymptotes where cos x = 0", "zeros where sin x = 0".
> Tangent is sine over cosine, so it's zero where sine is zero and undefined where cosine is zero. [pause]
> Near pi over two, cosine shrinks toward zero while sine is near one, so tangent goes off to infinity. The graph has a vertical asymptote there, and at every place cosine is zero. [pause]
> Each branch rises from negative infinity to infinity, and the pattern repeats every pi. Tangent's period is pi, half of sine's. [long pause]

## Secant, cosecant, cotangent
**On screen:** y = cos x dashed, with y = sec x in solid: U-shaped branches touching the cosine wave at its peaks and troughs, with asymptotes where cos x = 0. Then y = sin x dashed with y = csc x. A small note: "cot x = cos x / sin x: like tangent, falling, asymptotes at multiples of π".
> The reciprocal functions are easiest to picture from their partners. Secant is one over cosine, so where cosine is one, secant is one, and where cosine is near zero, secant goes off to infinity. [pause]
> That makes U-shaped branches that touch the cosine wave at its tops and bottoms, with asymptotes wherever cosine is zero. Cosecant does the same thing with sine. [pause]
> Cotangent looks like tangent turned around: falling branches, with asymptotes at the multiples of pi. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "sin, cos: period 2π, range [−1, 1]." "y = A sin(B(x − C)) + D: amplitude |A|, period 2π/|B|, shift C, midline y = D." "tan: period π, asymptotes where cos x = 0."
> Sine and cosine have period two pi and range from negative one to one. In A sine of B times x minus C, plus D, the amplitude is the absolute value of A, the period is two pi over the absolute value of B, the shift is C, and the midline is y equals D. Tangent has period pi and asymptotes where cosine is zero. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A reflected sine
**On screen:** "For y = −2 sin(πx/3) + 5, find the amplitude, period, midline, maximum, and minimum." Board: amplitude |−2| = 2; period 2π/(π/3) = 2π · 3/π = 6; midline y = 5; maximum 5 + 2 = 7; minimum 5 − 2 = 3; the negative A flips the wave: it starts at the midline going down. The graph on [0, 12].
> For y equals negative two sine of pi x over three, plus five, find the amplitude, period, midline, maximum, and minimum. [pause]
> The amplitude is the absolute value of negative two: two. Amplitude is a distance, so it's never negative. [pause]
> The period is two pi over pi over three. Dividing by a fraction is multiplying by its flip: two pi times three over pi, which is six.
> The midline is y equals five. So the maximum is five plus two, seven, and the minimum is five minus two, three. [pause]
> The negative sign flips the wave: instead of rising from the midline, it starts by going down. [long pause]

## Example 2: An equation from a graph
**On screen:** A graph of a cosine-shaped wave: a maximum of 5 at x = 0, a minimum of −1 at x = 2, the next maximum at x = 4. "Write an equation for this graph." Board: midline D = (5 + (−1))/2 = 2; amplitude A = (5 − (−1))/2 = 3; period 4, so 2π/B = 4 and B = π/2; starts at a maximum: cosine; y = 3 cos(πx/2) + 2.
> Write an equation for this graph. It has a maximum of five at x equals zero, a minimum of negative one at x equals two, and the next maximum at x equals four. [pause]
> The midline is halfway between the top and bottom: five plus negative one, over two. That's two. [pause]
> The amplitude is half the distance from top to bottom: five minus negative one, over two. That's three.
> One full cycle runs from zero to four, so the period is four. Two pi over B equals four, so B is pi over two. [pause]
> The graph starts at a maximum, which is what cosine does. So y equals three cosine of pi x over two, plus two. [long pause]

## Example 3: A Ferris wheel
**On screen:** "A Ferris wheel has radius 20 m, its center is 25 m above the ground, and it turns once every 4 minutes. A rider starts at the bottom. Write the rider's height h(t) after t minutes, and find h(1) and h(2)." The Ferris wheel picture with the rider at the bottom. Board: midline 25, amplitude 20; period 4, B = 2π/4 = π/2; starts at the bottom (a minimum): −cosine; h(t) = 25 − 20 cos(πt/2); h(1) = 25 − 20 cos(π/2) = 25 m; h(2) = 25 − 20 cos π = 45 m.
> A Ferris wheel has radius twenty meters, its center is twenty-five meters above the ground, and it turns once every four minutes. A rider starts at the bottom. Write the rider's height after t minutes, then find the height at one and two minutes. [pause]
> The height swings around the center's height, so the midline is twenty-five and the amplitude is the radius, twenty. [pause]
> One turn takes four minutes, so the period is four and B is two pi over four, pi over two.
> At time zero the rider is at the bottom, a minimum. Negative cosine starts at a minimum, so h of t is twenty-five minus twenty cosine of pi t over two. [pause]
> At one minute: cosine of pi over two is zero, so the height is twenty-five meters, level with the center. At two minutes: cosine of pi is negative one, so the height is twenty-five plus twenty, forty-five meters, the top. [long pause]

## Example 4: Asymptotes of a tangent
**On screen:** "Find the vertical asymptotes of y = tan(2x) for 0 ≤ x ≤ π, and its period." Board: tan u is undefined when u = π/2 + kπ; 2x = π/2 + kπ; x = π/4 + kπ/2; in [0, π]: x = π/4, 3π/4; period π/2. The graph with the two asymptotes dashed.
> Find the vertical asymptotes of y equals tangent of two x between zero and pi, and find its period. [pause]
> Tangent of u is undefined when u is pi over two plus a multiple of pi. Here u is two x. [pause]
> So two x equals pi over two plus k pi. Divide by two: x equals pi over four plus k pi over two.
> Between zero and pi, that's pi over four and three pi over four. [pause]
> Tangent's period is pi, and the two inside squeezes it in half, so the period is pi over two. That matches: the asymptotes are pi over two apart. [long pause]
