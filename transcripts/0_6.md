# 0.6 Solving Trigonometric Equations

## When is the rider up high
**On screen:** The Ferris wheel from 0.4 turning, beside the graph of h(t) = 25 − 20 cos(πt/2) over two turns (0 ≤ t ≤ 8). A horizontal line at h = 35 cuts the wave twice in each turn; the crossing points are marked with dots, and each dot is matched with the rider passing 35 m on the way up and on the way down.
> On the Ferris wheel from before, when is the rider exactly thirty-five meters up? [pause]
> The horizontal line at thirty-five crosses the height graph twice every turn: once on the way up and once on the way down. And the wheel keeps turning, so it happens again every four minutes. [pause]
> Trig equations usually have more than one solution, and often infinitely many. The job is to find all of them in the interval you're asked about. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 0.6 · Solving Trigonometric Equations." Unit 0 · Trig Review.

## Two angles for every value
**On screen:** A unit circle. The horizontal line y = ½ cuts the circle at two points, at π/6 and 5π/6, labeled. Beside it, the graph of y = sin x on [0, 4π] with the line y = ½ crossing at π/6, 5π/6, 13π/6, 17π/6. Then "x = π/6 + 2kπ or x = 5π/6 + 2kπ".
> Solve sine x equals one half. Sine is the y-coordinate, so look for points on the unit circle at height one half. [pause]
> There are two: one at pi over six in the first quadrant, and its mirror image at five pi over six in the second. [pause]
> Going around again adds two pi to each. So all the solutions are pi over six plus two k pi and five pi over six plus two k pi, for any whole number k. [pause]
> Most problems ask only for the solutions in one turn, from zero up to two pi. Here that's pi over six and five pi over six. [long pause]

## Solving 2cos x + 1 = 0
**On screen:** The problem: "Solve 2 cos x + 1 = 0 for 0 ≤ x < 2π." Board: 2 cos x = −1; cos x = −½; reference angle π/3; cos < 0 in quadrants II and III; x = π − π/3 = 2π/3; x = π + π/3 = 4π/3. The unit circle with the vertical line x = −½ cutting it at both points.
> Solve two cosine x plus one equals zero, for x from zero up to two pi. [try it]
> Treat cosine x like an unknown and isolate it. Subtract one: two cosine x equals negative one. [pause]
> Divide by two: cosine x equals negative one half. [pause]
> Cosine is one half at pi over three, so the reference angle is pi over three. Cosine is negative in the second and third quadrants. [pause]
> In the second quadrant that's pi minus pi over three: two pi over three. [pause]
> In the third it's pi plus pi over three: four pi over three. Two solutions. [long pause]

## Factoring
**On screen:** "Solve 2 sin² x = sin x, 0 ≤ x < 2π." A red warning first: "Don't divide by sin x: it loses the solutions where sin x = 0." Board: 2 sin² x − sin x = 0; sin x (2 sin x − 1) = 0; sin x = 0: x = 0, π; 2 sin x − 1 = 0: sin x = ½: x = π/6, 5π/6.
> When the equation has a square, treat it like a quadratic. Take two sine squared x equals sine x. [pause]
> It's tempting to divide both sides by sine x. Don't: wherever sine x is zero, you'd be dividing by zero, and those solutions vanish. Move everything to one side instead: two sine squared x minus sine x equals zero. [pause]
> Factor out sine x: sine x times two sine x minus one equals zero. Set each factor equal to zero. [pause]
> Sine x equals zero at zero and pi. Two sine x minus one equals zero means sine x is one half, at pi over six and five pi over six. That's four solutions. [long pause]

## Using an identity first
**On screen:** "Solve sin 2x = cos x, 0 ≤ x < 2π." Board: 2 sin x cos x = cos x; 2 sin x cos x − cos x = 0; cos x (2 sin x − 1) = 0; cos x = 0: x = π/2, 3π/2; sin x = ½: x = π/6, 5π/6.
> When the angles don't match, an identity can fix that. In sine two x equals cosine x, rewrite sine two x as two sine x cosine x. [pause]
> Now move everything to one side and factor out cosine x: cosine x times two sine x minus one equals zero. [pause]
> Cosine x is zero at pi over two and three pi over two. Sine x is one half at pi over six and five pi over six. [long pause]

## Multiple angles
**On screen:** "Solve sin 2x = 1, 0 ≤ x < 2π." Board: x from 0 to 2π means 2x from 0 to 4π; sin u = 1 at u = π/2, 5π/2; 2x = π/2 or 5π/2; x = π/4, 5π/4. The graph of y = sin 2x on [0, 2π] reaching 1 twice.
> If the angle is two x, the interval changes too. Solve sine two x equals one. [pause]
> When x runs from zero to two pi, two x runs from zero to four pi: two full turns. Sine equals one at pi over two, and again a turn later at five pi over two. [pause]
> So two x is pi over two or five pi over two, and x is pi over four or five pi over four. Forgetting the second turn would lose half the answers. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Isolate the trig function, or move everything to one side and factor." "Never divide by a trig function." "Reference angle, then the quadrants where the sign is right." "For kx, solve on k turns, then divide."
> Isolate the trig function, or move everything to one side and factor. Never divide by a trig function. Find the reference angle, then use every quadrant where the sign is right. And when the angle is k x, go around k times before dividing by k. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A tangent equation
**On screen:** "Solve tan x = −1, 0 ≤ x < 2π." Board: tan(π/4) = 1, reference angle π/4; tan < 0 in quadrants II and IV; x = π − π/4 = 3π/4; x = 2π − π/4 = 7π/4.
> Solve tangent x equals negative one, for x from zero up to two pi. [pause]
> Tangent of pi over four is one, so the reference angle is pi over four. [pause]
> Tangent is negative where sine and cosine have opposite signs: the second and fourth quadrants.
> In the second quadrant, pi minus pi over four is three pi over four. [pause]
> In the fourth, two pi minus pi over four is seven pi over four. [long pause]

## Example 2: A quadratic in sine
**On screen:** "Solve 2 sin² x − sin x − 1 = 0, 0 ≤ x < 2π." Board: let u = sin x: 2u² − u − 1 = 0; (2u + 1)(u − 1) = 0; u = −½ or u = 1; sin x = −½: x = 7π/6, 11π/6; sin x = 1: x = π/2.
> Solve two sine squared x minus sine x minus one equals zero, for x from zero up to two pi. [pause]
> Call sine x u, so it looks like an ordinary quadratic: two u squared minus u minus one equals zero. [pause]
> That factors as two u plus one, times u minus one. So u is negative one half or u is one.
> Sine x equals negative one half: reference angle pi over six, in the third and fourth quadrants, where sine is negative. That's seven pi over six and eleven pi over six. [pause]
> Sine x equals one only at the top of the circle: pi over two. Three solutions in all. [long pause]

## Example 3: An identity and a quadratic
**On screen:** "Solve cos 2x = cos x, 0 ≤ x < 2π." Board: 2cos² x − 1 = cos x; 2cos² x − cos x − 1 = 0; (2cos x + 1)(cos x − 1) = 0; cos x = −½: x = 2π/3, 4π/3; cos x = 1: x = 0.
> Solve cosine two x equals cosine x, for x from zero up to two pi. [pause]
> Pick the form of cosine two x that's all cosines: two cosine squared x minus one. So two cosine squared x minus one equals cosine x. [pause]
> Move everything to one side: two cosine squared x minus cosine x minus one equals zero. It factors the same way as the last example: two cosine x plus one, times cosine x minus one.
> Cosine x equals negative one half at two pi over three and four pi over three. [pause]
> Cosine x equals one at zero. Three solutions. [long pause]

## Example 4: A double angle
**On screen:** "Solve sin 2x = √3/2, 0 ≤ x < 2π." Board: 0 ≤ 2x < 4π; sin u = √3/2 at u = π/3, 2π/3 (first turn); u = 7π/3, 8π/3 (second turn); 2x = π/3, 2π/3, 7π/3, 8π/3; x = π/6, π/3, 7π/6, 4π/3.
> Solve sine two x equals root three over two, for x from zero up to two pi. [pause]
> If x runs from zero to two pi, then two x runs from zero to four pi. [pause]
> Sine is root three over two at pi over three and two pi over three. A turn later, add two pi: seven pi over three and eight pi over three.
> Those are the values of two x. [pause]
> Divide each by two: pi over six, pi over three, seven pi over six, and four pi over three. [long pause]
