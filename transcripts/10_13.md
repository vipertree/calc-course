# 10.13 Radius and Interval of Convergence of Power Series

## A series with an x in it
**On screen:** The power series 1 + x + x² + x³ + ... written out. A slider for x moves along a number line: for x inside (−1, 1), the partial sums level off (a green check); outside, they blow past any bound or bounce (a red cross). The interval (−1, 1) glows.
> A power series has a variable in it: one plus x plus x squared plus x cubed, and so on. Each value of x gives a different series of numbers. [pause]
> For some values of x the series converges, and for others it doesn't. This one is geometric with ratio x, so it converges exactly when x is between negative one and one. [pause]
> Every power series converges on an interval like this, centered at its center. Our job is to find that interval. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 10.13 · Radius and interval of convergence of power series." Unit 10 · Infinite Sequences and Series. A small "BC" tag.

## Radius and interval
**On screen:** Board: a power series centered at a: Σ cₙ(x − a)ⁿ. A number line with the center a and a symmetric interval from a − R to a + R: "radius R: converges absolutely for |x − a| < R, diverges for |x − a| > R". The endpoints a − R and a + R marked with question marks: "check separately". Side notes: "R can be 0 (only x = a) or ∞ (all x)".
> A power series centered at a is a sum of constants times powers of x minus a. [pause]
> There's a radius of convergence R. Closer than R to the center, the series converges absolutely. Farther than R, it diverges. [pause]
> At exactly distance R, the two endpoints, anything can happen. Each endpoint has to be checked separately. [pause]
> R can also be zero, so the series converges only at the center, or infinite, so it converges for every x. [long pause]

## Finding the interval
**On screen:** The problem: "Find the interval of convergence of Σ_{n=1}^∞ (x − 2)ⁿ/(n · 3ⁿ)." Board, one step per line: |aₙ₊₁/aₙ| = |x − 2|ⁿ⁺¹/((n + 1)3ⁿ⁺¹) · (n · 3ⁿ)/|x − 2|ⁿ; = |x − 2|/3 · n/(n + 1); L = |x − 2|/3; L < 1: |x − 2| < 3, so R = 3, −1 < x < 5; x = 5: Σ 3ⁿ/(n3ⁿ) = Σ 1/n diverges (harmonic); x = −1: Σ (−3)ⁿ/(n3ⁿ) = Σ (−1)ⁿ/n converges (AST); interval [−1, 5). A number line fills in: open dot at 5, closed dot at −1.
> Find the interval of convergence of x minus two to the n, over n times three to the n. [pause]
> Use the ratio test. The ratio of consecutive terms simplifies to the absolute value of x minus two, over three, times n over n plus one. [pause]
> As n goes to infinity, n over n plus one goes to one, so L is the absolute value of x minus two over three. [pause]
> The series converges when L is less than one: absolute value of x minus two less than three. So the radius is three, and x is between negative one and five. [pause]
> Now the endpoints. At x equals five, x minus two is three, and the series becomes the sum of one over n: the harmonic series, divergent. [pause]
> At x equals negative one, x minus two is negative three, and the series becomes negative one to the n over n: convergent by the alternating series test. [pause]
> So the interval of convergence is from negative one, included, to five, not included. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "ratio test on |aₙ₊₁/aₙ|: L < 1 gives |x − a| < R"; "check both endpoints separately (often p-series or AST)"; "interval: (a − R, a + R) plus any endpoints that converge".
> Use the ratio test to find where the series converges absolutely: that gives the radius. Then plug in each endpoint and test the resulting number series on its own. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Converges everywhere
**On screen:** "Find the radius of convergence of Σ xⁿ/n!." Board: |aₙ₊₁/aₙ| = |x|/(n + 1) → 0 < 1 for every x; R = ∞; interval (−∞, ∞).
> Find the radius of convergence of x to the n over n factorial. [pause]
> The ratio is the absolute value of x over n plus one, which goes to zero for every x. [pause]
> Zero is always less than one, so the series converges for all x. The radius is infinite. [long pause]

## Example 2: Converges only at the center
**On screen:** "Find the radius of convergence of Σ n! xⁿ." Board: |aₙ₊₁/aₙ| = (n + 1)|x| → ∞ unless x = 0; R = 0; converges only at x = 0.
> Find the radius of convergence of n factorial times x to the n. [pause]
> The ratio is n plus one times the absolute value of x, which goes to infinity unless x is zero. [pause]
> So the series converges only at its center, x equals zero. The radius is zero. [long pause]

## Example 3: Both endpoints included
**On screen:** "Find the interval of convergence of Σ_{n=1}^∞ xⁿ/n²." Board: L = |x| · n²/(n + 1)² → |x|; R = 1; x = 1: Σ 1/n² converges (p = 2); x = −1: Σ (−1)ⁿ/n² converges (absolutely); interval [−1, 1].
> Find the interval of convergence of x to the n over n squared. [pause]
> The ratio test gives L equals the absolute value of x, so the radius is one. [pause]
> At x equals one: the sum of one over n squared, a convergent p-series. At x equals negative one: negative one to the n over n squared, which converges absolutely. [pause]
> Both endpoints are included: the interval is from negative one to one, closed. [long pause]
