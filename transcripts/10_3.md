# 10.3 The nth Term Test for Divergence

## A pile that never stops growing
**On screen:** Blocks being stacked, each block a bit thinner than the last but approaching a thickness of half a block rather than zero: 1, 0.67, 0.6, 0.57, ... approaching 0.5. The stack keeps rising without bound. Caption: "if the pieces don't shrink to zero, the total can't settle".
> Stack blocks, each one a little thinner than the one before, but never thinner than half a block. [pause]
> No matter how many you stack, each new block adds at least about half a block of height. The pile grows forever. [pause]
> That's the idea behind the simplest test for series: if the terms don't shrink to zero, the sum can't settle down. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 10.3 · The nth term test for divergence." Unit 10 · Infinite Sequences and Series. A small "BC" tag.

## The test
**On screen:** Board, boxed: "If lim_{n→∞} aₙ ≠ 0 (or does not exist), then Σ aₙ diverges." Under it, the reason: "if Σ aₙ converges to S, then aₙ = Sₙ − Sₙ₋₁ → S − S = 0". Then a red warning: "If lim aₙ = 0, the test says nothing."
> Here's the test. If the terms a sub n don't approach zero, either because the limit is some other number or because it doesn't exist, the series diverges. [pause]
> Why? If the series converged to S, then each term is the difference of two partial sums, S sub n minus S sub n minus one. Both approach S, so their difference approaches zero. [pause]
> The test only works one way. If the terms do approach zero, the test tells you nothing. The series might converge, or it might not. [long pause]

## Using the test
**On screen:** The problem: "Does Σ_{n=1}^∞ n/(2n + 1) converge or diverge?" Board, one step per line: aₙ = n/(2n + 1); lim_{n→∞} n/(2n + 1) = ½ (divide top and bottom by n); ½ ≠ 0; by the nth term test, the series diverges.
> Does the series of n over two n plus one converge or diverge? [try it]
> Look at the terms: n over two n plus one. [pause]
> As n goes to infinity, divide top and bottom by n: one over two plus one over n, which approaches one half. [pause]
> The terms approach one half, not zero. So by the nth term test, the series diverges. Adding about one half infinitely many times can't settle down. [long pause]

## Zero isn't enough
**On screen:** Σ 1/n: terms 1, ½, ⅓, ¼ shrinking to 0, but the partial sums keep growing (a slowly rising graph labeled "diverges, shown in 10.5"). Σ 1/n²: terms also shrinking to 0, partial sums leveling off near 1.645 ("converges"). Caption: "terms → 0: the nth term test is silent; use another test".
> The test can't go the other way. The terms of one over n shrink to zero, yet the series diverges, very slowly. We'll prove that in topic 10.5. [pause]
> The terms of one over n squared also shrink to zero, and that series converges. [pause]
> Same limit, opposite outcomes. When the terms go to zero, you need a different test. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "lim aₙ ≠ 0 or does not exist: Σ aₙ diverges"; "lim aₙ = 0: inconclusive"; "check it first; it's quick".
> If the terms don't go to zero, the series diverges. If they do go to zero, the nth term test is inconclusive. It's a quick first check before trying anything harder. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: An oscillating series
**On screen:** "Does Σ (−1)ⁿ converge?" Board: aₙ = (−1)ⁿ alternates −1, 1, −1, ...; lim aₙ does not exist; diverges.
> Does the series of negative one to the n converge? [pause]
> The terms bounce between negative one and one, so their limit doesn't exist. By the nth term test, the series diverges. [long pause]

## Example 2: A limit that's e
**On screen:** "Does Σ_{n=1}^∞ (1 + 1/n)ⁿ converge?" Board: lim (1 + 1/n)ⁿ = e ≠ 0; diverges.
> Does the series of one plus one over n, to the n, converge? [pause]
> That's the famous limit that equals e, about two point seven one eight. Not zero, so the series diverges. [long pause]

## Example 3: When the test is silent
**On screen:** "What does the nth term test say about Σ_{n=1}^∞ 1/√n?" Board: lim 1/√n = 0; the test is inconclusive; (it diverges: a p-series with p = ½, topic 10.5).
> What does the nth term test say about one over root n? [pause]
> The terms go to zero, so the test says nothing at all. [pause]
> In fact this series diverges, but we need the p-series test from topic 10.5 to show it. Never write "converges by the nth term test". [long pause]
