# 1.7 Selecting Procedures for Determining Limits

## A pile of limits
**On screen:** Six limit problems tumble onto the screen like cards: a polynomial, a factorable rational, a square root difference, an absolute value quotient, a piecewise function, and a complex fraction.
> You now have several tools for limits. The hard part on a test is choosing the right one quickly.
> This lesson is about the choosing.

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 1.7 · Selecting procedures for determining limits."

## Step one is always the same
**On screen:** A flowchart grows from a single box, "Substitute." Three arrows leave it: "a number", "0 over 0", "nonzero over 0".
> Every limit starts the same way: substitute. The result tells you where to go next. [pause]
> If you get a number, and the function is a nice formula near that point, you're finished. [long pause]

## When you get zero over zero
**On screen:** The "0 over 0" arrow branches into four smaller boxes, each with an icon: a factored polynomial, a square root with a conjugate, a stacked fraction, a trig identity. Each card from the opening flies to its matching box.
> Zero over zero sends you to the algebra toolkit. Look at the form of the expression. [pause]
> Polynomials: factor. Square roots: conjugate. Fractions inside fractions: combine. Trig functions: an identity. [pause]
> Then substitute again, and read the new result the same way. The algebra can uncover a number, or it can uncover a nonzero over zero, and then the limit may not exist. [pause]
> Zero over zero isn't the only form that sends you here. Infinity over infinity is just as undecided, and it shows up when x heads off to infinity. Topic one point fifteen handles it the same way: rewrite first. [pause]

## When you get nonzero over zero
**On screen:** The "nonzero over 0" arrow leads to a box labeled "check the signs on each side." A number line near the bad point shows a plus sign on one side and a minus sign on the other.
> Nonzero over zero means the function is blowing up. Check the sign of the expression just left and just right of the point. [pause]
> Matching signs give infinity or negative infinity. Opposite signs mean the two-sided limit doesn't exist. [long pause]

## Signs near a zero denominator
**On screen:** Each limit with its 3/0 check, the sign of the denominator on each side of 2, and the conclusions ∞ and DNE.
> Find the limit as x approaches two of x plus one over x minus two squared, and of x plus one over x minus two. [try it]
> Both give three over zero, so check signs. In the first, x minus two squared is positive on both sides.
> So both one-sided limits are positive infinity, and the limit is infinity. [pause]
> In the second, x minus two is negative on the left and positive on the right: negative infinity from the left, infinity from the right.
> The sides disagree, so that limit does not exist. [long pause]

## Absolute values and pieces
**On screen:** The absolute value of x minus 2, over x minus 2. The number line splits at 2: on the left, the absolute value becomes negative (x minus 2), and the quotient is negative 1; on the right it's positive 1.
> Two special cases need their own step. Absolute values and piecewise functions change their formula at a point. [pause]
> So split into the two sides and use the correct formula on each. Here, the left side gives negative one and the right side gives one. No limit. [long pause]

## Absolute value
**On screen:** The left-hand and right-hand limits worked separately, giving −1 and 1, then 'does not exist.'
> Find the limit of the absolute value of x minus two, over x minus two, as x approaches two. [try it]
> From the left, x minus two is negative, so its absolute value is the negative of x minus two. The ratio is negative one.
> From the right, x minus two is positive, and the ratio is one. [pause]
> Negative one and one disagree, so the limit does not exist. [long pause]

## Working through one
**On screen:** The expression x squared plus 2x minus 8, over the square root of x plus 5, minus 1, as x approaches negative 4. Substitution gives 0 over 0. The conjugate is applied, then factoring, then substitution gives negative 12.
> Here's one that needs two tools. Substituting gives zero over zero. [pause]
> The square root says conjugate. Multiply top and bottom by the square root of x plus five, plus one.
> The bottom becomes x plus four. The top factors as x plus four, times x minus two. Cancel, substitute, and the limit is negative twelve. [pause]

## Two tools in one
**On screen:** Factoring the top and multiplying by the conjugate, the x + 4 factors struck out, and −12.
> Find the limit of x squared plus two x minus eight, over the square root of x plus five, minus one, as x approaches negative four. [try it]
> Zero over zero. Factor the top into x plus four times x minus two, and multiply by the conjugate of the bottom.
> The bottom becomes x plus five minus one, which is x plus four. It cancels with the top. [pause]
> Plug in: negative six times two. Negative twelve. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The completed flowchart, with every card from the opening sitting in its box. A note under the rewrite box: "coming in Unit 4: L'Hôpital's rule."
> Substitute, read the result, and let the form of the expression pick the tool. [pause]
> One more tool is coming. Once we can take derivatives, a result called L'Hôpital's rule will handle zero over zero and infinity over infinity in a new way. For now, algebra does the job. [long pause]

## Example 1: Nothing to fix
**On screen:** "Find the limit as x → 2 of (x² + 1)/(x + 3)." Substitution: 5/5 = 1. The flowchart lights the "a number" branch.
> Find the limit as x approaches two of x squared plus one, over x plus three. [pause]
> Substitute first. The top is five, the bottom is five. We got a number, and the denominator isn't zero.
> The limit is one. No algebra needed. [pause] Don't do more work than the problem asks for.

## Example 2: Fractions inside a fraction
**On screen:** "Find the limit as x → 0 of [1/(x + 2) − 1/2] / x." Substitution gives 0/0; the flowchart lights "combine fractions." Each line keeps the limit: the top becomes (2 − (x + 2)) / (2(x + 2)) = −x / (2(x + 2)); dividing by x gives −1/(2(x + 2)); the limit is −1/4.
> Find the limit as x approaches zero of one over x plus two, minus one half, all over x. [pause]
> Substitution gives one half minus one half, over zero. Zero over zero.
> The form is a fraction inside a fraction, so combine the top over the common denominator two times x plus two. [pause]
> The top becomes negative x, over two times x plus two. Dividing by x cancels the x, leaving negative one over two times x plus two.
> Now substitute zero: negative one over four. [pause]

## Example 3: Nonzero over zero
**On screen:** "Find the limit as x → 5 of (x + 1)/(x − 5)²." Substitution gives 6/0; the flowchart lights "check the signs." A sign line: the top is positive on both sides, and (x − 5)² is positive on both sides. Result: ∞.
> Find the limit as x approaches five of x plus one, over x minus five squared. [pause]
> Substitution gives six over zero. Nonzero over zero, so check signs on each side.
> The top is near six, positive, on both sides. The bottom is a square, so it's positive on both sides too, and shrinking to zero. [pause]
> Positive over tiny positive: the quotient grows without bound on both sides. We write the limit as infinity. [long pause]
