# 2.2 Defining the Derivative of a Function and Using Derivative Notation

## One slope, then every slope
**On screen:** The graph of f of x equals x squared. A tangent line sits at x equals 1, labeled "slope 2." Then the point of tangency starts to slide along the curve, and the tangent line rides with it, tilting as it goes. A readout shows the slope changing.
> Last time we found the slope of a tangent line at one point. [pause]
> But nothing is special about that point.
> Slide the point along the curve, and the tangent line tilts. [pause] At every x, there's a slope.
> So the slope is itself a function of x.

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 2.2 · Defining the derivative of a function and using derivative notation."

## Recording the slopes
**On screen:** Below the graph of x squared, a second set of axes. As the tangent slides, a dot is plotted below it at height equal to the current slope: at x equals negative 1 the dot is at negative 2, at 0 it's at 0, at 1 it's at 2. The dots trace out a straight line through the origin.
> Let's record those slopes. Each time the tangent line stops, plot its slope as a point on a new graph underneath.
> At x equals negative one, the slope is negative two. [pause]
> At zero, the tangent line is flat, so the slope is zero. [pause]
> At one, the slope is two.
> The points line up along a straight line, y equals two x. [pause]
> This new function is called the derivative of f.

## The definition
**On screen:** The limit definition from Topic 2.1, f prime of a, with the a's highlighted. Then each a morphs into an x.
> To compute it, we use the same limit as last time. [pause]
> Last time, a was one fixed input. Now we let the input be any x.
> f prime of x is the limit, as h approaches zero, of f of x plus h, minus f of x, all over h. [long pause]

## What h does here
**On screen:** On the parabola, a point at x and a second point at x plus h, with a bracket of width h along the x-axis between them. The secant line through the two points. As h shrinks, the second point slides toward the first.
> x is the point we care about. [pause] x plus h is a second point, h units to the right.
> f of x plus h, minus f of x, is the rise between them. h is the run. [pause]
> So the fraction is the slope of a secant line. Letting h approach zero pulls the second point in, and the secant slope becomes the tangent slope.

## The derivative of x squared
**On screen:** "Use the definition to find f′(x) for f(x) = x²." Each line keeps the limit. The x² terms are struck out as they cancel; then h is factored out of the top and the two h's are struck out.
> Let's compute the derivative of x squared from the definition. [try it]
> The numerator is x plus h, squared, minus x squared.
> Expand: x squared plus two x h plus h squared, minus x squared. The x squared terms cancel. [pause]
> Every term left has an h. Factor it out: h times two x plus h. Now cancel the h on top with the h on the bottom. That's allowed because h is never zero inside the limit. [pause]
> That leaves the limit of two x plus h. As h approaches zero, it's two x.
> That matches the slopes we plotted.

## Prime notation
**On screen:** f′(x) = 2x. Below it, y = x² and y′ = 2x. Then more pairs: g(t) = t³ with g′(t); A(r) = πr² with A′(r).
> One idea, several ways to write it. Start with the one we've been using: f prime of x. [pause]
> If we call the output y, so y equals x squared, then y prime means the same thing, just shorter. [pause]
> Later in the course we'll take derivatives of curves that aren't functions at all, like circles, and y prime will be handy there. [pause]
> The letters can be anything. If g of t is t cubed, its derivative is g prime of t. If A of r is the area of a circle of radius r, its derivative is A prime of r. [long pause]

## Little nudges
**On screen:** The parabola with a secant line and a rise-run triangle. The run is labeled Δx and the rise Δy, with a note: "Δ: capital delta, 'change in.'" The fraction Δy/Δx appears. The triangle shrinks; a side note shows Δ, then the lowercase δ, then d. The labels become dx and dy, and the fraction becomes dy/dx.
> The other common notation comes from the secant slope. [pause]
> The run is a change in x. We write it delta x, with a capital Greek delta meaning "change in." The rise is delta y. [pause]
> So the secant slope is delta y over delta x. [pause]
> Now shrink the triangle. The changes become tiny nudges. A lowercase delta looks a lot like the letter d, and that's the notation: d x for a tiny nudge in x, and d y for the nudge in y that it causes. [pause]
> So d y d x is the tangent slope: the ratio of two tiny changes. We read it "d y d x." [long pause]

## Other letters, same idea
**On screen:** "ds/dt: how fast position s changes per unit of time t." "dA/dr: how fast area A changes as the radius r grows." Then d/dx [x²] = 2x.
> The letters follow the variables. If s is position and t is time, d s d t is how fast the position changes per unit of time. [pause]
> d A d r is how fast the area changes as the radius grows. [pause]
> Sometimes the d over d x sits in front, like an instruction. d over d x of x squared means "take the derivative of x squared," which is two x. [long pause]

## Evaluating at a point
**On screen:** f′(x) = 2x, then f′(3) = 2 · 3 = 6. Beside it, dy/dx with a tall bar and "x = 3" at its foot, equal to 6. Both are circled with "the same number."
> To ask for the slope at one point, plug in. In prime notation, f prime of three is two times three: six. [pause]
> In d y d x notation, we write d y d x with a tall bar after it and x equals three at the bottom. It means the same thing: evaluate the derivative at three. Also six. [long pause]

## Point-slope form
**On screen:** A line through a point (x₁, y₁) with slope m, and the formula y − y₁ = m(x − x₁) in a box. Then the pieces are replaced: y − f(a) = f′(a)(x − a).
> Before tangent lines, a quick review. A line through the point x one, y one, with slope m, has the equation y minus y one equals m times the quantity x minus x one. That's point-slope form. [pause]
> A tangent line at x equals a goes through the point a, f of a, with slope f prime of a. Put those in, and you have the tangent line. [long pause]

## A tangent line
**On screen:** "Find the equation of the line tangent to f(x) = x² at x = 3. Write it in slope-intercept form." Point (3, 9); slope f′(3) = 6; y − 9 = 6(x − 3); y = 6x − 18 + 9 = 6x − 9. The line is drawn touching the parabola.
> Find the equation of the line tangent to x squared at x equals three. Write it in slope-intercept form. [try it]
> First the point. f of three is nine, so the point is three, nine.
> Then the slope. f prime of three is six. [pause]
> Point-slope form: y minus nine equals six times the quantity x minus three.
> For slope-intercept form, distribute and add nine: y equals six x minus eighteen plus nine, which is six x minus nine. [pause]
> Leaving a tangent line in point-slope form is fine, unless the question asks for a form, or you're matching a multiple-choice answer that's simplified. [long pause]

## Units
**On screen:** "V(t) is the volume of water in a tank, in liters, t minutes after it starts draining. Interpret V′(4) = −12." Units of V′: liters per minute. The sentence: "At t = 4 minutes, the volume of water is decreasing at 12 liters per minute."
> V of t is the volume of water in a tank, in liters, t minutes after it starts draining. What does V prime of four equals negative twelve mean? [try it]
> A derivative's units are output units per input unit. Here that's liters per minute. [pause]
> The negative sign means the volume is decreasing.
> So: at t equals four minutes, the volume of water is decreasing at twelve liters per minute. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The two stacked graphs, f above and f prime below, with the sliding tangent line connecting them.
> The derivative is a function. At every input, it reports the slope of the graph, and the rate of change. [long pause]
# Worked examples

## Example 1: A derivative from the definition
**On screen:** "Use the definition to find f′(x) for f(x) = 3x² − 5x." Each line keeps the limit. The 3x² and 5x terms are struck out as they cancel; then h is factored out and canceled.
> Use the definition to find f prime of x for three x squared minus five x. [try it]
> Write f of x plus h: three times x plus h, squared, minus five times x plus h. Subtract f of x.
> Expand. Three x squared plus six x h plus three h squared, minus five x minus five h, minus three x squared, plus five x. [pause]
> The three x squared terms cancel, and so do the five x terms. What's left is six x h plus three h squared minus five h.
> Every term has an h. Factor it out and cancel it with the h on the bottom: six x plus three h minus five. [pause]
> Let h approach zero. f prime of x is six x minus five.

## Example 2: A reciprocal
**On screen:** "Use the definition to find g′(x) for g(x) = 2/x." The steps: combine 2/(x + h) − 2/x over x(x + h); the 2x terms cancel to leave −2h; the h's cancel; the limit of −2/(x(x + h)) is −2/x².
> Now g of x equals two over x. [try it]
> The numerator is two over x plus h, minus two over x. Combine it over the common denominator x times x plus h.
> The top becomes two x minus two x minus two h. The two x's cancel, leaving negative two h. [pause]
> Dividing by h puts an h on the bottom too, and the h's cancel: negative two over x times x plus h.
> As h approaches zero, that becomes negative two over x squared.

## Example 3: A tangent line
**On screen:** "Find the equation for the line tangent to f(x) = 3x² − 5x at x = 2." Point: f(2) = 2, so (2, 2). Slope: f′(2) = 12 − 5 = 7. Point-slope: y − 2 = 7(x − 2). The line is drawn touching the parabola.
> Use Example 1 to find the equation for the line tangent to three x squared minus five x at x equals two. [try it]
> First the point. f of two is twelve minus ten, which is two. So the point is two, two.
> Then the slope. f prime of two is six times two minus five, which is seven. [pause]
> Point-slope form: y minus two equals seven times the quantity x minus two. Nobody asked for a particular form, so this is a complete answer. [long pause]
