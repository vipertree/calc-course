# 2.5 Applying the Power Rule

## A growing square
**On screen:** A square with sides labeled x, and "area x²" inside. The right and top edges push outward by a thin sliver. A small double-headed arrow marks the width of each sliver, labeled dx. The new area shows the original square plus two thin strips along the right and top edges, each labeled with its length x and width dx, and a tiny corner square, dx by dx. Each piece is shaded a different color.
> Here's a square with side x, so its area is x squared. [pause]
> Nudge the side length by a tiny amount. We'll call the nudge d x. [pause] Historically, people thought of d x as an infinitesimal: an infinitely small change in x. For us, d x is just a tiny nudge, and we'll watch what happens as that nudge shrinks toward zero. [pause] How much does the area grow?
> Two thin strips appear, each x long and d x wide. And a tiny square fills the corner, d x on each side. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 2.5 · Applying the power rule."

## From a nudge to a limit
**On screen:** The change in area builds from the picture: dA = 2x·dx + (dx)². Dividing by dx gives dA/dx = 2x + dx. A slider shrinks dx: the strips get thinner, and the corner square shrinks much faster than the strips. As dx → 0, dA/dx becomes 2x.
> The extra area, d A, is the two strips, two x times d x, plus the corner, d x squared. [pause]
> Divide by d x to get the rate: area gained per unit of side gained. d A over d x is two x plus d x.
> Now shrink the nudge. Watch the corner. When the nudge is a tenth, the strips have width a tenth, but the corner is only a hundredth. [pause]
> The corner shrinks much faster than the strips. As the nudge approaches zero, it disappears from the rate entirely.
> What's left is two x. [pause] That's the derivative of x squared. The picture shows exactly where each term comes from. [long pause]

## What d x means
**On screen:** The equation dA/dx = 2x + dx, with "dx → 0" written under it, becomes dA/dx = 2x. A caption: "think: infinitely small changes. officially: a limit."
> A word about that division. [pause]
> Dividing by d x, and then letting the nudge shrink to zero, is the same limit we've used all unit, in short form. That's where the notation d A d x comes from. [pause]
> It's fine to think of d x and d A as infinitely small changes. Just know that the official definition comes from limits. [long pause]

## A growing cube
**On screen:** A cube of side x. Three faces push outward by a thin dx. Three thin slabs appear, each x by x by dx. Along the edges between them, three thin rods, each x by dx by dx. At the far corner, one tiny cube, dx on each side. The pieces are counted: dV = 3x² dx + 3x (dx)² + (dx)³.
> Now a cube. Its volume is x cubed. Nudge every side by d x. [pause]
> The biggest new pieces are three thin slabs, one on each of three faces. Each is x by x by d x, so together they add three x squared d x.
> Along the edges where the slabs meet, three thin rods fill in, each x by d x by d x. And one tiny cube sits in the far corner. [pause]
> Divide the change in volume by d x: d V over d x is three x squared, plus three x d x, plus d x squared.
> As the nudge shrinks to zero, the rods and the tiny cube vanish from the rate. What's left is three x squared. [pause]
> That's the derivative of x cubed: the three slabs are the whole story. [long pause]

## The pattern
**On screen:** A column: x squared gives 2x, x cubed gives 3x squared, x to the fourth gives 4x cubed. The exponent drops in front and the new exponent is one less, animated with arrows.
> The pattern continues. For x to the n, bring the exponent down in front, and lower the exponent by one. [long pause]

## The power rule
**On screen:** A formula card: the derivative of x to the n is n times x to the n minus 1. A note: for any real number n.
> That's the power rule. The derivative of x to the n is n x to the n minus one. [pause]
> It works for every real exponent, not just positive whole numbers. [long pause]

## Two steps
**On screen:** d/dx [x⁵] =. The exponent 5 is circled, a copy swings around to the front, "5 − 1 = 4" appears above, and the result 5x⁴ is written.
> The easiest way to remember the rule is as two steps. [pause]
> Step one: multiply by the exponent, by bringing it down in front. Step two: subtract one from the exponent. [pause]
> For x to the fifth: circle the five, send it out front, and five minus one is four. Five x to the fourth. [long pause]

## Rewrite first
**On screen:** Expressions get rewritten as powers before differentiating: 1 over x squared becomes x to the negative 2, and the square root of x becomes x to the one half. Each derivative follows with the two steps drawn: circle the exponent, send it in front, lower it by one.
> To use the rule, write the function as a power of x first. [pause]
> One over x squared is x to the negative two. Send the negative two in front. Negative two minus one is negative three: negative two x to the negative three. [pause]
> The square root of x is x to the one half. Send the one half in front. One half minus one is negative one half: one half x to the negative one half. [long pause]

## In context
**On screen:** The graph of y equals x cubed with a tangent line at x equals 2, slope 12. Beside it: "If the side of a cube is growing, its volume grows at 12 cubic units per unit of side length when the side is 2."
> On the graph of x cubed, the tangent line at two has slope twelve. [pause]
> And if x is the side of a cube, then when the side is two, the volume is growing twelve times as fast as the side.

## Slope and rate
**On screen:** "(a) Find the slope of the tangent line to y = x⁴ at x = −1. (b) If A = s² is the area of a square with side s cm, how fast is the area changing with respect to s when s = 5?" Part (a) is worked, then part (b).
> Two quick ones. First, find the slope of the tangent line to x to the fourth at x equals negative one. [try it]
> The derivative is four x cubed. At negative one, that's four times negative one: negative four.
> Second, A equals s squared is the area of a square with side s centimeters. How fast is the area changing with respect to s when s is five? [try it]
> d A d s is two s, which is ten at five. Ten square centimeters per centimeter. [long pause]

## Tangent line
**On screen:** "Find the equation for the line tangent to y = √x at x = 4." Rewrite √x = x^(1/2); the two steps give (1/2)x^(−1/2) = 1/(2√x); slope 1/4; point (4, 2); y − 2 = (1/4)(x − 4).
> Find the equation for the line tangent to the square root of x at x equals four. [try it]
> Rewrite the root as x to the one half. Send the one half in front, and one half minus one is negative one half.
> So the derivative is one half x to the negative one half, which is one over two root x. At four, that's one fourth. [pause]
> The point is four, two. So the line is y minus two equals one fourth times x minus four. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The growing square and cube from the start, with the power rule card between them.
> Bring the exponent down, subtract one. The square and the cube show you why. [long pause]
# Worked examples

## Example 1: Straight from the rule
**On screen:** Two derivatives, worked side by side. Left: d/dx of x⁷ = 7x⁶. Right: d/dx of x⁻³ = −3x⁻⁴ = −3/x⁴. Arrows show the exponent dropping and decreasing by one.
> Find the derivatives of x to the seventh and x to the negative third. [pause]
> For x to the seventh, bring the seven down and lower the exponent to six: seven x to the sixth.
> For x to the negative third, bring down negative three. One less than negative three is negative four. [pause]
> Negative three x to the negative fourth, or negative three over x to the fourth.

## Example 2: Rewrite, then differentiate
**On screen:** "Find dy/dx at x = 4 for y = 1/√(x³)." The rewrite: √(x³) = x^(3/2), so y = x^(−3/2). The derivative: −(3/2)x^(−5/2). At x = 4: 4^(5/2) = 32, so the slope is −3/64.
> Find the slope of one over the square root of x cubed, at x equals four. [pause]
> First, rewrite as a power. The square root of x cubed is x to the three halves, and it's in the denominator, so y is x to the negative three halves.
> Power rule: negative three halves, times x to the negative five halves. [pause]
> At four: four to the five halves is the square root of four, to the fifth, which is thirty-two. So the slope is negative three over sixty-four.

## Example 3: A tangent line to a cube root
**On screen:** "Find the equation for the line tangent to y = ∛x at x = 8." The point (8, 2). The derivative (1/3)x^(−2/3); at 8 it's (1/3)(1/4) = 1/12. The line y − 2 = (1/12)(x − 8) is drawn.
> Find the equation for the line tangent to the cube root of x at x equals eight. [pause]
> The point: the cube root of eight is two, so eight, two.
> The slope: the cube root is x to the one third. Its derivative is one third x to the negative two thirds. [pause]
> At eight, eight to the two thirds is four, so the slope is one third times one fourth, one twelfth.
> The tangent line is y minus two equals one twelfth times x minus eight. [long pause]
