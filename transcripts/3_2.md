# 3.2 Implicit Differentiation

## A curve with no formula for y
**On screen:** The circle x² + y² = 25 draws itself. A vertical line sweeps across it and hits two points at once. Then the view zooms toward (3, 4), and the arc near that point straightens into a nearly straight segment.
> Here's a circle: x squared plus y squared equals twenty-five. [pause]
> It isn't the graph of a function. A vertical line can hit it twice.
> But zoom in near the point three, four, and the curve looks like a small piece of a function's graph. [pause] It has a tangent line. What's its slope? [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 3.2 · Implicit differentiation."

## Pretend y is a function
**On screen:** The equation x² + y² = 25 with y highlighted and a small label beneath it: "y(x), unknown but real." An arrow shows that moving x moves y along the arc.
> We could solve for y, but we don't have to. [pause]
> Near three, four, y really is some function of x. We just don't write down its formula.
> Every time x moves, y moves too, to keep the point on the circle. [long pause]

## The chain rule on y squared
**On screen:** d/dx [y²] expands: the outside u² gives 2y, the inside y gives dy/dx. Result: 2y · dy/dx, with dy/dx glowing.
> So what's the derivative of y squared, with respect to x? [pause]
> It's a composite: the outside squares, the inside is y. The chain rule gives two y, times the derivative of the inside, d y d x.
> That factor of d y d x is the whole trick. Every y term gets one. [long pause]

## Differentiate both sides
**On screen:** The equation x² + y² = 25. Under each term, its derivative appears: 2x, 2y dy/dx, 0. Then the solving: 2y dy/dx = −2x, dy/dx = −x/y.
> Differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to x. [pause]
> x squared gives two x. y squared gives two y d y d x. Twenty-five is a constant, so zero.
> Now solve for d y d x: it's negative x over y. [pause]
> At three, four, the slope is negative three fourths. [long pause]

## Why the answer has y in it
**On screen:** Two points on the circle above x = 3: (3, 4) and (3, −4), with tangent lines of slope −3/4 and 3/4.
> Notice the answer uses both x and y. [pause]
> That makes sense. Above x equals three, the circle passes through two points: three, four and three, negative four.
> They have different slopes, and negative x over y tells them apart. [long pause]

## A product term
**On screen:** x² + xy + y² = 7. The middle term xy is boxed and differentiated with the product rule: y + x dy/dx. The whole derivative: 2x + y + x dy/dx + 2y dy/dx = 0. The dy/dx terms slide together: (x + 2y) dy/dx = −(2x + y).
> Terms can mix x and y. Take x squared plus x y plus y squared equals seven. [pause]
> The middle term is a product. Its derivative is y, plus x times d y d x.
> Collect every d y d x term on one side: x plus two y, times d y d x, equals negative two x plus y. [pause]
> Divide, and d y d x is negative two x plus y, over x plus two y. At one, two, that's negative four fifths. [long pause]

## Horizontal and vertical tangents
**On screen:** Back to the circle. The slope formula −x/y. The numerator x = 0 lights the top and bottom points (0, ±5) with flat tangents. The denominator y = 0 lights (±5, 0) with vertical tangents.
> The slope formula also finds special tangents. [pause]
> A horizontal tangent means slope zero: the numerator is zero. For the circle, x equals zero, the top and bottom.
> A vertical tangent means the slope blows up: the denominator is zero. Here y equals zero, the left and right sides. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The recipe in two lines: "Differentiate both sides. Every y term gets dy/dx." and "Solve for dy/dx."
> Differentiate everything, and give every y term its d y d x. Then solve. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Two y terms
**On screen:** "Find dy/dx for 3x² + y² = 12." Work: 6x + 2y y′ = 0, so y′ = −3x/y.
> Find d y d x for three x squared plus y squared equals twelve. [pause]
> Three x squared gives six x. y squared gives two y d y d x. Twelve gives zero.
> Solve: d y d x equals negative six x over two y, which is negative three x over y. [pause]

## Example 2: A tangent line
**On screen:** "Find the equation for the line tangent to x³ + y³ = 9 at (1, 2)." Work: 3x² + 3y² y′ = 0, y′ = −x²/y² = −1/4. The line y − 2 = −1/4(x − 1) is drawn on the curve.
> Find the equation for the line tangent to x cubed plus y cubed equals nine at one, two. [pause]
> Check the point first: one plus eight is nine. Good.
> Differentiate: three x squared, plus three y squared d y d x, equals zero. So d y d x is negative x squared over y squared. [pause]
> At one, two, that's negative one fourth. The line is y minus two equals negative one fourth times x minus one.

## Example 3: Trig and exponential
**On screen:** "Find dy/dx for sin y + eˣ = y." Work: cos y · y′ + eˣ = y′; y′(1 − cos y) = eˣ; y′ = eˣ/(1 − cos y).
> Find d y d x for sine y plus e to the x equals y. [pause]
> Sine y gives cosine y, times d y d x. e to the x gives e to the x. And y gives d y d x.
> Move the d y d x terms together: d y d x times one minus cosine y equals e to the x. [pause]
> So d y d x is e to the x over one minus cosine y. [long pause]
