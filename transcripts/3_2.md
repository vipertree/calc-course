# 3.2 Implicit Differentiation

## A curve with no formula for y
**On screen:** The circle x² + y² = 25 draws itself. A note appears: "vertical line test: a function has exactly one output for each input." A vertical line at x = 3 hits the circle at (3, 4) and (3, −4): "one input, two outputs." The line sweeps across and hits two points almost everywhere. Then "That's okay: it still has tangent lines," and the view zooms toward (3, 4), where the arc straightens into a nearly straight segment.
> Here's a circle: x squared plus y squared equals twenty-five. [pause]
> Remember the vertical line test? A function has exactly one output for each input. [pause]
> A circle has lots of inputs with two outputs. At x equals three, y can be four or negative four. A vertical line hits the circle twice, so the circle isn't the graph of a function. [pause]
> But that's okay. We can still find tangent lines.
> Zoom in near the point three, four, and the curve looks like a small piece of a function's graph. [pause] It has a tangent line. What's its slope? [long pause]

## Title

## Pretend y is a function
**On screen:** The equation x² + y² = 25 with y highlighted and a small label beneath it: "y(x), unknown but real." An arrow shows that moving x moves y along the arc.
> We could solve for y, but we don't have to. [pause]
> Near three, four, y really is some function of x. We just don't write down its formula.
> Every time x moves, y moves too, to keep the point on the circle. [long pause]

## The chain rule on y squared
**On screen:** "d/dx [y²] = ?" Under it: "a derivative with respect to x of something written in y?" Then the chain rule relating rates: d/dx [y²] = d/dy [y²] · dy/dx. Then = 2y · dy/dx, with dy/dx glowing. Then a boxed key point: "Every time we take the derivative of y (or of something in y) with respect to x, we multiply by dy/dx."
> So what's the derivative of y squared, with respect to x? [pause]
> Slow down and look at that. We're taking a derivative with respect to x of something written in y. How can that work? [long pause]
> Remember what the chain rule does: it relates different rates. y squared changes with y, and y changes with x. So the rate with respect to x is the rate with respect to y, times d y d x. [pause]
> The derivative of y squared with respect to y is two y. Times d y d x. So the derivative of y squared with respect to x is two y times d y d x. [long pause]
> Here's the key point. Every time we take the derivative of y, or of something in y, with respect to x, we multiply by d y d x. [pause]
> That's the chain rule every time, because y is a function of x. [long pause]

## Differentiate both sides
**On screen:** The equation x² + y² = 25. Under it, written like algebra: d/dx(x² + y²) = d/dx(25). Then 2x + 2y dy/dx = 0, then 2y dy/dx = −2x, dy/dx = −x/y, and at (3, 4), −3/4.
> Differentiate both sides of the equation with respect to x. Write it like algebra: d d x of the whole left side equals d d x of the whole right side. [pause]
> x squared gives two x. y squared gives two y d y d x. Twenty-five is a constant, so zero.
> Now solve for d y d x: it's negative x over y. [pause]
> At three, four, the slope is negative three fourths. [long pause]

## Why the answer has y in it
**On screen:** Two points on the circle above x = 3: (3, 4) and (3, −4), with tangent lines of slope −3/4 and 3/4.
> Notice the answer uses both x and y. [pause]
> That makes sense. Above x equals three, the circle passes through two points: three, four and three, negative four.
> They have different slopes, and negative x over y tells them apart. [long pause]

## All tangent lines at x = 3
**On screen:** "Find the equations of all lines tangent to x² + y² = 25 at x = 3." The circle with both tangent lines at the right. Work: d/dx(x² + y²) = d/dx(25); 2x + 2y dy/dx = 0; dy/dx = −x/y; 9 + y² = 25, so y = 4 or y = −4; at (3, 4), y − 4 = −3/4 (x − 3); at (3, −4), y + 4 = 3/4 (x − 3).
> Find the equations of all the lines tangent to x squared plus y squared equals twenty-five at x equals three. [try it]
> First the slope. d d x of the left side equals d d x of the right side.
> That gives two x plus two y d y d x equals zero. So d y d x is negative x over y. [pause]
> The slope needs a y, so use the circle to find the points. Nine plus y squared is twenty-five, so y squared is sixteen, and y is four or negative four. Two points, so two tangent lines. [pause]
> At three, four, the slope is negative three fourths. So y minus four equals negative three fourths times x minus three.
> At three, negative four, the slope is three fourths. So y plus four equals three fourths times x minus three. [long pause]

## A product term
**On screen:** x² + xy + y² = 7, then d/dx(x² + xy + y²) = d/dx(7). The middle term xy is boxed and differentiated with the product rule: y + x dy/dx. The whole derivative: 2x + y + x dy/dx + 2y dy/dx = 0. The dy/dx terms slide together: (x + 2y) dy/dx = −(2x + y).
> Terms can mix x and y. Take x squared plus x y plus y squared equals seven, and take d d x of both sides. [pause]
> The middle term is a product. Its derivative is y, plus x times d y d x. That first y has no d y d x, because there we took the derivative of x. The d y d x shows up only where we took the derivative of y.
> Collect every d y d x term on one side: x plus two y, times d y d x, equals negative two x plus y. [pause]
> Divide, and d y d x is negative two x plus y, over x plus two y. At one, two, that's negative four fifths. [long pause]

## Horizontal and vertical tangents
**On screen:** Back to the circle, with the slope formula −x/y. "Horizontal: slope 0, so dy/dx = 0." "Numerator = 0: x = 0." The top and bottom points (0, ±5) light up with flat tangents. Then "Vertical: dy/dx blows up (dividing by 0)." "Denominator = 0: y = 0." The points (±5, 0) light up with vertical tangents.
> The slope formula also finds special tangents. [pause]
> First, what makes a tangent line horizontal? A horizontal line has slope zero. So we want d y d x to equal zero. [pause]
> Our slope is a fraction, negative x over y. A fraction is zero when its numerator is zero. So set the numerator equal to zero: x equals zero. [pause]
> On the circle, x is zero at the top and the bottom: zero, five and zero, negative five. [long pause]
> What about a vertical tangent? We haven't really talked about those. A vertical line is like a line with an infinite slope. The derivative blows up, because we'd be dividing by zero. [pause]
> So set the denominator equal to zero: y equals zero. [pause]
> On the circle, y is zero at the left and right sides: five, zero and negative five, zero. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The recipe in two lines: "1. Write d/dx(left side) = d/dx(right side). Each time you take the derivative of something in y, multiply by dy/dx." and "2. Solve for dy/dx."
> Take d d x of both sides. Every time you take the derivative of something in y with respect to x, multiply by d y d x. Then solve. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Two y terms
**On screen:** "Find dy/dx for 3x² + y² = 12." Work: d/dx(3x² + y²) = d/dx(12); 6x + 2y dy/dx = 0; dy/dx = −6x/(2y) = −3x/y.
> Find d y d x for three x squared plus y squared equals twelve. [pause]
> Take d d x of both sides: d d x of three x squared plus y squared equals d d x of twelve.
> Three x squared gives six x. y squared gives two y d y d x. Twelve gives zero.
> Solve: d y d x equals negative six x over two y, which is negative three x over y. [pause]

## Example 2: A tangent line
**On screen:** "Find the equation for the line tangent to x³ + y³ = 9 at (1, 2)." Work: 1 + 8 = 9; d/dx(x³ + y³) = d/dx(9); 3x² + 3y² dy/dx = 0, so dy/dx = −x²/y²; at (1, 2), −1/4. The line y − 2 = −1/4(x − 1) is drawn on the curve.
> Find the equation for the line tangent to x cubed plus y cubed equals nine at one, two. [pause]
> Check the point first: one plus eight is nine. Good.
> Take d d x of both sides. Three x squared, plus three y squared d y d x, equals zero.
> So d y d x is negative x squared over y squared. [pause]
> At one, two, that's negative one fourth. The line is y minus two equals negative one fourth times x minus one.

## Example 3: Trig and exponential
**On screen:** "Find dy/dx for sin y + eˣ = y." Work: d/dx(sin y + eˣ) = d/dx(y); cos y · dy/dx + eˣ = dy/dx; dy/dx (1 − cos y) = eˣ; dy/dx = eˣ/(1 − cos y).
> Find d y d x for sine y plus e to the x equals y. [pause]
> Take d d x of both sides.
> Sine y gives cosine y, times d y d x. e to the x gives e to the x. And y gives d y d x.
> Move the d y d x terms together: d y d x times one minus cosine y equals e to the x. [pause]
> So d y d x is e to the x over one minus cosine y. [long pause]

## Example 4: Every tangent line at one x
**On screen:** "Find the equations of all lines tangent to x² + 4y² = 20 at x = 2." Work: d/dx(x² + 4y²) = d/dx(20); 2x + 8y dy/dx = 0; dy/dx = −x/(4y); 4 + 4y² = 20, so y = 2 or y = −2; at (2, 2), y − 2 = −1/4 (x − 2); at (2, −2), y + 2 = 1/4 (x − 2).
> Find the equations of all the lines tangent to x squared plus four y squared equals twenty at x equals two. [pause]
> Take d d x of both sides.
> That gives two x plus eight y d y d x equals zero. So d y d x is negative two x over eight y, which is negative x over four y. [pause]
> We need y. Put x equals two into the curve: four plus four y squared is twenty, so y squared is four, and y is two or negative two. Two points. [pause]
> At two, two, the slope is negative one fourth. So y minus two equals negative one fourth times x minus two.
> At two, negative two, the slope is one fourth. So y plus two equals one fourth times x minus two. [long pause]
