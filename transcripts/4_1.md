# 4.1 Interpreting the Meaning of the Derivative in Context

## A number with a story
**On screen:** A glass water tank on a plinth, with a gauge in liters up its side and a drain pipe and valve at the bottom. Water drips from the pipe and the level slowly drops. Beside it, the statement W′(5) = −3 appears, plain, with no words around it.
> Here's a statement: W prime of five equals negative three. [pause]
> On its own, it's just a number. In a real problem, that number has a story, and the AP exam expects you to tell it. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 4.1 · Interpreting the meaning of the derivative in context." Unit 4 · Contextual Applications of Differentiation.

## Units first
**On screen:** W(t) is labeled "liters" and t is labeled "minutes." A fraction builds: units of W′ = liters ÷ minutes = liters per minute. Two more examples appear: V(t) in gallons with t in hours gives gallons per hour; C(n) in dollars with n items gives dollars per item.
> Start with units. W of t is the water in the tank, in liters, t minutes after the drain opens. [pause]
> A derivative is a rate: output units per input unit. So W prime is measured in liters per minute.
> The pattern always holds. Gallons over hours gives gallons per hour. Dollars over items gives dollars per item. [long pause]

## The sentence
**On screen:** A sentence template with four colored slots: "At [t = 5 minutes], [the amount of water in the tank] is [decreasing] at a rate of [3 liters per minute]." Each slot fills in turn.
> Now the sentence. It has four parts. [pause]
> When: at t equals five minutes.
> What: the amount of water in the tank.
> Which way: the derivative is negative, so the amount is decreasing.
> How fast: at a rate of three liters per minute. [long pause]

## Draining a tank
**On screen:** The slots filled in one at a time, each on its own line: when, what, which way, how fast. Then the full sentence.
> W of t is the liters of water in a tank, t minutes after a drain opens. Interpret W prime of five equals negative three. [pause]
> Fill the slots. When: at five minutes. What: the amount of water. Which way: negative means decreasing.
> How fast: three liters per minute. [pause]
> So: at t equals five minutes, the amount of water in the tank is decreasing at a rate of three liters per minute. [long pause]

## Three wrong readings
**On screen:** Three crossed-out statements: "The tank holds −3 liters," "3 liters drain over the next minute," "The derivative is −3."
> Now three things the sentence must not say. [pause]
> It doesn't say the tank holds negative three liters. That would be W of five, the amount, not the rate.
> It doesn't promise that exactly three liters drain over the next minute. The rate can change during that minute.
> And "the derivative is negative three" isn't an interpretation at all. Context is the whole point. [long pause]

## Value, average, instant
**On screen:** Three rows: W(5), "the amount at t = 5," liters; [W(8) − W(2)]/(8 − 2), "the average rate on [2, 8]," liters per minute; W′(5), "the rate at the instant t = 5," liters per minute.
> Keep three things apart. [pause]
> W of five is an amount, in liters. The difference quotient over an interval is an average rate, in liters per minute. And W prime of five is the rate at one instant. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The completed sentence, with its four slots highlighted.
> When, what, which way, how fast. Say all four, with units, every time. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A cooling cup of coffee
**On screen:** A table for H(t), the temperature of coffee in °F, t minutes after it's poured: t = 0, 4, 10, 16; H = 180, 162, 141, 126, with a steaming mug beside it. "Estimate H′(7) and explain its meaning." The columns t = 4 and t = 10 light up. A line notes that this average rate on [4, 10] is our best estimate of the instantaneous rate H′(7).
> The table gives the temperature of a cup of coffee at a few times. Estimate H prime of seven, and explain what it means. [pause]
> Seven isn't in the table. Use the closest times on each side: four and ten.
> One forty-one minus one sixty-two, over ten minus four, is negative twenty-one over six: negative three point five. [pause]
> Now, we just said an average rate isn't an instantaneous rate, and that's still true. This is the average rate from four to ten. But when all we have are values in a table, it's our best estimate of the instantaneous rate, H prime of seven. [pause]
> At seven minutes, the temperature of the coffee is decreasing at about three point five degrees per minute.

## Example 2: Marginal cost
**On screen:** "C(n) is the cost, in dollars, of making n bicycles. C′(200) = 85. What are the units of C′, and what does C′(200) = 85 mean?" The units dollars per bicycle appear, then the sentence.
> C of n is the cost, in dollars, of making n bicycles, and C prime of two hundred is eighty-five. What does that mean? [pause]
> The units are dollars per bicycle.
> At two hundred bicycles, the cost is increasing at eighty-five dollars per bicycle. [pause]
> In practice, that means the two hundred and first bicycle costs about eighty-five dollars to make.

## Example 3: From a formula
**On screen:** "A ball's height is h(t) = −16t² + 64t feet after t seconds. Find h′(3) and interpret it." Work: h′(t) = −32t + 64; h′(3) = −32. The sentence: at t = 3 seconds, the height is decreasing at 32 feet per second.
> A ball's height is negative sixteen t squared plus sixty-four t, in feet. Find h prime of three, and interpret it. [pause]
> The derivative is negative thirty-two t plus sixty-four. At three seconds, that's negative thirty-two.
> At three seconds, the ball's height is decreasing at thirty-two feet per second. [pause]
> Notice the sentence says decreasing at thirty-two. The negative sign became the word. [long pause]
