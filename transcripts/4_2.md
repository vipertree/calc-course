# 4.2 Straight-Line Motion: Connecting Position, Velocity, and Acceleration

## Back and forth on a line
**On screen:** A dot slides along a horizontal number line: right, then back left, then right again. Beside it, the graph of its position x(t) = t³ − 6t² + 9t draws itself, rising, falling, then rising.
> Here's a particle moving along a straight line. It goes right, turns around, comes back, and turns around again. [pause]
> Its position is x of t equals t cubed minus six t squared plus nine t. The graph tracks the position over time. [pause]
> Careful: the graph is not the path. The particle only ever moves left and right on its line. The graph's height is its position. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 4.2 · Straight-line motion: connecting position, velocity, and acceleration." Unit 4 · Contextual Applications of Differentiation.

## Three functions
**On screen:** A chain: x(t) → v(t) = x′(t) → a(t) = v′(t) = x″(t). Under each, its units: meters, meters per second, meters per second per second. For the running example: v(t) = 3t² − 12t + 9 and a(t) = 6t − 12.
> Position, velocity, acceleration. Each is the derivative of the one before. [pause]
> Velocity is the rate of change of position. Acceleration is the rate of change of velocity.
> For our particle, the velocity is three t squared minus twelve t plus nine, and the acceleration is six t minus twelve. [long pause]

## Direction
**On screen:** A sign chart for v(t) = 3(t − 1)(t − 3): plus before 1, minus between 1 and 3, plus after 3. Arrows on the number line point right, left, right. At t = 1 and t = 3 the dot pauses.
> The sign of the velocity tells the direction. [pause]
> Velocity factors as three times t minus one times t minus three. It's positive before one, negative between one and three, and positive after three.
> So the particle moves right, then left, then right. At one and three it's momentarily at rest: that's where it turns around. [long pause]

## Speed
**On screen:** Velocity −5 and velocity 5 appear side by side, each with an arrow. Both have the same speed label: 5. The formula speed = |v(t)|.
> Speed is how fast, with no direction. It's the absolute value of the velocity. [pause]
> A velocity of negative five and a velocity of five have the same speed: five. [long pause]

## Speeding up and slowing down
**On screen:** A car on a road. First it rolls backward (v < 0) while braking (a > 0): its speedometer needle falls. A rule appears: speeding up when v and a have the same sign; slowing down when they have opposite signs.
> When is the particle speeding up? It's tempting to say "when the acceleration is positive." But that's not right. [pause]
> Picture a car rolling backward while the driver brakes. Its velocity is negative. The braking pushes forward, so the acceleration is positive. And the car is slowing down. [pause]
> The rule: the particle speeds up when velocity and acceleration have the same sign, and slows down when they have opposite signs. [long pause]

## Distance versus displacement
**On screen:** The number line with the particle's trip from t = 0 to t = 4: from 0 out to 4, back to 0, out to 4 again. Two labels: displacement = x(4) − x(0) = 4; distance = 4 + 4 + 4 = 12.
> How far did the particle go between zero and four seconds? [pause]
> Its position went from zero to four. That change, four, is the displacement. [pause]
> But it went out to four, back to zero, then out to four again. The total distance is twelve. To get it, split the trip at the turning points and add each leg. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The chain x → v → a, with three short reminders: sign of v = direction; |v| = speed; v and a agree = speeding up.
> Velocity tells direction. Speed is its size. And speeding up means velocity and acceleration agree. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: When is it moving left?
**On screen:** "x(t) = t³ − 6t² + 9t for t ≥ 0. When is the particle at rest? When is it moving left?" Work: v(t) = 3t² − 12t + 9 = 3(t − 1)(t − 3); at rest at t = 1 and t = 3; v < 0 for 1 < t < 3.
> For x of t equals t cubed minus six t squared plus nine t, when is the particle at rest, and when is it moving left? [pause]
> Find the velocity: three t squared minus twelve t plus nine. Factor out three: three times t minus one times t minus three.
> The velocity is zero at t equals one and t equals three. Those are the times it's at rest. [pause]
> Between them, the velocity is negative, so the particle moves left for t between one and three.

## Example 2: Speeding up or slowing down?
**On screen:** "Same particle. Is it speeding up or slowing down at t = 1.5? At t = 2.5?" Work: v(1.5) = −2.25, a(1.5) = −3: same sign, speeding up. v(2.5) = −2.25, a(2.5) = 3: opposite signs, slowing down.
> Same particle. Is it speeding up or slowing down at one and a half seconds, and at two and a half? [pause]
> At one and a half, the velocity is negative two point two five, and the acceleration is six times one and a half minus twelve: negative three. Same sign. Speeding up.
> At two and a half, the velocity is again negative two point two five, but the acceleration is positive three. Opposite signs. Slowing down. [pause]
> Same velocity both times, but different stories. That's why you need the acceleration too.

## Example 3: Total distance
**On screen:** "Find the total distance traveled from t = 0 to t = 4." Work: turning points t = 1, 3; x(0) = 0, x(1) = 4, x(3) = 0, x(4) = 4; distance = |4 − 0| + |0 − 4| + |4 − 0| = 12. Displacement x(4) − x(0) = 4.
> Find the total distance the particle travels from t equals zero to t equals four. [pause]
> It turns around at one and three, so split the trip there. The positions are zero, four, zero, and four.
> The legs have lengths four, four, and four. The total distance is twelve. [pause]
> The displacement is only four, because the trip doubled back. [long pause]
