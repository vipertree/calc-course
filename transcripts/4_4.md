# 4.4 Introduction to Related Rates

## A ripple
**On screen:** A stone drops into a pond. A circular ripple spreads out, its radius growing steadily. Two readouts tick upward: r, and the area A inside the ripple.
> A stone drops into a pond, and a ripple spreads out. Its radius grows at a steady two centimeters per second. [pause]
> The area inside the ripple grows too, but not steadily. The bigger the circle, the faster its area grows. [pause]
> The radius and the area are linked, so their rates are linked. That's what this topic is about. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 4.4 · Introduction to related rates." Unit 4 · Contextual Applications of Differentiation.

## Everything depends on time
**On screen:** The equation A = πr², with small labels "A(t)" and "r(t)" under each variable. A clock icon beside it.
> In this problem, both the radius and the area change as time passes. Each one is a function of t. [pause]
> The equation A equals pi r squared holds at every moment. So we can differentiate both sides with respect to time. [long pause]

## The chain rule on every variable
**On screen:** d/dt [A] = d/dt [πr²] becomes dA/dt = 2πr · dr/dt, with the factor dr/dt highlighted.
> On the left, the derivative of A with respect to t is d A d t. [pause]
> On the right, r squared is a function of t, so the chain rule applies: two r, times d r d t. [pause]
> That factor, d r d t, is the whole point. Every variable gets one. [long pause]

## Differentiate first, then substitute
**On screen:** Two paths from A = πr². Wrong: substitute r = 10 to get A = 100π, then differentiate to get 0, crossed out. Right: differentiate to get dA/dt = 2πr dr/dt, then substitute r = 10 and dr/dt = 2 to get 40π.
> Here's the trap. [pause] If you plug in r equals ten first, the area becomes the constant one hundred pi, and its derivative is zero. That's wrong: the area is growing.
> The radius is only ten for an instant. Differentiate first, while it's still a variable. Then substitute. [pause]
> Two pi times ten times two: the area grows at forty pi square centimeters per second, at that instant. [long pause]

## A growing square
**On screen:** A = s² differentiated with respect to t, then the values substituted.
> The side of a square grows at three centimeters per second. How fast is the area growing when the side is four? [try it]
> Differentiate A equals s squared with respect to t: d A d t is two s d s d t. [pause]
> Now substitute: two times four times three. Twenty-four square centimeters per second. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The steps in a column: relate the quantities; differentiate with respect to t; substitute.
> An equation that relates quantities also relates their rates. Differentiate with respect to time, then substitute. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: The ripple's area
**On screen:** "A ripple's radius grows at 2 cm/s. How fast is the area growing when r = 10 cm?" Work: A = πr²; dA/dt = 2πr dr/dt = 2π(10)(2) = 40π cm²/s.
> A ripple's radius grows at two centimeters per second. How fast is the area growing when the radius is ten? [pause]
> Relate them: A equals pi r squared.
> Differentiate with respect to t: d A d t equals two pi r d r d t. [pause]
> Substitute: two pi times ten times two. Forty pi square centimeters per second.

## Example 2: A point on a circle
**On screen:** "x² + y² = 25. When x = 3 and y = 4, dx/dt = 2. Find dy/dt." Work: 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt = 0; 2(3)(2) + 2(4) dy/dt = 0; dy/dt = −3/2.
> x squared plus y squared equals twenty-five. When x is three and y is four, x is increasing at two units per second. Find d y d t. [pause]
> Differentiate with respect to t: two x d x d t, plus two y d y d t, equals zero.
> Substitute: two times three times two, plus two times four times d y d t, equals zero. [pause]
> So d y d t is negative twelve over eight: negative three halves. y is decreasing.

## Example 3: A melting ice cube
**On screen:** "An ice cube's edge shrinks at 0.1 cm/min. How fast is its volume changing when the edge is 5 cm?" Work: V = s³; dV/dt = 3s² ds/dt = 3(25)(−0.1) = −7.5 cm³/min.
> An ice cube's edge shrinks at a tenth of a centimeter per minute. How fast is its volume changing when the edge is five centimeters? [pause]
> V equals s cubed, so d V d t equals three s squared d s d t.
> The edge is shrinking, so d s d t is negative one tenth. [pause]
> Three times twenty-five times negative one tenth: the volume is decreasing at seven and a half cubic centimeters per minute. [long pause]
