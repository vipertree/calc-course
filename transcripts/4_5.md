# 4.5 Solving Related Rates Problems

## A sliding ladder
**On screen:** A wooden ladder leans against a brick wall. Its foot slides away from the wall at a steady pace, and its top slips down the wall, slowly at first and then faster and faster. Readouts show both speeds.
> A ladder leans against a wall, and its foot slides away at a steady pace. [pause]
> Watch the top. It barely moves at first. Then it drops faster and faster. [pause]
> The foot moves at a constant rate, but the top doesn't. How fast is the top falling at a given moment? Last lesson we wrote the equations and differentiated them. Now we put in the numbers and solve. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 4.5 · Solving related rates problems." Unit 4 · Contextual Applications of Differentiation.

## Six steps
**On screen:** A list builds up: 1. Know: the rates and values given, with units, and anything fixed. 2. Want: the rate you're after. 3. Equation: relate the quantities (fixed ones stay numbers). 4. Differentiate with respect to t. 5. Substitute the values at the instant, and solve. 6. Answer with units, and say what it means.
> Every problem goes the same way, in six steps. [pause]
> One: know. Draw and label the picture, and write down the rates and values you're given, with units. If something never changes, such as the length of a ladder, note that here too. [pause]
> Two: want. Name the rate you're looking for. [pause]
> Three: write the equation, the same way as in the last topic. Anything fixed goes in as a number. [pause]
> Four: differentiate both sides with respect to t. [pause]
> Five: substitute the values for the instant you're asked about, and solve for the rate you want. [pause]
> Six: answer with units, and say what the number means. [long pause]

## Differentiate first, then substitute
**On screen:** Two paths from A = πr². Wrong: substitute r = 10 to get A = 100π, then differentiate to get 0, crossed out. Right: differentiate to get dA/dt = 2πr dr/dt; then r = 10 and dr/dt = 2 go in.
> The order matters. [pause] If you plug in r equals ten first, the area becomes the constant one hundred pi, and its derivative is zero. That's wrong: the area is growing.
> The radius is only ten for an instant. Differentiate first, while it's still a variable. [pause]
> Only then do the values for that instant go in. [long pause]

## Shrinking means negative
**On screen:** A car drives west along a road toward an intersection, a brace under its distance x from the intersection counting down from 80 m to 40 m, then the label dx/dt = −20 m/s. A note: shrinking, a negative rate; growing, a positive rate.
> A rate's sign says which way the quantity goes. [pause]
> This car drives toward the intersection at twenty meters per second. Its distance x from the intersection is shrinking, so d x d t is negative twenty, not twenty. [pause]
> In the Know step, a shrinking quantity always gets a negative rate. And when an answer comes out negative, it means that quantity is shrinking. [long pause]

## The ripple
**On screen:** "A stone drops into a pond, and a circular ripple spreads out. Its radius grows at 2 cm/s. How fast is the area inside the ripple growing when the radius is 10 cm?" The pond with rings and the radius r. Work: know dr/dt = 2 cm/s, r = 10 cm; want dA/dt; A = πr²; dA/dt = 2πr dr/dt; dA/dt = 2π(10)(2); dA/dt = 40π ≈ 126 cm²/s; the area grows at about 126 cm²/s.
> A stone drops into a pond, and a circular ripple spreads out. Its radius grows at two centimeters per second. How fast is the area inside the ripple growing when the radius is ten centimeters? [pause]
> Know: d r d t is two centimeters per second, and at this instant, r is ten.
> Want: d A d t.
> The equation is the one from last lesson: A equals pi r squared.
> Differentiate: d A d t equals two pi r, d r d t.
> Now substitute: two pi, times ten, times two.
> That's forty pi.
> So the area grows at forty pi square centimeters per second, about one hundred twenty-six. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The six steps again, with "Differentiate" and "Substitute and solve" highlighted.
> Know, want, an equation, differentiate, substitute and solve, and answer with units. Differentiate before you substitute. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: The ladder
**On screen:** "A 13-ft ladder leans against a wall, and its bottom slides away from the wall at 2 ft/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the bottom is 5 ft from the wall?" The brick wall and ladder labeled x, y, 13. Work: know dx/dt = 2 ft/s, x = 5 ft, the ladder is fixed at 13 ft; want dy/dt; x² + y² = 13² (13 is fixed); 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt = 0; at x = 5, y = √(169 − 25) = 12; 2(5)(2) + 2(12) dy/dt = 0; 20 + 24 dy/dt = 0; dy/dt = −20/24 = −5/6 ft/s; the top slides down at 5/6 ft/s.
> A thirteen-foot ladder leans against a wall, and its bottom slides away from the wall at two feet per second. How fast is the top sliding down when the bottom is five feet from the wall? [pause]
> Know: d x d t is two feet per second, and at this instant, x is five. The ladder's length is fixed at thirteen feet.
> Want: d y d t, how fast the height of the top changes.
> The equation from last lesson: x squared plus y squared equals thirteen squared. The thirteen is fixed, so it stays a number.
> Differentiate: two x d x d t, plus two y d y d t, equals zero.
> To substitute, we need y at this instant too. When x is five, y is the square root of one sixty-nine minus twenty-five, which is twelve.
> Now substitute: two times five times two, plus two times twelve times d y d t, equals zero.
> That's twenty plus twenty-four d y d t equals zero.
> So d y d t is negative twenty over twenty-four: negative five sixths of a foot per second.
> Negative, because the height is shrinking. The top slides down at five sixths of a foot per second. [long pause]

## Example 2: Two cars
**On screen:** "Priya drives west toward an intersection at 20 m/s, and Kai drives north away from it at 15 m/s. How fast is the distance between the two cars changing when Priya is 80 m from the intersection and Kai is 60 m from it?" The two roads, Priya's car facing the intersection, Kai's car heading north, x, y and z with rate arrows. Work: know dx/dt = −20 m/s (x shrinking), x = 80 m, dy/dt = 15 m/s, y = 60 m; want dz/dt; z² = x² + y²; 2z dz/dt = 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt; z = √(6400 + 3600) = 100 m; 2(100) dz/dt = 2(80)(−20) + 2(60)(15); 200 dz/dt = −3200 + 1800 = −1400; dz/dt = −7 m/s; the distance is shrinking at 7 m/s.
> Priya drives west toward an intersection at twenty meters per second, and Kai drives north away from it at fifteen meters per second. How fast is the distance between the two cars changing when Priya is eighty meters from the intersection and Kai is sixty meters from it?
> Know: Priya is heading toward the intersection, so her distance x is shrinking. d x d t is negative twenty meters per second, and x is eighty.
> Kai is heading away, so d y d t is positive fifteen, and y is sixty.
> Want: d z d t, where z is the distance between the cars.
> The equation from last lesson: z squared equals x squared plus y squared.
> Differentiate: two z d z d t equals two x d x d t plus two y d y d t.
> We need z at this instant: the square root of sixty-four hundred plus thirty-six hundred, which is one hundred.
> Substitute: two times one hundred, d z d t, equals two times eighty times negative twenty, plus two times sixty times fifteen.
> That's two hundred d z d t equals negative thirty-two hundred plus eighteen hundred, which is negative fourteen hundred.
> So d z d t is negative seven meters per second.
> Negative means the distance is shrinking. The cars are getting closer at seven meters per second, even though Kai is driving away. [long pause]

## Example 3: A shadow
**On screen:** "Ana, who is 5 ft tall, walks away from a 15-ft lamppost at 4 ft/s. How fast is her shadow lengthening when she is 10 ft from the post?" The lamppost, Ana walking away, and her shadow, labeled 15, 5, x and s. Work: know dx/dt = 4 ft/s, x = 10 ft, the heights are fixed at 15 ft and 5 ft; want ds/dt; 15/(x + s) = 5/s, so s = x/2; ds/dt = ½ dx/dt; ds/dt = ½(4) = 2 ft/s; the shadow lengthens at 2 ft/s.
> Ana is five feet tall, and she walks away from a fifteen-foot lamppost at four feet per second. How fast is her shadow lengthening when she is ten feet from the post?
> Know: d x d t is four feet per second, and x is ten. The heights are fixed: fifteen feet and five feet.
> Want: d s d t, where s is the length of her shadow.
> The equation from last lesson: the similar triangles gave fifteen over x plus s equals five over s, and that simplified to s equals x over two.
> Differentiate: d s d t is one half of d x d t.
> Substitute: one half of four.
> So the shadow lengthens at two feet per second.
> Notice that x equals ten never went in. The shadow grows at two feet per second wherever she is. [long pause]

## Example 4: Filling a cone
**On screen:** "Water pours into a cone, point down, that is 9 ft tall with radius 3 ft at the top, at 2 ft³/min. How fast is the water rising when it is 6 ft deep? (The volume of a cone is V = ⅓πr²h.)" The glass cone labeled h, r, 9, 3. Work: know dV/dt = 2 ft³/min, h = 6 ft, the cone is fixed at 9 ft tall with radius 3 ft at the top; want dh/dt; r = h/3, so V = (π/27)h³; dV/dt = (π/9)h² dh/dt; 2 = (π/9)(6)² dh/dt; 2 = 4π dh/dt; dh/dt = 1/(2π) ≈ 0.159 ft/min; the water rises at about 0.159 ft/min.
> Water pours into a cone, point down, that is nine feet tall with a radius of three feet at the top, at two cubic feet per minute. How fast is the water rising when it's six feet deep? The problem gives the cone's volume formula.
> Know: d V d t is two cubic feet per minute, and h is six. The cone is fixed: nine feet tall, radius three at the top.
> Want: d h d t.
> The equation from last lesson: similar triangles gave r equals h over three, so V equals pi over twenty-seven, h cubed.
> Differentiate: d V d t equals pi over nine, h squared, d h d t.
> Substitute d V d t equals two and h equals six: two equals pi over nine, times six squared, d h d t.
> Pi over nine times thirty-six is four pi, so two equals four pi d h d t.
> So d h d t is two over four pi: one over two pi.
> The water rises at one over two pi feet per minute, about point one five nine. [long pause]
