# 4.5 Solving Related Rates Problems

## A sliding ladder
**On screen:** A ladder leans against a wall. Its foot slides away from the wall at a steady pace, and its top slips down the wall, slowly at first and then faster and faster.
> A ladder leans against a wall, and its foot slides away at a steady pace. [pause]
> Watch the top. It barely moves at first. Then it drops faster and faster. [pause]
> The foot moves at a constant rate, but the top doesn't. How fast is the top falling at a given moment? That's a related rates problem. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 4.5 · Solving related rates problems." Unit 4 · Contextual Applications of Differentiation.

## Five steps
**On screen:** A list builds up: 1. Draw and label. 2. Rates with signs. 3. An equation. 4. Differentiate with respect to t. 5. Substitute, then solve.
> Every related rates problem goes the same way. [pause]
> Draw a picture. Label everything that changes with a letter, and everything fixed with its number. [pause]
> Write down the rates you know and the rate you want. A quantity that's shrinking has a negative rate. [pause]
> Find an equation that relates the quantities and holds at every moment. [pause]
> Differentiate both sides with respect to t. Only then substitute the numbers for the moment you're asked about, and solve. [long pause]

## Where the equation comes from
**On screen:** Four small cards: a right triangle with x² + y² = z²; two similar triangles; a cone with V = (1/3)πr²h; a right triangle with tan θ = y/x.
> The equation almost always comes from geometry. [pause]
> Right triangles give the Pythagorean theorem. Shadows and cones give similar triangles. [pause]
> Tanks and balloons give volume formulas, and angles give right-triangle trig. [long pause]

## Eliminate first
**On screen:** A cone, point down, with water inside. The water's radius r and depth h are labeled, and the matching similar triangles light up: r/h = 3/9, so r = h/3.
> Sometimes the equation has one variable too many. [pause]
> In a cone of water, both the radius and the depth change. But the water is always the same shape as the cone, so similar triangles tie them together. [pause]
> Write the radius in terms of the depth before you differentiate, and the extra variable disappears. [long pause]

## A shadow
**On screen:** The lamppost, Ana, and her shadow, with the two similar triangles; the proportion, s = x/2, and ds/dt = 2.
> Ana is five feet tall, and she walks away from a fifteen-foot lamppost at four feet per second. How fast is her shadow lengthening? [try it]
> Let x be her distance from the post and s her shadow's length. The big triangle and the small one are similar: fifteen over x plus s equals five over s.
> Cross-multiply and simplify: s equals x over two. [pause]
> Differentiate: d s d t is one half of d x d t, which is two feet per second, at every moment. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The five steps again, with "Differentiate, then substitute" highlighted.
> Picture, rates with signs, an equation, differentiate, then substitute. The order matters. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: The ladder
**On screen:** "A 13-ft ladder leans against a wall. The bottom slides away at 2 ft/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the bottom is 5 ft from the wall?" Work: x² + y² = 169; 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt = 0; at x = 5, y = 12; 2(5)(2) + 2(12) dy/dt = 0; dy/dt = −5/6 ft/s.
> A thirteen-foot ladder leans against a wall. The bottom slides away at two feet per second. How fast is the top sliding down when the bottom is five feet from the wall? [pause]
> The ladder is the hypotenuse: x squared plus y squared equals one hundred sixty-nine.
> Differentiate: two x d x d t, plus two y d y d t, equals zero. [pause]
> When x is five, y is twelve, from the Pythagorean theorem.
> Substitute: two times five times two, plus two times twelve times d y d t, equals zero.
> So d y d t is negative twenty over twenty-four: negative five sixths. The top slides down at five sixths of a foot per second.

## Example 2: Filling a cone
**On screen:** "Water pours into a cone, point down, 9 ft tall with radius 3 ft, at 2 ft³/min. How fast is the water rising when it is 6 ft deep?" Work: r = h/3; V = (π/27)h³; dV/dt = (π/9)h² dh/dt; 2 = 4π dh/dt; dh/dt = 1/(2π) ft/min.
> Water pours into a cone, point down, nine feet tall with a radius of three feet, at two cubic feet per minute. How fast is the water rising when it's six feet deep? [pause]
> Similar triangles: r over h equals three over nine, so r equals h over three.
> Substitute that into the volume: one third pi times h over three squared, times h. That's pi over twenty-seven, h cubed. [pause]
> Differentiate: d V d t equals pi over nine, h squared, d h d t.
> At h equals six: two equals four pi d h d t.
> So the water rises at one over two pi feet per minute, about point one six.

## Example 3: Two cars
**On screen:** "Two cars leave an intersection at the same time, one north at 30 mph and one east at 40 mph. How fast is the distance between them growing after 2 hours?" Work: z² = x² + y²; 2z dz/dt = 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt; x = 80, y = 60, z = 100; 100 dz/dt = 80(40) + 60(30) = 5000; dz/dt = 50 mph.
> Two cars leave an intersection at the same time, one heading north at thirty miles per hour and one heading east at forty. How fast is the distance between them growing after two hours? [pause]
> The distance is the hypotenuse: z squared equals x squared plus y squared.
> Differentiate and cancel the twos: z d z d t equals x d x d t plus y d y d t. [pause]
> After two hours, x is eighty, y is sixty, and z is one hundred.
> One hundred d z d t equals eighty times forty plus sixty times thirty: five thousand.
> So the distance grows at fifty miles per hour. [long pause]
