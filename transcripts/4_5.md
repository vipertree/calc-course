# 4.5 Solving Related Rates Problems

## A sliding ladder
**On screen:** A wooden ladder leans against a brick wall. Its foot slides away from the wall at a steady pace, and its top slips down the wall, slowly at first and then faster and faster. Readouts show both speeds.
> A ladder leans against a wall, and its foot slides away at a steady pace. [pause]
> Watch the top. It barely moves at first. Then it drops faster and faster. [pause]
> The foot moves at a constant rate, but the top doesn't. How fast is the top falling at a given moment? Last lesson we set problems like this up. Now we solve them. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 4.5 · Solving related rates problems." Unit 4 · Contextual Applications of Differentiation.

## Six steps
**On screen:** The five setup steps from 4.4 (know, want, fixed, equation, differentiate), then a sixth: substitute and solve, with units.
> You already know the first five steps: know, want, fixed, an equation, and differentiate. [pause]
> Step six is new: substitute the values for the instant you're asked about, and solve for the rate you want. Give units, and say what it means. [pause]
> The order matters. Differentiate first, then substitute. [long pause]

## Where the equation comes from
**On screen:** Four small cards: a right triangle with x² + y² = z²; two similar triangles; a glass cone with V = (1/3)πr²h, marked "given"; a right triangle with tan θ = y/x.
> The equation almost always comes from geometry. [pause]
> Right triangles give the Pythagorean theorem. Shadows and cones give similar triangles. [pause]
> Tanks and balloons give volume formulas, and the problem gives you V equals one third pi r squared h when you need it. Angles give right-triangle trig. [long pause]

## Eliminate first
**On screen:** A glass cone, point down, with water inside. The water's radius r and depth h are labeled, with the cone's 9 and 3. The similar triangles give r/h = 3/9, so r = h/3, and V = (π/27)h³.
> Sometimes the equation has one variable too many. [pause]
> In a cone of water, both the radius and the depth change. But the water is always the same shape as the cone, so similar triangles tie them together. [pause]
> Write the radius in terms of the depth before you differentiate, and the extra variable disappears. [long pause]

## A shadow
**On screen:** The lamppost, Ana walking away, and her shadow, labeled 15, 5, x and s. Work: set up in 4.4 (know dx/dt = 4, want ds/dt, fixed 15 and 5); s = x/2 and ds/dt = ½ dx/dt; ds/dt = ½(4) = 2 ft/s.
> Ana is five feet tall, and she walks away from a fifteen-foot lamppost at four feet per second. How fast is her shadow lengthening? We set this one up last lesson. [pause]
> Know: d x d t is four. Want: d s d t. Fixed: the heights, fifteen and five.
> The similar triangles gave s equals x over two, so d s d t is one half of d x d t. [pause]
> Substitute and solve: one half of four is two feet per second.
> And notice: it's two at every moment, no matter where she is. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: The ladder
**On screen:** "A 13-ft ladder leans against a wall. The bottom slides away at 2 ft/s. How fast is the top sliding down when the bottom is 5 ft from the wall?" The brick wall and ladder labeled x, y, 13. Work: know dx/dt = 2, x = 5, want dy/dt, fixed 13; x² + y² = 13², 2x dx/dt + 2y dy/dt = 0; at x = 5, y = 12; 2(5)(2) + 2(12) dy/dt = 0; dy/dt = −5/6 ft/s; the top slides down at 5/6 ft/s.
> A thirteen-foot ladder leans against a wall. The bottom slides away at two feet per second. How fast is the top sliding down when the bottom is five feet from the wall? We set this up in the last lesson. [pause]
> Know: d x d t is two, and x is five. Want: d y d t. Fixed: the ladder, thirteen feet.
> The equation was x squared plus y squared equals thirteen squared, and differentiating gave two x d x d t plus two y d y d t equals zero.
> Now substitute. We need y at this instant: when x is five, y is the square root of one sixty-nine minus twenty-five, which is twelve.
> Two times five times two, plus two times twelve times d y d t, equals zero.
> So d y d t is negative twenty over twenty-four: negative five sixths. The top slides down at five sixths of a foot per second.

## Example 2: Filling a cone
**On screen:** "Water pours into a cone, point down, 9 ft tall with radius 3 ft, at 2 ft³/min. How fast is the water rising when it is 6 ft deep? (The volume of a cone is V = ⅓πr²h.)" The glass cone labeled h, r, 9, 3. Work: know dV/dt = 2, h = 6, want dh/dt, fixed r = h/3; V = (π/27)h³, dV/dt = (π/9)h² dh/dt; 2 = 4π dh/dt; dh/dt = 1/(2π) ≈ 0.16 ft/min.
> Water pours into a cone, point down, nine feet tall with a radius of three feet, at two cubic feet per minute. How fast is the water rising when it's six feet deep? The problem gives you V equals one third pi r squared h. [pause]
> Know: d V d t is two, and h is six. Want: d h d t. Fixed: the cone's shape, so r equals h over three.
> From the setup: V is pi over twenty-seven, h cubed, and d V d t equals pi over nine, h squared, d h d t.
> Substitute h equals six and d V d t equals two: two equals pi over nine times thirty-six, d h d t. That's four pi d h d t. [pause]
> So the water rises at one over two pi feet per minute, about point one six.

## Example 3: Two cars
**On screen:** "Two cars leave an intersection at the same time, one north at 30 mph and one east at 40 mph. How fast is the distance between them growing after 2 hours?" The two roads, cars, x, y and z. Work: know dx/dt = 40, dy/dt = 30, t = 2, want dz/dt; z² = x² + y², z dz/dt = x dx/dt + y dy/dt; x = 80, y = 60, z = 100; 100 dz/dt = 80(40) + 60(30) = 5000; dz/dt = 50 mph.
> Two cars leave an intersection at the same time, one heading north at thirty miles per hour and one heading east at forty. How fast is the distance between them growing after two hours? [pause]
> Know: d x d t is forty and d y d t is thirty. The instant is two hours in. Want: d z d t.
> From the setup: z squared equals x squared plus y squared. Differentiate and cancel the twos: z d z d t equals x d x d t plus y d y d t.
> Now the values at two hours: x is eighty, y is sixty, and z is the square root of sixty-four hundred plus thirty-six hundred, one hundred. [pause]
> One hundred d z d t equals eighty times forty plus sixty times thirty: five thousand.
> So the distance grows at fifty miles per hour.

## Example 4: The ripple's area
**On screen:** "A ripple's radius grows at 2 cm/s. How fast is the area growing when r = 10 cm?" The pond with the radius r. Work: know dr/dt = 2, r = 10, want dA/dt; A = πr², dA/dt = 2πr dr/dt; dA/dt = 2π(10)(2) = 40π ≈ 126 cm²/s.
> Back to the ripple: its radius grows at two centimeters per second. How fast is the area growing when the radius is ten? [pause]
> Know: d r d t is two, and r is ten. Want: d A d t.
> From the setup: d A d t equals two pi r d r d t.
> Substitute: two pi times ten times two. Forty pi square centimeters per second, about one hundred twenty-six. [long pause]
