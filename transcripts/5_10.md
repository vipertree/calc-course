# 5.10 Introduction to Optimization Problems

## A fixed amount of fence
**On screen:** A river along the top of the screen. A rectangular pen hangs off the river, fenced on three sides. As the sides perpendicular to the river grow and shrink, the side along the river changes to use exactly 200 feet of fence. An area readout changes, and a graph of area against x is traced to the right.
> Here's a pen along a river. We have two hundred feet of fence, and the river side needs none. [pause]
> Make the pen deep and narrow, and the area is small. Make it shallow and wide, and the area is small again. [pause]
> Somewhere in between is the biggest pen. The graph of area against x shows exactly where. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 5.10 · Introduction to optimization problems." Unit 5 · Analytical Applications of Differentiation.

## One quantity, one variable
**On screen:** The pen with its sides labeled x, x and y. "Area: A = xy." "Constraint: 2x + y = 200." Then y is replaced: "A(x) = x(200 − 2x)." Then "domain: 0 < x < 100."
> Every optimization problem starts the same way. Name the quantity you want to make biggest or smallest. Here, the area: x times y. [pause]
> That has two variables. The constraint, two hundred feet of fence, connects them: two x plus y is two hundred. [pause]
> Solve for y and substitute. Now the area is a function of one variable: x times two hundred minus two x. [pause]
> Last, the domain. x has to be positive, and two x can't use more than two hundred feet. So x is between zero and one hundred. [long pause]

## Setting up
**On screen:** The constraint solved for ℓ, the area in one variable, and the domain.
> A rectangle has perimeter forty. Write its area as a function of its width w, and give the domain. [try it]
> The constraint: two w plus two l is forty, so l is twenty minus w.
> The area is w times l: w times twenty minus w. [pause]
> Both sides must be positive, so w is between zero and twenty. [long pause]

## Then it's a Unit 5 problem
**On screen:** "A′(x) = 200 − 4x = 0 at x = 50." "A″(x) = −4 < 0: maximum." "A(50) = 50 · 100 = 5000 ft²."
> Now it's the same problem as the last few topics: find the absolute maximum of a function on an interval. [pause]
> A prime of x is two hundred minus four x, which is zero at fifty. A double prime is negative, so that's a maximum. The biggest pen is five thousand square feet. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** Four steps: "1. Name the quantity. 2. Write it with the variables. 3. Use the constraint to get one variable. 4. Find the domain." Then: "Then find the absolute max or min."
> Name the quantity, use the constraint to get one variable, find the domain, and then you're back on familiar ground. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Largest product
**On screen:** "Two numbers add to 20. What is the largest possible product?" Work: P = xy, x + y = 20; P(x) = x(20 − x) = 20x − x²; P′(x) = 20 − 2x = 0 at x = 10; P″ = −2 < 0; largest product 10 · 10 = 100.
> Two numbers add to twenty. What is the largest possible product? [pause]
> The product is x times y, and the constraint is x plus y equals twenty.
> So the product is x times twenty minus x. [pause]
> Its derivative is twenty minus two x, zero at ten. The second derivative is negative, so that's a maximum.
> Both numbers are ten, and the largest product is one hundred.

## Example 2: The riverside pen
**On screen:** "A rectangular pen along a river uses 200 ft of fence on three sides. What dimensions give the largest area?" Work: A(x) = x(200 − 2x), 0 < x < 100; A′(x) = 200 − 4x = 0 at x = 50; A″ = −4 < 0, the only critical point: absolute max; 50 ft by 100 ft, area 5000 ft².
> A rectangular pen along a river uses two hundred feet of fence on three sides. What dimensions give the largest area? [pause]
> With x as each side perpendicular to the river, the area is x times two hundred minus two x, for x between zero and one hundred.
> A prime is two hundred minus four x, zero at fifty. [pause]
> A double prime is negative, and it's the only critical point, so it's the absolute maximum.
> The pen is fifty feet by one hundred feet, with an area of five thousand square feet.

## Example 3: A number and its reciprocal
**On screen:** "Find the positive number x for which x + 9/x is as small as possible." Work: f′(x) = 1 − 9/x² = 0 at x = 3; f″(x) = 18/x³ > 0; only critical point on (0, ∞): absolute min; f(3) = 6.
> Find the positive number x for which x plus nine over x is as small as possible. [pause]
> The derivative is one minus nine over x squared, which is zero at x equals three.
> The second derivative, eighteen over x cubed, is positive. [pause]
> It's the only critical point for positive x, so it's the absolute minimum. At three, the sum is six. [long pause]
