# 5.12 Exploring Behaviors of Implicit Relations

## A curve that isn't a function
**On screen:** The curve y² = x³ − 3x + 3 drawn in full: a loop-free curve with an upper and a lower branch. A tangent line rides along it, flattening at some points and standing straight up at others.
> This curve comes from the equation y squared equals x cubed minus three x plus three. It isn't the graph of a function: some x values have two y values. [pause]
> Ride a tangent line along it. In some places the tangent lies flat. In one place it stands straight up. [pause]
> Implicit differentiation tells us exactly where. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 5.12 · Exploring behaviors of implicit relations." Unit 5 · Analytical Applications of Differentiation.

## Top and bottom of the slope
**On screen:** dy/dx = N/D, with N labeled "top" and D labeled "bottom." Two cards: "horizontal tangent: N = 0, D ≠ 0" with a flat tangent; "vertical tangent: D = 0, N ≠ 0" with an upright tangent.
> Implicit differentiation gives d y d x as a fraction, with x and y in it. [pause]
> Where the top is zero and the bottom isn't, the slope is zero: a horizontal tangent. [pause]
> Where the bottom is zero and the top isn't, the slope blows up: a vertical tangent. [long pause]

## Back to the curve
**On screen:** The circle x² + y² = 25 with the line x = 0 drawn through it. The line meets the circle at (0, 5) and (0, −5), which light up.
> One more step people forget. Setting the top equal to zero gives a line or a curve of candidates, not points. [pause]
> For the circle, the top is zero along the whole line x equals zero. Only the two places where that line meets the circle count: zero, five and zero, negative five. [long pause]

## Concavity
**On screen:** "d²y/dx²: differentiate dy/dx again, then substitute dy/dx." At a horizontal tangent: "d²y/dx² > 0: relative minimum; < 0: relative maximum."
> To see how the curve bends, differentiate d y d x again, and substitute d y d x wherever it appears. [pause]
> At a horizontal tangent, a positive second derivative means the curve bottoms out there. Negative means it peaks. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Horizontal: top = 0. Vertical: bottom = 0. Then solve with the curve's equation."
> Top zero, horizontal. Bottom zero, vertical. And always solve with the curve's own equation. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A shifted circle
**On screen:** "Find the points on x² + y² − 4x + 6y = 12 where the tangent is horizontal, and where it is vertical." Work: 2x + 2yy′ − 4 + 6y′ = 0, y′ = (2 − x)/(y + 3); horizontal: x = 2, y² + 6y − 16 = 0, (2, 2) and (2, −8); vertical: y = −3, x² − 4x − 21 = 0, (7, −3) and (−3, −3).
> Find the points on x squared plus y squared minus four x plus six y equals twelve where the tangent is horizontal, and where it's vertical. [pause]
> Differentiate: two x plus two y y prime minus four plus six y prime equals zero. Solve: y prime is two minus x, over y plus three.
> Horizontal where the top is zero: x equals two. Put that in the curve: y squared plus six y minus sixteen is zero, so y is two or negative eight. [pause]
> The points are two, two and two, negative eight.
> Vertical where the bottom is zero: y equals negative three. Then x squared minus four x minus twenty-one is zero, so x is seven or negative three: the points seven, negative three and negative three, negative three.

## Example 2: A cubic curve
**On screen:** "Find the points on y² = x³ − 3x + 3 where the tangent is horizontal." Work: 2yy′ = 3x² − 3, y′ = (3x² − 3)/(2y); top zero at x = ±1; x = 1: y² = 1, (1, 1), (1, −1); x = −1: y² = 5, (−1, √5), (−1, −√5).
> Find the points on y squared equals x cubed minus three x plus three where the tangent is horizontal. [pause]
> Differentiate: two y y prime equals three x squared minus three. So y prime is three x squared minus three, over two y.
> The top is zero at x equals one and negative one. [pause]
> At x equals one, y squared is one: the points one, one and one, negative one.
> At x equals negative one, y squared is five: the points negative one, root five and negative one, negative root five.

## Example 3: Which way it bends
**On screen:** "On y² = x³ − 3x + 3, find d²y/dx² at (1, 1). What does the curve have there?" Work: differentiate 2yy′ = 3x² − 3: 2(y′)² + 2yy″ = 6x; at (1, 1), y′ = 0: 2y″ = 6, y″ = 3 > 0; horizontal tangent and concave up: a relative minimum.
> On the same curve, find the second derivative at one, one. What does the curve have there? [pause]
> Differentiate two y y prime equals three x squared minus three again. The product rule gives two y prime squared plus two y y double prime equals six x.
> At one, one, y prime is zero. So two y double prime equals six, and y double prime is three. [pause]
> A horizontal tangent where the curve is concave up: a relative minimum. [long pause]
