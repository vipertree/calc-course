# 5.12 Exploring Behaviors of Implicit Relations

## A curve that isn't a function
**On screen:** The curve y² = x³ − 3x + 3 drawn in full: a loop-free curve with an upper and a lower branch. A tangent line rides along it, flattening at some points and standing straight up at others.
> This curve comes from the equation y squared equals x cubed minus three x plus three. It isn't the graph of a function: some x values have two y values. [pause]
> Ride a tangent line along it. In some places the tangent lies flat. In one place it stands straight up. [pause]
> Implicit differentiation tells us exactly where. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 5.12 · Exploring behaviors of implicit relations." Unit 5 · Analytical Applications of Differentiation.

## N and D
**On screen:** dy/dx written as a fraction. The numerator is boxed and named N ("numerator"), the denominator boxed and named D ("denominator"): dy/dx = N/D. Then two cards: "N = 0, D ≠ 0: slope 0, horizontal tangent" with a flat tangent; then, after a pause, "D = 0, N ≠ 0: slope goes off to infinity, vertical tangent" with an upright tangent. Last, a small note: "N = 0 and D = 0: look more closely."
> Implicit differentiation often gives d y d x as a fraction, with x and y in it. [pause]
> Let's name the pieces. Call the numerator N and the denominator D. We can study each one on its own. [pause]
> Where N is zero and D isn't, the slope is zero divided by something: zero. That's a horizontal tangent. [pause]
> Now think about this one. What would make the slope go off to infinity? [long pause]
> A denominator heading to zero. Where D is zero and N isn't, the tangent line gets steeper and steeper until it stands straight up: a vertical tangent. [pause]
> And if N and D are both zero, this test can't decide. You have to look more closely. [long pause]

## Back to the curve
**On screen:** The circle x² + y² = 25 with the line x = 0 drawn through it. The line meets the circle at (0, 5) and (0, −5), which light up.
> One more step people forget. Setting N equal to zero gives a line or a curve of candidates, not points. [pause]
> For the circle, d y d x is negative x over y, so N is zero along the whole line x equals zero. Only the two places where that line meets the circle count: zero, five and zero, negative five. [long pause]

## Not on the curve
**On screen:** The hyperbola xy = 1, both branches. "dy/dx = −y/x": N = −y, D = x. The dashed line x = 0 is drawn, labeled "D = 0 here". The branches run alongside it without touching. A note: "no point of the curve has x = 0: no tangent line at all".
> Here's a trap. Take the curve x y equals one. Implicit differentiation gives d y d x equals negative y over x. [pause]
> The denominator is zero when x is zero. So is there a vertical tangent there? [pause]
> Check the curve. If x is zero, then x y is zero, and it can never equal one. No point of the curve has x equals zero. [pause]
> So there's no tangent line there at all, vertical or otherwise. The curve isn't even defined there. Before you call something a vertical tangent, make sure the point is on the curve. [long pause]

## Concavity
**On screen:** "d²y/dx²: differentiate dy/dx again, then substitute dy/dx." At a horizontal tangent: "d²y/dx² > 0: relative minimum; < 0: relative maximum."
> To see how the curve bends, differentiate d y d x again, and substitute d y d x wherever it appears. [pause]
> At a horizontal tangent, a positive second derivative means the curve bottoms out there. Negative means it peaks. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Horizontal: N = 0. Vertical: D = 0. Then solve with the curve's equation, and check the point is on the curve."
> N zero, horizontal. D zero, vertical. And always solve with the curve's own equation, so you know the point is really there. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A shifted circle
**On screen:** "Find the points on x² + y² − 4x + 6y = 12 where the tangent is horizontal, and where it is vertical." Board: 2x + 2yy′ − 4 + 6y′ = 0; y′(2y + 6) = 4 − 2x; y′ = (2 − x)/(y + 3): N = 2 − x, D = y + 3; horizontal: N = 0, x = 2; 4 + y² − 8 + 6y = 12, y² + 6y − 16 = 0, (y + 8)(y − 2) = 0; (2, 2) and (2, −8), D ≠ 0; vertical: D = 0, y = −3; x² + 9 − 4x − 18 = 12, x² − 4x − 21 = 0, (x − 7)(x + 3) = 0; (7, −3) and (−3, −3), N ≠ 0.
> Find the points on x squared plus y squared minus four x plus six y equals twelve where the tangent is horizontal, and where it's vertical. [pause]
> Differentiate: two x plus two y y prime minus four plus six y prime equals zero. [pause]
> Collect the y prime terms: y prime times two y plus six equals four minus two x. Divide, and simplify: y prime is two minus x, over y plus three. So N is two minus x, and D is y plus three.
> Horizontal where N is zero: x equals two. Put that in the curve: four plus y squared minus eight plus six y equals twelve, so y squared plus six y minus sixteen is zero. [pause]
> That factors as y plus eight times y minus two, so y is two or negative eight. D isn't zero at either. The points are two, two and two, negative eight.
> Vertical where D is zero: y equals negative three. Put that in the curve: x squared plus nine minus four x minus eighteen equals twelve, so x squared minus four x minus twenty-one is zero. [pause]
> That factors as x minus seven times x plus three. N isn't zero there, so the vertical tangents are at seven, negative three and negative three, negative three. [long pause]

## Example 2: A cubic curve
**On screen:** "Find the points on y² = x³ − 3x + 3 where the tangent is horizontal." Board: 2yy′ = 3x² − 3; y′ = (3x² − 3)/(2y): N = 3x² − 3, D = 2y; 3x² − 3 = 0, x² = 1, x = ±1; x = 1: y² = 1 − 3 + 3 = 1, (1, 1), (1, −1); x = −1: y² = −1 + 3 + 3 = 5, (−1, √5), (−1, −√5); D ≠ 0 at all four.
> Find the points on y squared equals x cubed minus three x plus three where the tangent is horizontal. [pause]
> Differentiate: two y y prime equals three x squared minus three. So y prime is three x squared minus three, over two y. [pause]
> N is zero when three x squared equals three: x squared is one, so x is one or negative one.
> At x equals one, y squared is one minus three plus three, one. The points one, one and one, negative one. [pause]
> At x equals negative one, y squared is negative one plus three plus three, five. The points negative one, root five and negative one, negative root five. D isn't zero at any of them. [long pause]

## Example 3: Which way it bends
**On screen:** "On y² = x³ − 3x + 3, find d²y/dx² at (1, 1). What does the curve have there?" Board: differentiate 2yy′ = 3x² − 3 again; 2y′ · y′ + 2y · y″ = 6x, so 2(y′)² + 2yy″ = 6x; at (1, 1), y′ = 0: 0 + 2y″ = 6, y″ = 3 > 0; horizontal tangent and concave up: a relative minimum.
> On the same curve, find the second derivative at one, one. What does the curve have there? [pause]
> Differentiate two y y prime equals three x squared minus three again. The left side needs the product rule: two y prime times y prime, plus two y times y double prime. That equals six x. [pause]
> At one, one, y prime is zero, so the first term drops out. Two y double prime equals six, and y double prime is three.
> A horizontal tangent where the curve is concave up: a relative minimum. [long pause]

## Example 4: Two tangent lines at one x
**On screen:** "Find the equations of all lines tangent to x² + xy + y² = 7 at x = 1." On the right, the tilted ellipse x² + xy + y² = 7, the dashed line x = 1 crossing it at two points, and a tangent line drawn at each as it is found. Board: 1 + y + y² = 7; y² + y − 6 = 0, (y + 3)(y − 2) = 0: points (1, 2) and (1, −3); 2x + y + xy′ + 2yy′ = 0; y′(x + 2y) = −(2x + y), y′ = −(2x + y)/(x + 2y); at (1, 2): y′ = −4/5; at (1, −3): y′ = −(2 − 3)/(1 − 6) = −1/5; y − 2 = −(4/5)(x − 1); y + 3 = −(1/5)(x − 1).
> Find the equations of all lines tangent to x squared plus x y plus y squared equals seven at x equals one. [try it]
> First, find the points. Put x equals one into the curve: one plus y plus y squared equals seven. [pause]
> So y squared plus y minus six is zero, which factors as y plus three times y minus two. There are two points, one, two and one, negative three, so there are two tangent lines. [pause]
> Differentiate implicitly. The x y term needs the product rule: two x plus y plus x y prime plus two y y prime equals zero.
> Collect the y prime terms: y prime times x plus two y equals negative two x plus y. So y prime is negative two x plus y, over x plus two y. [pause]
> At one, two: negative two plus two, over one plus four. Negative four fifths.
> At one, negative three: negative two minus three, over one minus six. That's negative one fifth. [pause]
> The tangent lines are y minus two equals negative four fifths times x minus one, and y plus three equals negative one fifth times x minus one. [long pause]
