# 5.3 Determining Intervals on Which a Function Is Increasing or Decreasing

## Uphill and downhill
**On screen:** The graph of a wavy function. A small tangent line rides along the curve, colored green where it slopes up and red where it slopes down. Under the graph, the same stretches are marked "increasing" and "decreasing."
> Ride a tangent line along this curve. [pause]
> Where the graph goes uphill, the tangent slopes up: f prime is positive. Where it goes downhill, f prime is negative. [pause]
> So the sign of the derivative tells you the direction of the function. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 5.3 · Determining intervals on which a function is increasing or decreasing." Unit 5 · Analytical Applications of Differentiation.

## The rule
**On screen:** Two lines: "f′(x) > 0 on an interval ⇒ f is increasing there." "f′(x) < 0 on an interval ⇒ f is decreasing there."
> If f prime is positive on an interval, f is increasing there. [pause]
> If f prime is negative on an interval, f is decreasing there. It's the sign that matters, not the size. [long pause]

## A sign chart
**On screen:** f′(x) = 3x² − 12 = 3(x − 2)(x + 2). A number line with −2 and 2 marked. Test values −3, 0 and 3 are plugged in, and the signs +, −, + appear above the pieces, with "inc", "dec", "inc" beneath.
> Here's how to find the intervals without a graph. Take f prime of x equals three x squared minus twelve. [pause]
> It's zero at negative two and two. Those critical points cut the number line into three pieces. [pause]
> In between, f prime can't change sign, so one test value per piece is enough. Try negative three: positive. Zero: negative. Three: positive. [pause]
> So f is increasing, then decreasing, then increasing again. [long pause]

## Increasing from a sign chart
**On screen:** f′ factored, the test values in each piece with their signs, and the increasing intervals.
> Where is x cubed minus three x squared plus four increasing? [try it]
> f prime is three x squared minus six x, which factors as three x times x minus two. Critical points: zero and two.
> Test one value in each piece: f prime of negative one is nine, f prime of one is negative three, f prime of three is nine. [pause]
> So f is increasing on negative infinity to zero, and on two to infinity, because f prime is positive there. [long pause]

## Justify with the derivative
**On screen:** A model sentence: "f is decreasing on (−2, 2) because f′(x) < 0 there."
> On the AP exam, give the reason in terms of f prime. Say f is decreasing on negative two to two because f prime is negative there. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The sign chart again, with "the sign of f′ is the direction of f" beneath it.
> The sign of f prime is the direction of f. Find the critical points, test one value per piece, and read the signs. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A cubic
**On screen:** "On what intervals is f(x) = x³ − 12x + 1 increasing? Decreasing?" Work: f′(x) = 3x² − 12 = 3(x − 2)(x + 2); critical points x = −2, 2; signs +, −, +; increasing on (−∞, −2) and (2, ∞); decreasing on (−2, 2).
> On what intervals is f of x equals x cubed minus twelve x plus one increasing? Decreasing? [pause]
> f prime is three x squared minus twelve, which factors as three times x minus two times x plus two.
> The critical points are negative two and two. [pause]
> Testing a value in each piece gives plus, minus, plus.
> So f is increasing on negative infinity to negative two and on two to infinity, and decreasing on negative two to two.

## Example 2: An exponential
**On screen:** "On what intervals is f(x) = xe^(−x) increasing? Decreasing?" Work: f′(x) = e^(−x) − xe^(−x) = (1 − x)e^(−x); e^(−x) > 0, so the sign is the sign of 1 − x; increasing on (−∞, 1), decreasing on (1, ∞).
> On what intervals is x times e to the negative x increasing? Decreasing? [pause]
> The product rule: e to the negative x, minus x e to the negative x. Factor: one minus x, times e to the negative x.
> e to the negative x is always positive, so the sign of f prime is the sign of one minus x. [pause]
> f is increasing for x less than one and decreasing for x greater than one.

## Example 3: A repeated factor
**On screen:** "f′(x) = (x + 1)(x − 3)². On what intervals is f increasing?" Work: critical points x = −1, 3; signs −, +, +; increasing on (−1, 3) and (3, ∞); f′ does not change sign at x = 3.
> f prime of x is x plus one, times x minus three squared. On what intervals is f increasing? [pause]
> The critical points are negative one and three.
> Test each piece: negative, positive, positive. The squared factor never makes f prime negative, so the sign doesn't change at three. [pause]
> f is increasing on negative one to three, and on three to infinity. [long pause]
