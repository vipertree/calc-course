# 5.4 Using the First Derivative Test to Determine Relative (Local) Extrema

## Three flat spots
**On screen:** Three small graphs side by side, each with a horizontal tangent at its middle point: a hilltop, a valley, and the graph of x³ at 0. Above each, the sign of f′ on the left and right: "+ then −", "− then +", "+ then +".
> All three of these graphs have a horizontal tangent in the middle. Each middle point is a critical point. [pause]
> The first is a peak: the graph goes uphill, then downhill. The second is a valley: downhill, then uphill. [pause]
> The third goes uphill, flattens for an instant, then keeps going uphill. No peak, no valley. [pause]
> What tells them apart is what f prime does on either side. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 5.4 · Using the First Derivative Test to determine relative (local) extrema." Unit 5 · Analytical Applications of Differentiation.

## The test
**On screen:** The First Derivative Test as three lines, each with a tiny sign chart: + to − gives a relative maximum; − to + gives a relative minimum; no change gives neither.
> Here's the First Derivative Test. At a critical point, look at the sign of f prime on each side. [pause]
> If f prime changes from positive to negative, f has a relative maximum. [pause]
> If f prime changes from negative to positive, f has a relative minimum. [pause]
> If f prime doesn't change sign, f has neither. [long pause]

## Write the reason
**On screen:** A model justification: "f has a relative maximum at x = 2 because f′ changes from positive to negative at x = 2." Below it, two crossed-out weak reasons: "because f′(2) = 0" and "because the graph goes up then down."
> On the AP exam, the reason is worth as much as the answer. [pause]
> Say: f has a relative maximum at x equals two because f prime changes from positive to negative there. [pause]
> "Because f prime of two is zero" isn't enough: that only makes two a critical point. And talking about the graph of f doesn't count either. Name f prime and its sign change. [long pause]

## From a sign chart
**On screen:** The problem on top. Board: x = 0 or x − 4 = 0; x = 0 or x = 4; test values −1, 1, 5; f′(−1) = (−1)(25) = −25 < 0; f′(1) = (1)(9) = 9 > 0; f′(5) = (5)(1) = 5 > 0; x = 0: − to +, relative minimum; x = 4: no sign change, no extremum. On the right, a number line with 0 and 4; each sign appears as it is tested, "dec, inc, inc" beneath, then "min" over 0 and "neither" over 4.
> f prime of x is x times x minus four squared. Find and classify the critical points of f. [pause]
> f prime is zero when x is zero or x minus four is zero: at x equals zero and x equals four. Those are the critical points. [pause]
> Mark them on a number line, and test one value in each piece: negative one, one, and five.
> f prime of negative one: negative one times twenty-five. Negative.
> f prime of one: one times nine. Positive.
> f prime of five: five times one. Positive. [pause]
> At zero, f prime changes from negative to positive: a relative minimum.
> At four, f prime is positive on both sides. No sign change, so no extremum. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** Three mini sign charts: "+ −" max, "− +" min, "+ +" neither.
> Plus to minus is a max. Minus to plus is a min. No change is neither. And always say it in terms of f prime. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A flat spot that isn't an extremum
**On screen:** "Find and classify the critical points of f(x) = x⁴ − 4x³." Board: f′(x) = 4x³ − 12x²; = 4x²(x − 3); x = 0 or x = 3; f′(−1) = 4(1)(−4) = −16 < 0; f′(1) = 4(1)(−2) = −8 < 0; f′(4) = 4(16)(1) = 64 > 0; x = 0: no sign change, neither; x = 3: − to +, relative minimum. On the right, a number line with 0 and 3; signs −, −, + appear as tested, "dec, dec, inc" beneath, then "neither" over 0 and "min" over 3.
> Find and classify the critical points of x to the fourth minus four x cubed. [pause]
> f prime is four x cubed minus twelve x squared. [pause]
> Factor out four x squared: four x squared, times x minus three.
> Four x squared is zero at zero, and x minus three is zero at three. The critical points are zero and three. [pause]
> Test negative one: four, times one, times negative four. Negative sixteen. Negative.
> Test one: four, times one, times negative two. Negative eight. Negative.
> Test four: four, times sixteen, times one. Sixty-four. Positive. [pause]
> At zero, f prime is negative on both sides. No sign change, so no extremum. [pause]
> At three, f prime changes from negative to positive: a relative minimum. [long pause]

## Example 2: A trig function
**On screen:** "Find the relative extrema of f(x) = x + 2sin x on (0, 2π)." Board: f′(x) = 1 + 2cos x; 1 + 2cos x = 0; cos x = −1/2; x = 2π/3 or x = 4π/3; f′(π/2) = 1 + 2(0) = 1 > 0; f′(π) = 1 + 2(−1) = −1 < 0; f′(3π/2) = 1 + 2(0) = 1 > 0; relative max at 2π/3 (+ to −); relative min at 4π/3 (− to +). On the right, a number line with 2π/3 and 4π/3; signs +, −, + appear as tested, "inc, dec, inc" beneath, then "max" and "min" over the points.
> Find the relative extrema of x plus two sine x, between zero and two pi. [pause]
> f prime is one plus two cosine x. [pause]
> Set it equal to zero: two cosine x equals negative one, so cosine x equals negative one half.
> Between zero and two pi, that happens at two pi over three and four pi over three. [pause]
> Test pi over two: cosine is zero, so f prime is one. Positive.
> Test pi: cosine is negative one, so f prime is one minus two. Negative one. Negative.
> Test three pi over two: cosine is zero again, so f prime is one. Positive. [pause]
> At two pi over three, f prime goes from positive to negative: a relative maximum.
> At four pi over three, it goes from negative to positive: a relative minimum. [long pause]

## Example 3: Given the derivative
**On screen:** "f′(x) = (x − 1)(x + 2)e^x. Find the relative extrema of f and justify." Board: e^x > 0, so f′ has the sign of (x − 1)(x + 2); x = 1 or x = −2; f′(−3) = (−4)(−1)e^(−3) > 0; f′(0) = (−1)(2)(1) = −2 < 0; f′(2) = (1)(4)e² > 0; relative max at x = −2: f′ changes from + to −; relative min at x = 1: f′ changes from − to +. On the right, a number line with −2 and 1; signs +, −, + appear as tested, "inc, dec, inc" beneath, then "max" and "min" over the points.
> f prime of x is x minus one, times x plus two, times e to the x. Find the relative extrema of f, and justify. [pause]
> e to the x is always positive, so it never changes the sign. The sign of f prime is the sign of x minus one, times x plus two. [pause]
> That's zero at x equals one and x equals negative two. [pause]
> Test negative three: negative four times negative one is positive, and e to the negative three is positive. Positive.
> Test zero: negative one times two, times one. Negative.
> Test two: one times four, times e squared. Positive. [pause]
> f has a relative maximum at x equals negative two, because f prime changes from positive to negative there.
> And a relative minimum at x equals one, because f prime changes from negative to positive. [long pause]
