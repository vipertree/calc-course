# 5.5 Using the Candidates Test to Determine Absolute (Global) Extrema

## A short list
**On screen:** A curve on a closed interval [a, b]. Dots appear at the two endpoints and at the two critical points inside. Each dot gets a height label; the highest and lowest are circled.
> On a closed interval, a continuous function has a highest value and a lowest value. Where can they be? [pause]
> Inside the interval, a highest or lowest point has to be a critical point. Otherwise it could go a little higher or lower. [pause]
> Or it can sit at an endpoint, where the interval just stops. [pause]
> So there's a short list of candidates: the critical points and the endpoints. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 5.5 · Using the Candidates Test to determine absolute (global) extrema." Unit 5 · Analytical Applications of Differentiation.

## The test
**On screen:** Three steps: 1. Find the critical points in (a, b). 2. Evaluate f at each one and at both endpoints. 3. The largest is the absolute max; the smallest is the absolute min.
> Here's the Candidates Test. Find the critical points inside the interval. [pause]
> Evaluate f at each of them, and at both endpoints. [pause]
> The biggest value is the absolute maximum. The smallest is the absolute minimum. [long pause]

## Don't skip the endpoints
**On screen:** The graph of y = x³ − 3x on [0, 3]. The only critical point, x = 1, gives −2. The endpoint x = 3 gives 18, far higher.
> The endpoints matter. On zero to three, x cubed minus three x has one critical point, at x equals one. [pause]
> f of one is negative two. That's the minimum. But the maximum isn't at a critical point at all. [pause]
> It's at the endpoint: f of three is eighteen. [long pause]

## A parabola on an interval
**On screen:** The critical point, the table of three candidates, and the absolute max and min.
> Find the absolute extrema of x squared minus four x on zero to five. [try it]
> f prime is two x minus four, zero at two.
> The candidates: f of zero is zero, f of two is negative four, f of five is five. [pause]
> The absolute maximum is five, at the endpoint five. The absolute minimum is negative four, at two. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** A table with columns "x" and "f(x)": endpoints and critical points in the rows, the largest and smallest values highlighted.
> Make the table, fill it in, and pick the biggest and smallest. On the AP exam, the table is your justification. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A cubic
**On screen:** "Find the absolute extrema of f(x) = x³ − 3x + 1 on [0, 3]." Work: f′(x) = 3x² − 3 = 0 at x = 1 (−1 is not in the interval); f(0) = 1, f(1) = −1, f(3) = 19; absolute max 19 at x = 3; absolute min −1 at x = 1.
> Find the absolute extrema of x cubed minus three x plus one on zero to three. [pause]
> f prime is three x squared minus three, which is zero at x equals one and negative one. Only one is in the interval.
> The candidates: f of zero is one. f of one is negative one. f of three is nineteen. [pause]
> The absolute maximum is nineteen, at x equals three. The absolute minimum is negative one, at x equals one.

## Example 2: A logarithm
**On screen:** "Find the absolute extrema of f(x) = x − 2ln x on [1, e²]." Work: f′(x) = 1 − 2/x = 0 at x = 2; f(1) = 1, f(2) = 2 − 2ln 2 ≈ 0.614, f(e²) = e² − 4 ≈ 3.389; absolute max e² − 4 at x = e²; absolute min 2 − 2ln 2 at x = 2.
> Find the absolute extrema of x minus two natural log x, on one to e squared. [pause]
> f prime is one minus two over x, which is zero at x equals two.
> The candidates: f of one is one. f of two is two minus two ln two, about point six one. [pause]
> And f of e squared is e squared minus two times two: e squared minus four, about three point three nine.
> So the absolute maximum is e squared minus four, at the right endpoint, and the absolute minimum is two minus two ln two, at x equals two.

## Example 3: A trig function
**On screen:** "Find the absolute extrema of f(x) = sin x + cos x on [0, π]." Work: f′(x) = cos x − sin x = 0 at x = π/4; f(0) = 1, f(π/4) = √2, f(π) = −1; absolute max √2 at x = π/4; absolute min −1 at x = π.
> Find the absolute extrema of sine x plus cosine x, on zero to pi. [pause]
> f prime is cosine x minus sine x. That's zero where they're equal: at pi over four.
> The candidates: f of zero is one. f of pi over four is root two over two plus root two over two: root two. f of pi is negative one. [pause]
> The absolute maximum is root two, at pi over four. The absolute minimum is negative one, at pi. [long pause]
