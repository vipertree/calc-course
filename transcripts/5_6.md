# 5.6 Determining Concavity of Functions over Their Domains

## Cups and caps
**On screen:** Two arcs side by side: one bending up like a cup, one bending down like a cap. On each, a tangent line slides from left to right while a readout shows its slope: rising on the cup, falling on the cap.
> Two curves, both smooth. One bends upward like a cup. The other bends downward like a cap. [pause]
> Slide a tangent line across the cup, and watch its slope: it keeps increasing. [pause]
> Across the cap, the slope keeps decreasing. [pause]
> That's the derivative telling us which way the graph bends. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 5.6 · Determining concavity of functions over their domains." Unit 5 · Analytical Applications of Differentiation.

## Concavity and the second derivative
**On screen:** "Concave up: f′ increasing, f″ > 0. The graph lies above its tangent lines." "Concave down: f′ decreasing, f″ < 0. The graph lies below its tangent lines."
> When the slopes increase, the graph is concave up. Slopes increasing means f prime is increasing, so f double prime is positive. [pause]
> When the slopes decrease, the graph is concave down, and f double prime is negative. [pause]
> Remember the tangent lines from linearization: a concave up graph sits above its tangent lines, and a concave down graph sits below them. [long pause]

## Points of inflection
**On screen:** The graph of y = x³ − 3x² + 1 with its cap part and cup part colored differently, meeting at the point (1, −1), which is marked "point of inflection." Below, the sign chart of f″(x) = 6x − 6: −, then +.
> Where a graph switches from one kind of bend to the other, it has a point of inflection. [pause]
> Here, f double prime is six x minus six. It's negative before one and positive after. So the concavity changes at x equals one. [pause]
> The test is a sign change of f double prime, not just f double prime equal to zero. x to the fourth has f double prime zero at the origin, but it's a cup on both sides. [long pause]

## Concavity of a cubic
**On screen:** f″, its sign on each side of −1, the concavity intervals, and the inflection point.
> Where is x cubed plus three x squared concave up? Find any points of inflection. [try it]
> f prime is three x squared plus six x, and f double prime is six x plus six.
> f double prime is positive for x greater than negative one: concave up there. [pause]
> And negative for x less than negative one: concave down.
> It changes sign at negative one. f of negative one is negative one plus three, two. The inflection point is negative one, two. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** A cup labeled "f″ > 0," a cap labeled "f″ < 0," and between them a point labeled "f″ changes sign: inflection."
> The sign of f double prime tells which way the graph bends. Where it changes sign, there's a point of inflection. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A cubic
**On screen:** "Where is f(x) = x³ − 6x² + 5 concave up? Concave down? Find any points of inflection." Work: f′(x) = 3x² − 12x; f″(x) = 6x − 12; f″ < 0 for x < 2: concave down on (−∞, 2); f″ > 0 for x > 2: concave up on (2, ∞); inflection point at x = 2: (2, −11).
> Where is x cubed minus six x squared plus five concave up? Concave down? Find any points of inflection. [pause]
> f prime is three x squared minus twelve x, and f double prime is six x minus twelve.
> That's negative for x less than two: concave down on negative infinity to two. [pause]
> And positive for x greater than two: concave up on two to infinity.
> The concavity changes at x equals two. f of two is eight minus twenty-four plus five, negative eleven. The point of inflection is two, negative eleven.

## Example 2: Two candidates
**On screen:** "Find the points of inflection of f(x) = x⁴ − 4x³." Work: f″(x) = 12x² − 24x = 12x(x − 2); signs +, −, +; inflection points at x = 0 and x = 2: (0, 0) and (2, −16).
> Find the points of inflection of x to the fourth minus four x cubed. [pause]
> f double prime is twelve x squared minus twenty-four x, which factors as twelve x times x minus two.
> The signs: positive, negative, positive. [pause]
> f double prime changes sign at both zero and two. The points of inflection are zero, zero and two, negative sixteen.

## Example 3: An exponential
**On screen:** "Where is f(x) = xe^x concave down?" Work: f′(x) = (x + 1)e^x; f″(x) = (x + 2)e^x; e^x > 0, so f″ < 0 when x < −2; concave down on (−∞, −2).
> Where is x times e to the x concave down? [pause]
> The product rule twice: f prime is x plus one times e to the x, and f double prime is x plus two times e to the x.
> e to the x is always positive, so f double prime is negative exactly when x plus two is negative. [pause]
> The graph is concave down on negative infinity to negative two. [long pause]
