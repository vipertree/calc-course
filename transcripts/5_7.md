# 5.7 Using the Second Derivative Test to Determine Extrema

## Flat on top, flat on the bottom
**On screen:** Two graphs, each with a horizontal tangent at a marked point. The first is a cap (concave down) with its flat spot at the top; the second is a cup (concave up) with its flat spot at the bottom.
> Both of these graphs have a horizontal tangent at the marked point. [pause]
> The first one bends downward, like a cap. A cap with a flat spot has to be flat at the top: a maximum. [pause]
> The second bends upward, like a cup. A cup with a flat spot is flat at the bottom: a minimum. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 5.7 · Using the Second Derivative Test to determine extrema." Unit 5 · Analytical Applications of Differentiation.

## The test
**On screen:** "Suppose f′(c) = 0." Then three lines: "f″(c) < 0: relative maximum." "f″(c) > 0: relative minimum." "f″(c) = 0: no conclusion."
> Here's the Second Derivative Test. Start with a critical point where f prime of c is zero. [pause]
> If f double prime of c is negative, the graph is concave down there: a relative maximum. [pause]
> If f double prime of c is positive, concave up: a relative minimum. [pause]
> And if f double prime of c is zero, the test says nothing. [long pause]

## Using the test
**On screen:** The critical points, f″, and the sign of f″ at each point with its classification.
> Classify the critical points of two x cubed minus six x. [try it]
> f prime is six x squared minus six, zero at one and negative one.
> f double prime is twelve x. [pause]
> At negative one it's negative twelve: concave down, a relative maximum. At one it's twelve: concave up, a relative minimum. [long pause]

## When the test is silent
**On screen:** Three small graphs: y = x⁴ (a minimum), y = −x⁴ (a maximum), y = x³ (neither). All three have f′(0) = 0 and f″(0) = 0.
> Zero tells you nothing. These three functions all have f prime and f double prime equal to zero at the origin. [pause]
> The first has a minimum there, the second a maximum, and the third neither. [pause]
> When the second derivative test is silent, go back to the first derivative test. [long pause]

## One critical point
**On screen:** A curve on (0, ∞) with a single valley. A dot at the bottom is labeled "only critical point: relative min, so absolute min."
> One more useful fact. Suppose a continuous function has only one critical point on an interval, and it's a relative minimum. [pause]
> Then it's the absolute minimum on that interval. To come back down below it, the graph would need another critical point. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "f′(c) = 0 and f″(c) < 0: max. f′(c) = 0 and f″(c) > 0: min. f″(c) = 0: use the first derivative test."
> At a horizontal tangent, concave down is a max and concave up is a min. When f double prime is zero, use the first derivative test. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A cubic
**On screen:** "Use the Second Derivative Test to classify the critical points of f(x) = x³ − 3x² + 4." Work: f′(x) = 3x² − 6x = 3x(x − 2); critical points 0, 2; f″(x) = 6x − 6; f″(0) = −6 < 0: relative max; f″(2) = 6 > 0: relative min.
> Use the Second Derivative Test to classify the critical points of x cubed minus three x squared plus four. [pause]
> f prime is three x squared minus six x, which is three x times x minus two. The critical points are zero and two.
> f double prime is six x minus six. [pause]
> At zero, that's negative six: concave down, so a relative maximum.
> At two, it's positive six: concave up, so a relative minimum.

## Example 2: When the test fails
**On screen:** "Classify the critical point of f(x) = x⁴." Work: f′(x) = 4x³ = 0 at x = 0; f″(x) = 12x², f″(0) = 0: inconclusive; f′ < 0 for x < 0 and f′ > 0 for x > 0: relative min by the First Derivative Test.
> Classify the critical point of x to the fourth. [pause]
> f prime is four x cubed, which is zero at x equals zero.
> f double prime is twelve x squared, which is zero at zero. The second derivative test is inconclusive. [pause]
> So use the first derivative test: f prime is negative before zero and positive after. A relative minimum.

## Example 3: The only critical point
**On screen:** "Find the absolute minimum value of f(x) = x + 4/x on (0, ∞). Justify." Work: f′(x) = 1 − 4/x² = 0 at x = 2; f″(x) = 8/x³, f″(2) = 1 > 0: relative min; it is the only critical point on (0, ∞), so it is the absolute min; f(2) = 4.
> Find the absolute minimum value of x plus four over x, for positive x. Justify your answer. [pause]
> f prime is one minus four over x squared, which is zero at x equals two.
> f double prime is eight over x cubed. At two, that's one: positive, so a relative minimum. [pause]
> It's the only critical point on the interval, so it's the absolute minimum.
> f of two is two plus two: four. [long pause]
