# 5.8 Sketching Graphs of Functions and Their Derivatives

## Two graphs, one story
**On screen:** Two stacked graphs with the same x-axis: f on top, a wavy curve with a peak and a valley; f′ underneath, starting empty. A tangent line rides along f, and as it moves, a pencil draws f′ below at the height of the tangent's slope.
> On top is a function f. Below it, we'll draw its derivative, one slope at a time. [pause]
> Where f climbs, the slopes are positive, so the lower graph sits above the axis. Where f falls, it sits below. [pause]
> At the peak and the valley of f, the tangent is flat, so f prime crosses zero. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 5.8 · Sketching graphs of functions and their derivatives." Unit 5 · Analytical Applications of Differentiation.

## Reading the graph of f prime
**On screen:** The two stacked graphs again. Dashed vertical lines connect matching features: f′ crossing zero ↔ f's peak and valley; f′'s own valley ↔ f's inflection point. Labels appear: "f′ > 0: f rises," "f′ crosses zero: f turns," "f′ rising: f concave up," "valley of f′: inflection of f."
> Every feature of f shows up in f prime, one level down. [pause]
> f prime above the axis: f rises. Below: f falls. Crossing the axis: f turns around. [pause]
> Where f prime is rising, f is concave up. Where it's falling, f is concave down. [pause]
> And where f prime has its own peak or valley, f changes concavity: a point of inflection. [long pause]

## Reading this graph
**On screen:** The graph of f′ with its three zeros marked; the crossing at x = 1 labeled 'max', the crossings at −2 and 3 labeled 'min'.
> Here's the graph of f prime, not f. Where does f have a relative maximum? A relative minimum? [try it]
> Look where f prime crosses the axis. At one, it goes from above to below: positive to negative. A relative maximum of f. [pause]
> At negative two and at three, it goes from below to above: relative minima. [long pause]

## The trap
**On screen:** A single graph labeled "y = f′(x)" with its peak circled and the wrong label "maximum of f?" crossed out, replaced by "inflection point of f."
> The most common mistake is reading a graph of f prime as if it were f. [pause]
> A peak on the graph of f prime is not a maximum of f. It's where f stops getting steeper: an inflection point. Always read the label on the axis. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** A two-column table: "graph of f′" and "f": above axis / rises; crosses zero / turns; rising / concave up; peak or valley / inflection.
> The graph of f prime is a graph of slopes. Read it one level down. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Sketching the derivative
**On screen:** "Sketch the graph of f′ for f(x) = x³ − 3x." A graph of f is shown. Work: f′(x) = 3x² − 3; f′ = 0 at x = ±1 (the peak and valley of f); f′ > 0 for |x| > 1 and f′ < 0 for |x| < 1; f′ is a parabola opening up with vertex (0, −3).
> Sketch the graph of f prime for x cubed minus three x. [pause]
> f prime is three x squared minus three.
> It's zero at negative one and one, exactly where f has its peak and its valley. [pause]
> f rises outside those points and falls between them, so f prime is positive outside and negative between.
> The sketch is a parabola opening up, with its vertex at zero, negative three: where f is falling most steeply.

## Example 2: From f prime to f
**On screen:** "The graph of f′(x) = 4 − x² is shown. Describe f: where it increases, its extrema, its concavity." Work: f′ > 0 on (−2, 2): f increasing; f′ < 0 for |x| > 2: decreasing; relative min at x = −2, relative max at x = 2; f′ rising for x < 0: concave up; falling for x > 0: concave down; inflection at x = 0.
> The graph of f prime of x equals four minus x squared is shown. Describe f: where it increases, its extrema, and its concavity. [pause]
> f prime is above the axis between negative two and two, so f is increasing there, and decreasing outside.
> f prime goes from negative to positive at negative two: a relative minimum. From positive to negative at two: a relative maximum. [pause]
> f prime rises until zero and then falls. So f is concave up for x less than zero and concave down after.
> f prime peaks at zero, so f has a point of inflection there.

## Example 3: From conditions to a sketch
**On screen:** "Sketch a continuous f with f(0) = 1, f′(x) < 0 for x < 0, f′(x) > 0 for x > 0, and f″(x) > 0 for all x." Work: f decreases then increases: relative (and absolute) min at (0, 1); concave up everywhere: a bowl shape with its lowest point at (0, 1).
> Sketch a continuous function with f of zero equal to one, f prime negative for x less than zero, positive for x greater than zero, and f double prime positive everywhere. [pause]
> f falls, then rises, with the turn at zero. So the point zero, one is the minimum.
> f double prime is positive everywhere, so the graph is a bowl the whole way. [pause]
> The sketch is a bowl with its lowest point at zero, one. [long pause]
