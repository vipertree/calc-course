# 6.10 Integrating Functions Using Long Division and Completing the Square

## Two rewrites
**On screen:** Two integrals side by side. Left: "∫ (x² + 3x + 5)/(x + 1) dx" labeled "top degree ≥ bottom degree: divide". Right: "∫ 1/(x² + 4x + 13) dx" labeled "quadratic bottom, no real roots: complete the square". Beneath both: "rewrite into a form you know".
> Some fractions don't match any integration rule as written. Two algebra moves fix most of them, and you may not have used either one in a while, so we'll take them slowly. [pause]
> When the top has degree at least as big as the bottom, divide first. [pause]
> When the bottom is a quadratic that doesn't factor, complete the square. Then an arctangent or arcsine rule takes over. [long pause]

## Division with numbers
**On screen:** The long division of 17 by 5: 5 outside, 17 inside, 3 on top, 15 under the 17, a line, 2 under it. Labels: "quotient" at the 3, "remainder" at the 2. Then "17/5 = 3 + 2/5", read as "quotient + remainder/divisor".
> Start with numbers. Seventeen divided by five: five goes into seventeen three times. Three times five is fifteen, and seventeen minus fifteen leaves two. [pause]
> So seventeen fifths is three, plus two fifths: the quotient, plus the remainder over what we divided by. [pause]
> Polynomial long division works the same way, and it gives the same kind of answer. [long pause]

## Polynomial long division
**On screen:** Left: the long division of x² + 3x + 5 by x + 1, built one move at a time, with like powers lined up in columns. Right: the four moves, each lighting up in turn: "1. Divide the leading terms." "2. Multiply." "3. Subtract." "4. Bring down the next term." Under them, the current side calculation: "x² ÷ x = x", then "x(x + 1) = x² + x", then "2x ÷ x = 2", then "2(x + 1) = 2x + 2". The division: x on top; −(x² + x) under x² + 3x; a line; 2x, with the + 5 brought down by an arrow; + 2 on top; −(2x + 2); a line; 3. Labels "quotient" at x + 2 and "remainder" at 3.
> Divide x squared plus three x plus five by x plus one. Set it up like number division: x plus one outside, x squared plus three x plus five inside, with the powers in order from highest to lowest. [pause]
> Step one: divide the leading terms. x squared divided by x is x. Write x on top, over the x term. [pause]
> Step two: multiply. x times x plus one is x squared plus x. Write it under the matching powers. [pause]
> Step three: subtract. x squared minus x squared is zero, and three x minus x is two x. [pause]
> Step four: bring down the next term, the plus five. Now we have two x plus five. [pause]
> Repeat. Divide the leading terms: two x divided by x is two. Write plus two on top. [pause]
> Multiply: two times x plus one is two x plus two. Subtract: two x minus two x is zero, and five minus two is three. [pause]
> Three has a lower degree than x plus one, so we stop. The quotient is x plus two, and the remainder is three. [long pause]

## The result
**On screen:** "17/5 = 3 + 2/5" above "(x² + 3x + 5)/(x + 1) = x + 2 + 3/(x + 1)", lined up, with "quotient" and "remainder/divisor" labels under the matching parts of both. Then "check: (x + 1)(x + 2) + 3"; "= x² + 3x + 2 + 3"; "= x² + 3x + 5".
> Just like seventeen fifths, the answer is the quotient plus the remainder over what we divided by: x plus two, plus three over x plus one. [pause]
> Check by multiplying back. x plus one times x plus two is x squared plus three x plus two. Add the remainder, three, and we're back to x squared plus three x plus five. [long pause]

## Synthetic division
**On screen:** Left, headed "Long division": the finished division from before, smaller. Right, headed "Synthetic division": "only for dividing by x − c" in a box. "x + 1 = x − (−1), so c = −1." Then the table: −1 in the corner; 1, 3, 5 across the top. The 1 drops to the bottom row; −1 appears under the 3, then 2 below the line; −2 under the 5, then 3, boxed. Under it: "1, 2 | 3: quotient x + 2, remainder 3." The quotient (x + 2 and 1, 2) is one color in both methods, the remainder (3) another.
> There's a shortcut called synthetic division. It works in one situation only: dividing by x minus a number, x minus c. [pause]
> x plus one is x minus negative one, so c is negative one. Write c in the corner, then only the coefficients of the top: one, three, five. [pause]
> Bring down the first coefficient, one. [pause]
> Multiply it by c: one times negative one is negative one. Write that under the three and add: three plus negative one is two. [pause]
> Multiply again: two times negative one is negative two. Write that under the five and add: five plus negative two is three. [pause]
> The last number is the remainder, three. The others are the quotient's coefficients, starting one degree lower than the top: one x plus two, which is just x plus two. [pause]
> That's the same answer as the long division, built from the same numbers. Synthetic division skips writing the x's. [pause]
> Remember the limit: it only works when you divide by x minus c. To divide by something such as x squared plus one, or two x plus one, use long division. [long pause]

## Integrate the pieces
**On screen:** "∫ (x² + 3x + 5)/(x + 1) dx = ∫ (x + 2 + 3/(x + 1)) dx". Then "= x²/2 + 2x + 3 ln|x + 1| + C", with a side note at the last term: "u = x + 1, du = dx".
> Back to the integral. The integral of x squared plus three x plus five over x plus one becomes the integral of x plus two, plus three over x plus one. [pause]
> Now each piece has a rule. x gives x squared over two, and two gives two x. [pause]
> For three over x plus one, let u be x plus one, so d u is d x. That gives three ln of the absolute value of x plus one. [pause]
> All together: x squared over two, plus two x, plus three ln of absolute x plus one, plus C. [long pause]

## Two more forms
**On screen:** Two boxed rules: "∫ 1/(a² + u²) du = (1/a) arctan(u/a) + C" and "∫ 1/√(a² − u²) du = arcsin(u/a) + C". Then: "Complete the square to make a² + u² or a² − u²."
> These two rules come from the inverse trig derivatives, with a constant a squared in place of one. [pause]
> The integral of one over a squared plus u squared is one over a times arctangent of u over a. [pause]
> The integral of one over the square root of a squared minus u squared is arcsine of u over a. [pause]
> To use them, complete the square in the bottom so it looks like a squared plus u squared, or a squared minus u squared. [long pause]

## Complete the square
**On screen:** The problem: "Rewrite x² + 4x + 13 by completing the square." The goal under it, in gray: "(x + __)² + __". Board: half of 4 is 2, and 2² = 4; x² + 4x + 13 = (x² + 4x + 4) + 9; = (x + 2)² + 9; check: (x + 2)² + 9 = x² + 4x + 4 + 9 = x² + 4x + 13.
> Rewrite x squared plus four x plus thirteen by completing the square. [pause]
> The goal is x plus some number, squared, plus another number. [pause]
> Look at the x coefficient, four. Take half of it, two, and square that: four. [pause]
> x squared plus four x plus four is a perfect square, so split the thirteen into four plus nine. That gives x squared plus four x plus four, plus nine. [pause]
> The first part is x plus two, squared. So the quadratic is x plus two, squared, plus nine. [pause]
> Check by expanding. x plus two, squared, is x squared plus four x plus four, and adding nine gives x squared plus four x plus thirteen. [long pause]

## An arctangent
**On screen:** The problem: "Find ∫ 1/(x² + 4x + 13) dx." The arctangent rule stays in the corner. Board: x² + 4x + 13 = (x + 2)² + 3²; u = x + 2, du = dx, a = 3; ∫ du/(u² + 3²) = ⅓ arctan(u/3) + C; = ⅓ arctan((x + 2)/3) + C.
> Find the integral of one over x squared plus four x plus thirteen, d x. [pause]
> We just completed the square: the bottom is x plus two, squared, plus nine, and nine is three squared. [pause]
> That matches u squared plus a squared, with u equal to x plus two and a equal to three. d u is just d x. [pause]
> The rule gives one over a times arctangent of u over a: one third arctangent of u over three, plus C. [pause]
> Put x back: one third arctangent of x plus two over three, plus C. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Top degree ≥ bottom degree: divide first (long division, or synthetic for x − c)." "Quadratic bottom that won't factor: complete the square." "∫ du/(a² + u²) = (1/a) arctan(u/a) + C." "∫ du/√(a² − u²) = arcsin(u/a) + C."
> If the top's degree is at least the bottom's, divide, with synthetic division as a shortcut when you divide by x minus c. If the bottom is a quadratic that won't factor, complete the square and use arctangent or arcsine. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A cubic over a line
**On screen:** "Find ∫ (x³ + 1)/(x − 1) dx." On the right, a synthetic division table: 1 in the corner; 1, 0, 0, 1 across the top; it fills in to 1, 1, 1 | 2. Board: c = 1; x³ + 0x² + 0x + 1; bottom row: 1, 1, 1 | 2; (x³ + 1)/(x − 1) = x² + x + 1 + 2/(x − 1); ∫ = x³/3 + x²/2 + x + 2ln|x − 1| + C.
> Find the integral of x cubed plus one, over x minus one, d x. [try it]
> The top's degree is bigger, so divide. We're dividing by x minus one, so synthetic division works, with c equal to one. [pause]
> Careful with the coefficients. x cubed plus one has no x squared term and no x term, so write zeros for them: one, zero, zero, one. [pause]
> Bring down the one. Times one is one, and zero plus one is one. Times one is one, and zero plus one is one. Times one is one, and one plus one is two. [pause]
> The quotient is x squared plus x plus one, and the remainder is two. So the fraction is x squared plus x plus one, plus two over x minus one. [pause]
> Integrate each piece: x cubed over three, plus x squared over two, plus x, plus two ln of absolute x minus one, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 2: An arcsine
**On screen:** "Find ∫ 1/√(−x² + 6x − 5) dx." The arcsine rule stays in the corner. Board: −x² + 6x − 5 = −(x² − 6x) − 5; = −(x² − 6x + 9) + 9 − 5; = −(x − 3)² + 4 = 4 − (x − 3)²; u = x − 3, du = dx, a = 2; ∫ du/√(2² − u²) = arcsin(u/2) + C = arcsin((x − 3)/2) + C.
> Find the integral of one over the square root of negative x squared plus six x minus five, d x. [try it]
> A square root of a quadratic in the bottom points to the arcsine form, a squared minus u squared. So complete the square. [pause]
> The x squared has a negative in front, so first factor the negative out of the x terms: negative, times x squared minus six x, then minus five. [pause]
> Inside the parentheses, half of negative six is negative three, and negative three squared is nine. Add nine inside. The negative out front makes that a subtraction of nine, so add nine outside to balance it. [pause]
> That's negative x minus three, squared, plus nine minus five. Rearranged: four minus x minus three, squared. [pause]
> So a squared is four, a is two, and u is x minus three, with d u equal to d x. [pause]
> The arcsine rule gives arcsine of u over two: arcsine of x minus three over two, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 3: A definite integral
**On screen:** "Evaluate ∫_0^1 x/(x + 1) dx." Board: x ÷ (x + 1): quotient 1, remainder x − (x + 1) = −1; x/(x + 1) = 1 − 1/(x + 1); ∫_0^1 (1 − 1/(x + 1)) dx = [x − ln|x + 1|]_0^1; = (1 − ln 2) − (0 − ln 1); = 1 − ln 2.
> Evaluate the integral from zero to one of x over x plus one, d x. [try it]
> Same degree on top and bottom, so divide. x plus one goes into x one time. One times x plus one is x plus one, and x minus that leaves negative one. [pause]
> So x over x plus one is one, plus the remainder negative one over x plus one: one minus one over x plus one. [pause]
> An antiderivative is x minus ln of absolute x plus one. [pause]
> At one: one minus ln two. At zero: zero minus ln one, which is zero. [pause]
> The integral is one minus ln two. [long pause]
