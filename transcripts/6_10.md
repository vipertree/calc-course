## Divide first
**On screen:** The long division of x² + 3x + 5 by x + 1 laid out: x(x + 1) = x² + x, subtract to get 2x + 5; 2(x + 1) = 2x + 2, subtract to get 3. Result: quotient x + 2, remainder 3. Board: (x² + 3x + 5)/(x + 1) = x + 2 + 3/(x + 1); ∫ (x + 2 + 3/(x + 1)) dx = x²/2 + 2x + 3ln|x + 1| + C.
> Find the integral of x squared plus three x plus five, over x plus one. The top has degree two, the bottom degree one, so divide. [pause]
> x plus one goes into x squared x times. x times x plus one is x squared plus x. Subtract, and two x plus five is left.
> It goes into that two times. Two times x plus one is two x plus two. Subtract: the remainder is three. [pause]
> So the fraction equals x plus two, plus three over x plus one.
> Now each piece has a rule. x gives x squared over two, two gives two x, and three over x plus one gives three ln of absolute x plus one. Plus C. [long pause]

## Two more forms
**On screen:** Two boxed rules, each derived on the board by substituting u = a·w: "∫ 1/(a² + u²) du = (1/a) arctan(u/a) + C" and "∫ 1/√(a² − u²) du = arcsin(u/a) + C". Then: "Complete the square to make a² + u² or a² − u²."
> These two rules come from the inverse trig derivatives, with a constant a squared in place of one. [pause]
> The integral of one over a squared plus u squared is one over a times arctangent of u over a. [pause]
> The integral of one over the square root of a squared minus u squared is arcsine of u over a. [pause]
> To use them, complete the square in the bottom so it looks like a squared plus u squared, or a squared minus u squared. [long pause]

## Complete the square
**On screen:** The problem: "Find ∫ 1/(x² + 4x + 13) dx." Board: x² + 4x + 13 = (x² + 4x + 4) + 9 = (x + 2)² + 9; u = x + 2, du = dx, a = 3; ∫ 1/(u² + 3²) du = ⅓ arctan(u/3); = ⅓ arctan((x + 2)/3) + C.
> Find the integral of one over x squared plus four x plus thirteen. [try it]
> The bottom doesn't factor, so complete the square. Half of four is two, and two squared is four. Thirteen is four plus nine. [pause]
> So the bottom is x plus two, squared, plus nine. That's u squared plus three squared, with u equals x plus two and a equals three.
> d u is just d x. The rule gives one third arctangent of u over three. [pause]
> Put x back: one third arctangent of x plus two over three, plus C. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Top degree ≥ bottom degree: divide first." "Quadratic bottom that won't factor: complete the square." "∫ du/(a² + u²) = (1/a) arctan(u/a) + C." "∫ du/√(a² − u²) = arcsin(u/a) + C."
> If the top's degree is at least the bottom's, divide. If the bottom is a quadratic that won't factor, complete the square and use arctangent or arcsine. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A cubic over a line
**On screen:** "Find ∫ (x³ + 1)/(x − 1) dx." Board: divide: x³ + 1 = (x − 1)(x² + x + 1) + 2; (x³ + 1)/(x − 1) = x² + x + 1 + 2/(x − 1); ∫ = x³/3 + x²/2 + x + 2ln|x − 1| + C.
> Find the integral of x cubed plus one, over x minus one. [pause]
> The top's degree is bigger, so divide. x minus one goes into x cubed plus one with quotient x squared plus x plus one, and remainder two. [pause]
> So the fraction is x squared plus x plus one, plus two over x minus one.
> Integrate each piece: x cubed over three, plus x squared over two, plus x, plus two ln of absolute x minus one, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 2: An arcsine
**On screen:** "Find ∫ 1/√(−x² + 6x − 5) dx." Board: −x² + 6x − 5 = −(x² − 6x) − 5; = −(x² − 6x + 9) + 9 − 5 = 4 − (x − 3)²; u = x − 3, a = 2; ∫ du/√(2² − u²) = arcsin(u/2); = arcsin((x − 3)/2) + C.
> Find the integral of one over the square root of negative x squared plus six x minus five. [pause]
> Complete the square. Factor the negative out of the x terms: negative, times x squared minus six x, minus five. [pause]
> Add and subtract nine inside: negative x minus three squared, plus nine, minus five. That's four minus x minus three squared.
> So u is x minus three and a is two, and the arcsine rule applies. [pause]
> The answer is arcsine of x minus three over two, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 3: A definite integral
**On screen:** "Evaluate ∫_0^1 x/(x + 1) dx." Board: x/(x + 1) = 1 − 1/(x + 1); ∫_0^1 (1 − 1/(x + 1)) dx = [x − ln|x + 1|]_0^1; = (1 − ln 2) − (0 − ln 1) = 1 − ln 2.
> Evaluate the integral from zero to one of x over x plus one. [pause]
> Same degree on top and bottom, so divide. x over x plus one is one, minus one over x plus one. [pause]
> An antiderivative is x minus ln of absolute x plus one.
> At one: one minus ln two. At zero: zero minus ln one, which is zero. [pause]
> The integral is one minus ln two. [long pause]
