# 6.11 Integrating Using Integration by Parts

## The product rule, backward
**On screen:** "(uv)′ = u′v + uv′". Integrate both sides: "uv = ∫ v du + ∫ u dv". Rearranged and boxed: "∫ u dv = uv − ∫ v du". A note: "BC only".
> Substitution undoes the chain rule. Integration by parts undoes the product rule. This topic is for BC. [pause]
> The product rule says the derivative of u times v is u prime v plus u v prime. Integrate both sides, and u v equals the integral of v d u plus the integral of u d v. [pause]
> Move one piece across: the integral of u d v equals u v minus the integral of v d u. That trade is the whole method. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 6.11 · Integrating using integration by parts." Unit 6 · Integration and Accumulation of Change.

## Choosing u
**On screen:** A table with four cells, filled for each problem: "u =", "dv =", "du =", "v =". Beside it, the word LIATE spelled vertically: Logarithmic, Inverse trig, Algebraic, Trig, Exponential, with "choose u from the top of the list" and "dv is everything else, including dx".
> Split the integrand into u, which you'll differentiate, and d v, which you'll integrate. A four-box table keeps it straight: u, d v, d u, and v. [pause]
> Choose u to be the part that gets simpler when you differentiate it. A common guide is LIATE: logs, inverse trig, algebraic, trig, exponential. Pick u from the top of the list. [pause]
> d v is everything else, including the d x. You need to be able to integrate it. [long pause]

## A first example
**On screen:** The problem: "Find ∫ x eˣ dx." The table: u = x, dv = eˣ dx, du = dx, v = eˣ. Board: ∫ x eˣ dx = x eˣ − ∫ eˣ dx; = x eˣ − eˣ + C.
> Find the integral of x e to the x. [pause]
> x is algebraic and e to the x is exponential, so u is x and d v is e to the x d x. [pause]
> Differentiate u: d u is d x. Integrate d v: v is e to the x.
> Now the formula: u v, which is x e to the x, minus the integral of v d u, which is the integral of e to the x d x. [pause]
> That last integral is just e to the x. So the answer is x e to the x minus e to the x, plus C. [long pause]

## A definite integral
**On screen:** The problem: "Evaluate ∫_1^e ln x dx." The table: u = ln x, dv = dx, du = (1/x) dx, v = x. Board: [x ln x]_1^e − ∫_1^e x · (1/x) dx; = [x ln x − x]_1^e; = (e · 1 − e) − (1 · 0 − 1); = 0 − (−1) = 1.
> Evaluate the integral from one to e of ln x. There's only one factor, so let u be ln x and d v be d x. [pause]
> Then d u is one over x d x, and v is x.
> The formula gives x ln x, minus the integral of x times one over x, which is the integral of one. So the antiderivative is x ln x minus x. [pause]
> At e: e times one, minus e, which is zero. At one: one times zero, minus one, which is negative one. [pause]
> Zero minus negative one: one. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "∫ u dv = uv − ∫ v du." "u: the part that simplifies when differentiated (LIATE)." "dv: the rest, including dx." "Use the four-box table."
> Integration by parts trades the integral of u d v for u v minus the integral of v d u. Choose u to simplify when differentiated, and keep the four boxes straight. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A trig factor
**On screen:** "Find ∫ x cos x dx." Table: u = x, dv = cos x dx, du = dx, v = sin x. Board: = x sin x − ∫ sin x dx; = x sin x − (−cos x); = x sin x + cos x + C.
> Find the integral of x cosine x. [pause]
> Algebraic comes before trig, so u is x and d v is cosine x d x. Then d u is d x and v is sine x. [pause]
> The formula: x sine x, minus the integral of sine x.
> The integral of sine x is negative cosine x, so subtracting it gives plus cosine x. [pause]
> The answer is x sine x plus cosine x plus C. [long pause]

## Example 2: Parts twice
**On screen:** "Find ∫ x² eˣ dx." First table: u = x², dv = eˣ dx, du = 2x dx, v = eˣ. Board: = x²eˣ − ∫ 2x eˣ dx. Second table: u = 2x, dv = eˣ dx, du = 2 dx, v = eˣ. Board: ∫ 2x eˣ dx = 2x eˣ − ∫ 2eˣ dx = 2x eˣ − 2eˣ; so ∫ x² eˣ dx = x²eˣ − 2x eˣ + 2eˣ + C = eˣ(x² − 2x + 2) + C.
> Find the integral of x squared e to the x. [pause]
> Let u be x squared and d v be e to the x d x. Then d u is two x d x and v is e to the x. The formula gives x squared e to the x, minus the integral of two x e to the x. [pause]
> That new integral still has a product, but the power went down. Use parts again, with u equals two x: it's two x e to the x minus two e to the x.
> Put it back, minding the minus sign: x squared e to the x, minus two x e to the x, plus two e to the x. [pause]
> Factor out e to the x: e to the x times x squared minus two x plus two, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 3: An inverse trig function
**On screen:** "Find ∫ arctan x dx." Table: u = arctan x, dv = dx, du = 1/(1 + x²) dx, v = x. Board: = x arctan x − ∫ x/(1 + x²) dx; substitution w = 1 + x², dw = 2x dx: ∫ x/(1 + x²) dx = ½ ln(1 + x²); answer: x arctan x − ½ ln(1 + x²) + C.
> Find the integral of arctangent x. [pause]
> One factor, so u is arctangent x and d v is d x. Then d u is one over one plus x squared d x, and v is x. [pause]
> The formula gives x arctangent x, minus the integral of x over one plus x squared.
> That last one is a substitution: let w be one plus x squared, so it's one half ln of one plus x squared. [pause]
> The answer: x arctangent x minus one half ln of one plus x squared, plus C. [long pause]
