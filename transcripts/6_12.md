# 6.12 Integrating Using Linear Partial Fractions

## Adding fractions, backward
**On screen:** Forward: "2/(x − 1) + 3/(x + 2) = (2(x + 2) + 3(x − 1))/((x − 1)(x + 2)) = (5x + 1)/((x − 1)(x + 2))". Then an arrow back from the right side to the left, labeled "partial fractions". A note: "BC only".
> In algebra, you added fractions like two over x minus one plus three over x plus two by finding a common denominator. You get five x plus one over the product. This topic is for BC. [pause]
> Partial fractions runs that backward: it splits one fraction into simpler pieces. [pause]
> And each simple piece, a number over a linear factor, integrates to a natural log. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 6.12 · Integrating using linear partial fractions." Unit 6 · Integration and Accumulation of Change.

## The method
**On screen:** On ∫ 1/(x² − 1) dx: factor x² − 1 = (x − 1)(x + 1). Board: 1/((x − 1)(x + 1)) = A/(x − 1) + B/(x + 1); multiply through: 1 = A(x + 1) + B(x − 1); x = 1: 1 = 2A, A = ½; x = −1: 1 = −2B, B = −½; ∫ (½/(x − 1) − ½/(x + 1)) dx = ½ln|x − 1| − ½ln|x + 1| + C.
> Take the integral of one over x squared minus one. Factor the bottom: x minus one times x plus one. [pause]
> Write the fraction as A over x minus one plus B over x plus one, with A and B unknown numbers.
> Multiply both sides by the whole denominator: one equals A times x plus one, plus B times x minus one. [pause]
> Now pick x values that make terms vanish. At x equals one, the B term drops out: one equals two A, so A is one half. At x equals negative one, the A term drops out: one equals negative two B, so B is negative one half. [pause]
> Each piece integrates to a log: one half ln of absolute x minus one, minus one half ln of absolute x plus one, plus C. [long pause]

## Using the method
**On screen:** The problem: "Find ∫ (x + 7)/(x² − x − 6) dx." Board: x² − x − 6 = (x − 3)(x + 2); (x + 7)/((x − 3)(x + 2)) = A/(x − 3) + B/(x + 2); x + 7 = A(x + 2) + B(x − 3); x = 3: 10 = 5A, A = 2; x = −2: 5 = −5B, B = −1; ∫ (2/(x − 3) − 1/(x + 2)) dx = 2ln|x − 3| − ln|x + 2| + C.
> Find the integral of x plus seven, over x squared minus x minus six. [try it]
> Factor the bottom: x minus three, times x plus two. [pause]
> Set up A over x minus three plus B over x plus two. Multiply through: x plus seven equals A times x plus two, plus B times x minus three.
> At x equals three: ten equals five A, so A is two. At x equals negative two: five equals negative five B, so B is negative one. [pause]
> Integrate the pieces: two ln of absolute x minus three, minus ln of absolute x plus two, plus C. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Factor the denominator into distinct linear factors." "Write A/(first factor) + B/(second factor)." "Multiply through; plug in each root to find A and B." "Each piece: a ln|linear|."
> Factor the bottom, split into A and B pieces, find A and B by plugging in the roots, and integrate each piece to a log. If the top's degree is too big, divide first. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Undo the addition
**On screen:** "Find ∫ (5x + 1)/((x − 1)(x + 2)) dx." Board: 5x + 1 = A(x + 2) + B(x − 1); x = 1: 6 = 3A, A = 2; x = −2: −9 = −3B, B = 3; ∫ (2/(x − 1) + 3/(x + 2)) dx = 2ln|x − 1| + 3ln|x + 2| + C.
> Find the integral of five x plus one, over x minus one times x plus two. [pause]
> Multiply through: five x plus one equals A times x plus two, plus B times x minus one. [pause]
> At x equals one: six equals three A, so A is two. At x equals negative two: negative nine equals negative three B, so B is three.
> These are the fractions from the opening. Integrate: two ln of absolute x minus one, plus three ln of absolute x plus two, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 2: A definite integral
**On screen:** "Evaluate ∫_2^3 1/(x(x − 1)) dx." Board: 1 = A(x − 1) + Bx; x = 0: A = −1; x = 1: B = 1; 1/(x(x − 1)) = −1/x + 1/(x − 1); [ln|x − 1| − ln|x|]_2^3; = (ln 2 − ln 3) − (ln 1 − ln 2) = 2ln 2 − ln 3 = ln(4/3).
> Evaluate the integral from two to three of one over x times x minus one. [pause]
> Write it as A over x plus B over x minus one, and multiply through: one equals A times x minus one, plus B times x. [pause]
> At x equals zero: one equals negative A, so A is negative one. At x equals one: B is one.
> An antiderivative is ln of absolute x minus one, minus ln of absolute x. [pause]
> At three: ln two minus ln three. At two: ln one minus ln two, which is negative ln two. Subtract: two ln two minus ln three, which is ln of four thirds. [long pause]
