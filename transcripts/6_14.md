# 6.14 Selecting Techniques for Antidifferentiation

## A toolkit
**On screen:** A toolkit tray with labeled tools laid out: "basic rules", "rewrite first", "substitution", "long division", "complete the square". Above the tray, a scattered pile of integrals.
> By now you have a whole toolkit for antiderivatives. On a test, the hard part is choosing which tool to use. [pause]
> There's no fixed list of steps to march through. Look at the integral, notice its features, and pick the tool those features call for. [pause]
> If the first tool doesn't work, put it back and try another. That's normal. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "6.14 AB · Selecting techniques for antidifferentiation." Unit 6 · Integration and Accumulation of Change.

## What to look for
**On screen:** A two-column chart, built row by row: "a basic form (power, trig, eˣ, 1/x) | basic rules"; "a product, a power of a sum, or a sum over one term | rewrite first"; "an inside function whose derivative is a factor | substitution"; "a fraction, top degree ≥ bottom degree | long division"; "1 over a quadratic that won't factor | complete the square: arctan or arcsin".
> Start with the features. If it's already a basic form, use the basic rules. [pause]
> If it's a product you can multiply out, a power of a sum, or a sum over a single term, rewrite it first. [pause]
> If there's an inside function and its derivative is a factor, up to a constant, use substitution. [pause]
> If it's a fraction whose top has degree at least the bottom's, divide. [pause]
> If it's one over a quadratic that won't factor, complete the square. That leads to an arctangent, or with a square root, an arcsine. [long pause]

## Choosing the tool
**On screen:** The problem: "Choose a technique for each: (a) ∫ x cos(x²) dx (b) ∫ (x² + 1)² dx (c) ∫ 1/(x² + 6x + 10) dx (d) ∫ sin²x cos x dx." Board: (a) inside x², derivative 2x is a factor: substitution, ½ sin(x²) + C; (b) a power of a sum: expand; (c) quadratic bottom: (x + 3)² + 1, arctan(x + 3) + C; (d) inside sin x, derivative cos x is a factor: substitution, ⅓ sin³x + C.
> For each of these four integrals, which tool? [pause]
> The first has x squared inside the cosine, and its derivative, two x, is a factor up to a constant. Substitution, giving one half sine of x squared. [pause]
> The second is a power of a sum. Expand it, then use the power rule.
> The third is one over a quadratic. Complete the square: x plus three, squared, plus one. That's arctangent of x plus three. [pause]
> The fourth has sine x inside the square, and cosine x, its derivative, is a factor. Substitution again: one third sine cubed x. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The toolkit tray again, with "Notice the features. Pick the tool. Try another if it fails." beneath.
> Look at the features of the integral, pick the tool they call for, and don't be afraid to try another. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A substitution in disguise
**On screen:** "Find ∫ (x + 1)/(x² + 2x + 5) dx." Board: the derivative of x² + 2x + 5 is 2x + 2 = 2(x + 1): a factor; u = x² + 2x + 5, du = 2(x + 1) dx, (x + 1) dx = ½ du; ∫ ½ (1/u) du = ½ ln|u|; = ½ ln(x² + 2x + 5) + C.
> Find the integral of x plus one, over x squared plus two x plus five. [pause]
> Notice the derivative of the bottom: two x plus two, which is two times the top. So it's substitution. [pause]
> Let u be the bottom. Then d u is two times x plus one d x, so x plus one d x is one half d u.
> The integral becomes one half the integral of one over u: one half ln of absolute u. [pause]
> The bottom is always positive, so the answer is one half ln of x squared plus two x plus five, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 2: Same bottom, different top
**On screen:** "Find ∫ 1/(x² + 2x + 5) dx." Board: no x on top, so no substitution; x² + 2x + 5 = (x + 1)² + 4; u = x + 1, a = 2; ∫ du/(u² + 2²) = ½ arctan(u/2); = ½ arctan((x + 1)/2) + C.
> Now the same bottom with just a one on top: the integral of one over x squared plus two x plus five. [pause]
> The derivative of the bottom isn't a factor anymore, so substitution won't work. It's one over a quadratic that doesn't factor: complete the square. [pause]
> x squared plus two x plus one, plus four: x plus one squared, plus four. So u is x plus one and a is two.
> The arctangent rule gives one half arctangent of x plus one over two, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 3: Flip the fraction
**On screen:** "Find ∫ (x² + 2x + 5)/(x + 1) dx." Board: top degree 2 ≥ bottom degree 1: divide; x² + 2x + 5 = (x + 1)(x + 1) + 4; (x² + 2x + 5)/(x + 1) = x + 1 + 4/(x + 1); ∫ = x²/2 + x + 4ln|x + 1| + C.
> Finally, flip it over: the integral of x squared plus two x plus five, over x plus one. [pause]
> Now the top has the bigger degree, so divide first. [pause]
> x plus one goes in x plus one times, with remainder four. So the fraction is x plus one, plus four over x plus one.
> Integrate: x squared over two, plus x, plus four ln of absolute x plus one, plus C. Three integrals built from the same pieces, three different tools. [long pause]

## Example 4: Substitution or arcsine?
**On screen:** "Find ∫ x/√(9 − x²) dx." Board: the derivative of 9 − x² is −2x, and x is a factor up to a constant; u = 9 − x², du = −2x dx; x dx = −½ du; ∫ x/√(9 − x²) dx = −½ ∫ u^(−1/2) du; = −½ · 2u^(1/2); = −√(9 − x²) + C; with 1 on top instead: ∫ dx/√(9 − x²) = arcsin(x/3) + C.
> Find the integral of x over the square root of nine minus x squared.
> A square root of a minus x squared might make you think of arcsine. But look at the top first. The derivative of nine minus x squared is negative two x, and x is right there on top. That's substitution. [pause]
> Let u be nine minus x squared. Then d u is negative two x d x.
> Divide both sides by negative two: x d x is negative one half d u.
> The integral becomes negative one half, times the integral of u to the negative one half, d u.
> Add one to the exponent and divide: negative one half, times two u to the one half. [pause]
> That's negative the square root of nine minus x squared, plus C. [pause]
> With just a one on top, there's no factor for substitution, and the arcsine rule gives arcsine of x over three. One small change on top, a different tool. [long pause]
