# 6.3 Riemann Sums, Summation Notation, and Definite Integral Notation

> The sums close in on one number. [pause]
> Take infinitely many rectangles, a limit, and you get the exact area. To write that down, we need two pieces of notation. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 6.3 · Riemann sums, summation notation, and definite integral notation." Unit 6 · Integration and Accumulation of Change.

## Sigma notation
**On screen:** A big Σ. Then "Σ_{k=1}^{4} (2k + 1)" with labels: "k = 1: start here", "4: stop here", "2k + 1: what to add". Beneath, the terms appear one at a time: k = 1: 3, k = 2: 5, k = 3: 7, k = 4: 9; then "3 + 5 + 7 + 9 = 24".
> The capital Greek letter sigma means "add up." [pause]
> The letter underneath, k, is a counter. It starts at the bottom number, one, and goes up to the top number, four. [pause]
> For each k, work out the expression and add. When k is one, two k plus one is three. Then five, seven, and nine. [pause]
> The sum is three plus five plus seven plus nine: twenty-four. [long pause]

## A Riemann sum in sigma notation
**On screen:** The right sum for f(x) = x³ on [0, 2] with n pieces. Board: Δx = (2 − 0)/n = 2/n; right ends: x_k = 0 + k · (2/n) = 2k/n; heights: f(x_k) = (2k/n)³; sum: Σ_{k=1}^{n} (2k/n)³ · (2/n). On the graph, the k-th rectangle is highlighted with its right end x_k and its height f(x_k) labeled.
> Now write a right Riemann sum this way. Take x cubed on zero to two, split into n equal pieces. [pause]
> Each width is two minus zero over n: delta x equals two over n.
> The right end of the k-th piece is k widths past the start: x sub k equals two k over n. [pause]
> Its height is f of x sub k: two k over n, cubed.
> So the sum adds up height times width, for k from one to n: two k over n cubed, times two over n. [long pause]

## The definite integral
**On screen:** "lim_{n→∞} Σ_{k=1}^{n} f(x_k) Δx = ∫_a^b f(x) dx". Then the integral alone, enlarged, with labels: the integral sign "a stretched S, for sum"; a and b "the lower and upper limits"; f(x) "the integrand"; dx "a tiny width". A small picture beside it: one thin rectangle of height f(x) and width dx.
> The limit of the Riemann sums as n goes to infinity has its own name: the definite integral of f from a to b. [pause]
> The integral sign is a stretched-out S, for sum. The numbers a and b are the limits of integration: where the area starts and stops. [pause]
> f of x is the integrand, the height. And d x stands for a tiny width. So the notation reads like what it means: add up height times width, over infinitely many tiny pieces. [pause]
> For a positive function, the definite integral is the exact area under the curve. [long pause]

## From sum to integral
**On screen:** The problem: "Write lim_{n→∞} Σ_{k=1}^{n} (1 + 2k/n)² (2/n) as a definite integral." Board: Δx = 2/n, so b − a = 2; x_k = 1 + 2k/n, so a = 1; b = 1 + 2 = 3; f(x) = x²; ∫_1^3 x² dx. On the right, the graph of x² with the region from 1 to 3 shaded.
> Write this limit as a definite integral. [try it]
> Find the width first. The factor two over n is delta x, so the interval has length two. [pause]
> The inside, one plus two k over n, is x sub k: start at one and take k steps of two over n. So a is one.
> The interval has length two, so b is one plus two: three. [pause]
> What's being done to x sub k? It's squared. So f of x is x squared.
> The limit is the integral from one to three of x squared d x. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Σ: add up, k from bottom to top." "Right sum: Σ f(a + kΔx) Δx, Δx = (b − a)/n." "Definite integral: ∫_a^b f(x) dx = lim_{n→∞} Σ f(x_k) Δx."
> Sigma means add up. A right sum adds f of a plus k delta x, times delta x. And the definite integral is the limit of those sums: the exact accumulated amount. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Evaluate a sum
**On screen:** "Evaluate Σ_{k=1}^{4} (2k/4)³ (2/4)." Board: k = 1: (1/2)³(1/2) = 1/16; k = 2: (1)³(1/2) = 1/2; k = 3: (3/2)³(1/2) = 27/16; k = 4: (2)³(1/2) = 4; total: 1/16 + 8/16 + 27/16 + 64/16 = 100/16 = 6.25.
> Evaluate this sum. It's the right Riemann sum for x cubed on zero to two, with four pieces. [pause]
> When k is one, two k over four is one half. One half cubed is one eighth, times one half: one sixteenth.
> When k is two: one cubed times one half, one half.
> When k is three: three halves cubed is twenty-seven eighths, times one half: twenty-seven sixteenths.
> When k is four: two cubed is eight, times one half: four. [pause]
> In sixteenths: one plus eight plus twenty-seven plus sixty-four, a hundred sixteenths. Six point two five. [long pause]

## Example 2: Another limit
**On screen:** "Write lim_{n→∞} Σ_{k=1}^{n} √(4k/n) · (4/n) as a definite integral." Board: Δx = 4/n, so b − a = 4; x_k = 4k/n = 0 + k(4/n), so a = 0; b = 4; f(x) = √x; ∫_0^4 √x dx.
> Write this limit as a definite integral. [pause]
> The width is four over n, so the interval has length four. [pause]
> Inside the root is four k over n: start at zero, take k steps of four over n. So a is zero and b is four.
> The function takes the square root, so f of x is root x.
> The limit is the integral from zero to four of root x d x. [long pause]

## Example 3: An integral by geometry
**On screen:** "Evaluate ∫_{−2}^{2} √(4 − x²) dx." On the right, y = √(4 − x²) drawn: the top half of a circle of radius 2, its inside shaded. Board: y = √(4 − x²) is the upper half of x² + y² = 4; radius 2; area = ½π(2)² = 2π; so the integral is 2π.
> Evaluate the integral from negative two to two of the square root of four minus x squared. [pause]
> Recognize the graph. y equals root four minus x squared is the top half of the circle x squared plus y squared equals four, with radius two. [pause]
> The integral is the area under it: half the area of the circle.
> One half times pi times two squared: two pi. [long pause]
