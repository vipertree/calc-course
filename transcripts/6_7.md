# 6.7 The Fundamental Theorem of Calculus and Definite Integrals

## A shortcut for area
**On screen:** The region under y = x² from 0 to 3, with a pile of thin rectangles and the words "limit of Riemann sums: hard". Then the rectangles clear and the board shows: "a function whose derivative is x²: F(x) = x³/3"; "F(3) − F(0) = 27/3 − 0 = 9". The region is labeled "area = 9".
> Finding the area under x squared from zero to three with Riemann sums means a limit of sums. That's a lot of work. [pause]
> Here's a shortcut. Think of a function whose derivative is x squared: x cubed over three works. [pause]
> Evaluate it at the ends and subtract: twenty-seven over three, minus zero. Nine. That's the exact area. [long pause]

## Why it works
**On screen:** "A(x) = ∫_a^x f(t) dt has A′ = f (part 1)." "F is any function with F′ = f: an antiderivative." "Two functions with the same derivative differ by a constant: F(x) = A(x) + C." Then: F(b) − F(a) = (A(b) + C) − (A(a) + C) = A(b) − A(a) = A(b) − 0 = ∫_a^b f(x) dx. The boxed theorem: "If F′ = f on [a, b], then ∫_a^b f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a)."
> From part one, the accumulation function A has derivative f. [pause]
> Now take any function F whose derivative is f. It's called an antiderivative of f. Two functions with the same derivative can only differ by a constant, so F is A plus some constant C. [pause]
> Then F of b minus F of a is A of b plus C, minus A of a plus C. The C's cancel, and A of a is zero. [pause]
> What's left is A of b: the integral from a to b. That's the second part of the Fundamental Theorem. Any antiderivative, evaluated at the ends and subtracted, gives the definite integral. [long pause]

## Reversing derivatives
**On screen:** A two-column chart, "f(x)" and "an antiderivative F(x)", each row checked by differentiating: xⁿ (n ≠ −1) | xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1); cos x | sin x; sin x | −cos x; eˣ | eˣ; 1/x | ln|x|; sec²x | tan x. Beneath, the notation: "F(x) |_a^b = F(b) − F(a)".
> To use it, you need an antiderivative: run a derivative rule backward. [pause]
> For x to the n, add one to the power and divide by the new power. Check: the derivative of x to the n plus one over n plus one is x to the n. [pause]
> Cosine comes from sine. Sine comes from negative cosine. e to the x is its own antiderivative. One over x comes from the natural log of the absolute value of x. And secant squared comes from tangent. [pause]
> When you evaluate, write the antiderivative with a bar and the limits, then subtract: top minus bottom. [long pause]

## Net change
**On screen:** "∫_a^b f′(x) dx = f(b) − f(a)": "the integral of a rate of change is the net change." Then "f(b) = f(a) + ∫_a^b f′(x) dx": "where it ends = where it starts + how much it changed". A small picture: a level starting at f(a), with the area under f′ added on top to reach f(b).
> Read the theorem with f prime as the integrand. The integral of f prime from a to b is f of b minus f of a. [pause]
> In words: the integral of a rate of change is the net change in the amount. That's what we saw with areas in topic six point one, and now we can compute it. [pause]
> Rearranged, it says where you end equals where you start, plus how much you changed: f of b equals f of a plus the integral of f prime. [long pause]

## Evaluating an integral
**On screen:** The problem: "Evaluate ∫_1^4 (3x² − 2x) dx." Board: antiderivative: x³ − x²; [x³ − x²]_1^4; = (4³ − 4²) − (1³ − 1²); = (64 − 16) − (1 − 1); = 48 − 0 = 48.
> Evaluate the integral from one to four of three x squared minus two x. [try it]
> Find an antiderivative, term by term. Three x squared comes from x cubed. Two x comes from x squared. So x cubed minus x squared. [pause]
> Evaluate at the top: four cubed minus four squared, sixty-four minus sixteen.
> Evaluate at the bottom: one cubed minus one squared, which is zero. [pause]
> Top minus bottom: forty-eight minus zero. Forty-eight. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "∫_a^b f(x) dx = F(b) − F(a), where F′ = f." "Antiderivatives: run derivative rules backward." "∫_a^b f′(x) dx = f(b) − f(a): net change." "f(b) = f(a) + ∫_a^b f′(x) dx."
> To evaluate a definite integral, find an antiderivative, plug in the top and the bottom, and subtract. And the integral of a rate is the net change. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A trig integral
**On screen:** "Evaluate ∫_0^π sin x dx." Board: antiderivative: −cos x; [−cos x]_0^π; = −cos π − (−cos 0); = −(−1) − (−1); = 1 + 1 = 2.
> Evaluate the integral from zero to pi of sine x. [pause]
> Sine comes from negative cosine, so the antiderivative is negative cosine x. [pause]
> At the top: negative cosine of pi. Cosine of pi is negative one, so that's positive one.
> At the bottom: negative cosine of zero, which is negative one. [pause]
> Top minus bottom: one minus negative one. Two. [long pause]

## Example 2: A root
**On screen:** "Evaluate ∫_1^9 √x dx." Board: √x = x^{1/2}; antiderivative: x^{3/2}/(3/2) = (2/3)x^{3/2}; [(2/3)x^{3/2}]_1^9; = (2/3)(9^{3/2}) − (2/3)(1^{3/2}); = (2/3)(27) − 2/3 = 18 − 2/3 = 52/3.
> Evaluate the integral from one to nine of the square root of x. [pause]
> Write the root as a power: x to the one half. Add one to get three halves, and divide by three halves, which is the same as multiplying by two thirds. [pause]
> At the top: two thirds times nine to the three halves. The square root of nine is three, cubed is twenty-seven. Two thirds of twenty-seven is eighteen.
> At the bottom: two thirds times one, two thirds. [pause]
> Eighteen minus two thirds: fifty-two thirds. [long pause]

## Example 3: Net change
**On screen:** "A plant is 10 cm tall at t = 1 week and grows at h′(t) = 6t − 2 cm per week. How tall is it at t = 3?" Board: h(3) = h(1) + ∫_1^3 h′(t) dt; ∫_1^3 (6t − 2) dt = [3t² − 2t]_1^3; = (27 − 6) − (3 − 2) = 21 − 1 = 20; h(3) = 10 + 20 = 30 cm.
> A plant is ten centimeters tall at week one and grows at h prime of t equals six t minus two centimeters per week. How tall is it at week three? [pause]
> Where it ends is where it starts plus how much it grew: h of three equals h of one plus the integral of h prime from one to three. [pause]
> An antiderivative of six t minus two is three t squared minus two t.
> At three: twenty-seven minus six, twenty-one. At one: three minus two, one. The plant grew twenty centimeters. [pause]
> So h of three is ten plus twenty: thirty centimeters. [long pause]
