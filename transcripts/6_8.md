# 6.8 Finding Antiderivatives and Indefinite Integrals: Basic Rules and Notation

## A whole family
**On screen:** The curves y = x², y = x² + 1, y = x² + 3 and y = x² − 2 drawn together, stacked vertically. At x = 1, short tangent lines on every curve all have the same slope, 2. Label: "every one has derivative 2x". Then "x² + C".
> Which function has derivative two x? x squared does. So does x squared plus one, and x squared plus three, and x squared minus two. [pause]
> They're the same curve, shifted up or down, so at any x they all have the same slope. [pause]
> Adding a constant never changes the derivative. So the full answer is x squared plus C, where C can be any constant. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 6.8 · Finding antiderivatives and indefinite integrals: basic rules and notation." Unit 6 · Integration and Accumulation of Change.

## Indefinite integrals
**On screen:** "∫ f(x) dx = F(x) + C, where F′(x) = f(x)." Labels: "no limits: indefinite"; "+ C: the constant of integration". Beside it, a comparison: "∫_1^3 2x dx = 8 is a number" and "∫ 2x dx = x² + C is a family of functions".
> The indefinite integral of f is written like a definite integral without limits. It means the whole family of antiderivatives: F of x plus C. [pause]
> The C is called the constant of integration. Leaving it off is the most common mistake on these problems. [pause]
> Notice the difference. A definite integral, with limits, is a number. An indefinite integral is a family of functions. [long pause]

## The basic rules
**On screen:** A chart builds in three groups, each line checked by differentiating the right side. Powers and constants: ∫ xⁿ dx = xⁿ⁺¹/(n + 1) + C (n ≠ −1); ∫ k f(x) dx = k∫ f(x) dx; ∫ [f(x) ± g(x)] dx = ∫ f(x) dx ± ∫ g(x) dx. Exponentials and logs: ∫ eˣ dx = eˣ + C; ∫ (1/x) dx = ln|x| + C. Trig: ∫ cos x dx = sin x + C; ∫ sin x dx = −cos x + C; ∫ sec²x dx = tan x + C; ∫ csc²x dx = −cot x + C; ∫ sec x tan x dx = sec x + C; ∫ csc x cot x dx = −csc x + C. Inverse trig: ∫ 1/(1 + x²) dx = arctan x + C; ∫ 1/√(1 − x²) dx = arcsin x + C.
> Every rule is a derivative rule read backward. [pause]
> Powers: add one to the exponent and divide by the new exponent. That works for every power except negative one. Constants come out front, and sums split. [pause]
> e to the x is its own antiderivative. One over x, the power that the power rule skips, gives the natural log of the absolute value of x. [pause]
> The trig rules come from the six trig derivatives. The minus signs belong to the co-functions: sine, cosecant squared, and cosecant cotangent all pick up a negative. [pause]
> And the two inverse trig derivatives give arctangent and arcsine. [long pause]

## Rewrite first
**On screen:** Three integrands, each rewritten before integrating: "∫ 1/√x dx: write x^{−1/2}"; "∫ (x³ − 2x)/x dx: split, x² − 2"; "∫ (x + 1)² dx: expand, x² + 2x + 1". Beneath: "No product rule or quotient rule for integrals: rewrite into sums of powers."
> Many integrals need rewriting before a rule applies. [pause]
> Roots become powers: one over root x is x to the negative one half. A fraction with one term on the bottom splits into separate terms. A product or a square gets multiplied out. [pause]
> There's no product rule or quotient rule for integrals, so rewriting is how you get to something the rules can handle. [long pause]

## Term by term
**On screen:** The problem: "Find ∫ (3√x − 4/x² + 2cos x) dx." Board: rewrite: 3x^{1/2} − 4x^{−2} + 2cos x; 3 · x^{3/2}/(3/2) = 2x^{3/2}; −4 · x^{−1}/(−1) = 4x^{−1} = 4/x; 2 sin x; answer: 2x^{3/2} + 4/x + 2sin x + C.
> Find the integral of three root x, minus four over x squared, plus two cosine x. [pause]
> Rewrite with powers: three x to the one half, minus four x to the negative two, plus two cosine x. [pause]
> First term: x to the three halves over three halves, times three. That's two x to the three halves.
> Second term: x to the negative one over negative one, times negative four. That's positive four x to the negative one, or four over x.
> Third term: two sine x. [pause]
> Put them together, and don't forget the C: two x to the three halves, plus four over x, plus two sine x, plus C. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "∫ f(x) dx = F(x) + C." "Read derivative rules backward." "Rewrite into powers before integrating." "Always + C."
> An indefinite integral is a family: an antiderivative plus C. Read the derivative rules backward, rewrite into powers when you need to, and always add C. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Expand first
**On screen:** "Find ∫ (x² + 1)² dx." Board: (x² + 1)² = x⁴ + 2x² + 1; ∫ (x⁴ + 2x² + 1) dx; = x⁵/5 + 2x³/3 + x + C.
> Find the integral of x squared plus one, squared. [pause]
> There's no rule for a square, so multiply it out: x to the fourth plus two x squared plus one. [pause]
> Integrate term by term. x to the fourth gives x to the fifth over five. Two x squared gives two x cubed over three. One gives x.
> The answer is x to the fifth over five, plus two x cubed over three, plus x, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 2: Split the fraction
**On screen:** "Find ∫ (x³ − 2x + 5)/x dx." Board: (x³ − 2x + 5)/x = x² − 2 + 5/x; ∫ (x² − 2 + 5/x) dx; = x³/3 − 2x + 5ln|x| + C.
> Find the integral of x cubed minus two x plus five, all over x. [pause]
> One term on the bottom, so split: x squared, minus two, plus five over x. [pause]
> x squared gives x cubed over three. Negative two gives negative two x. Five over x gives five natural log of the absolute value of x.
> The answer: x cubed over three minus two x plus five ln of absolute x, plus C. [long pause]

## Example 3: Finding C
**On screen:** "f′(x) = 6x² − 4 and f(1) = 3. Find f(x)." Board: f(x) = 2x³ − 4x + C; f(1) = 2 − 4 + C = 3; −2 + C = 3, C = 5; f(x) = 2x³ − 4x + 5.
> f prime of x is six x squared minus four, and f of one is three. Find f of x. [pause]
> Integrate: f of x is two x cubed minus four x, plus C. [pause]
> Use the point to find C. f of one is two minus four plus C, and that equals three.
> So negative two plus C is three, and C is five. [pause]
> f of x is two x cubed minus four x plus five. [long pause]
