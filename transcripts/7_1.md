# 7.1 Modeling Situations with Differential Equations

## A rule for the rate
**On screen:** A steaming mug of coffee beside a thermometer reading 90°F, in a 70°F room. A graph of temperature against time: the curve drops steeply at first, then levels off toward a dashed line at 70. Beside it: "the hotter it is compared to the room, the faster it cools". Then "dT/dt = −k(T − 70)".
> A hot cup of coffee cools off. But not at a steady rate: it cools fast when it's much hotter than the room, and slowly as it gets close to room temperature. [pause]
> So we may not know a formula for the temperature. What we do know is a rule for how fast it changes. [pause]
> Written as an equation, the rate of change of T is negative k times T minus seventy. An equation like this, one that involves a derivative, is called a differential equation. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 7.1 · Modeling situations with differential equations." Unit 7 · Differential Equations.

## What a differential equation is
**On screen:** "differential equation: an equation with a derivative in it". Examples: dy/dx = 3x²; dP/dt = 0.2P; dy/dx = x/y. Then "a solution is a function, not a number". Under dy/dx = 3x², the family y = x³ + C appears.
> A differential equation is any equation that has a derivative in it. [pause]
> Some give the rate in terms of x alone, like d y d x equals three x squared. Some give it in terms of the quantity itself, like d P d t equals point two P: the bigger P is, the faster it grows. [pause]
> A solution of a differential equation isn't a number. It's a function whose derivative obeys the rule. For d y d x equals three x squared, every function x cubed plus C works. [long pause]

## Translating words
**On screen:** A two-column chart builds row by row: "the rate of change of y is proportional to y | dy/dt = ky"; "… proportional to the square root of y | dy/dt = k√y"; "… inversely proportional to y | dy/dt = k/y"; "… proportional to the difference between y and 70 | dy/dt = k(y − 70)"; "… jointly proportional to y and 1000 − y | dy/dt = ky(1000 − y)". The words "rate of change" and "proportional to" are highlighted in each row.
> AP problems often describe a differential equation in words. "The rate of change of y" means d y d t. [pause]
> "Proportional to" means a constant times. So "the rate of change of y is proportional to y" is d y d t equals k y. [pause]
> "Proportional to the square root of y" is k root y. "Inversely proportional to y" is k over y. [pause]
> "Proportional to the difference between y and seventy" is k times y minus seventy. And "jointly proportional" to two things means k times their product. [long pause]

## Reading the rate
**On screen:** The problem: "dP/dt = 0.3P(1 − P/500), where P is a population. (a) Find dP/dt when P = 100. (b) Is P increasing or decreasing when P = 600?" Board: (a) 0.3(100)(1 − 100/500) = 30(0.8) = 24 individuals per unit time; (b) 0.3(600)(1 − 600/500) = 180(−0.2) = −36 < 0: decreasing.
> d P d t equals point three P times one minus P over five hundred. Find the rate when P is one hundred, and decide whether P is increasing or decreasing when P is six hundred. [try it]
> Plug in one hundred: point three times one hundred is thirty, and one minus one fifth is point eight. Thirty times point eight is twenty-four. [pause]
> So when the population is one hundred, it's growing at twenty-four individuals per unit of time.
> Plug in six hundred: point three times six hundred is one eighty, and one minus six fifths is negative point two. [pause]
> One eighty times negative point two is negative thirty-six. The rate is negative, so the population is decreasing. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "A differential equation gives the rate of change." "Its solutions are functions." "Proportional to: a constant k times." "Plug in to read the rate and its sign."
> A differential equation is a rule for a rate of change, and its solutions are functions. Translate "proportional to" as a constant times, and plug in values to read how fast and which way the quantity is changing. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A rumor
**On screen:** "In a school of 1000 students, a rumor spreads at a rate jointly proportional to the number N who have heard it and the number who have not. Write a differential equation for N." Board: rate of change: dN/dt; heard: N; not heard: 1000 − N; jointly proportional: dN/dt = kN(1000 − N).
> In a school of a thousand students, a rumor spreads at a rate jointly proportional to the number N who have heard it and the number who haven't. Write a differential equation for N. [pause]
> The rate of change is d N d t. The number who have heard it is N, and the number who haven't is a thousand minus N. [pause]
> Jointly proportional means k times the product.
> So d N d t equals k N times a thousand minus N. [long pause]

## Example 2: A slope at a point
**On screen:** "dy/dx = x² − y. Find dy/dx at the point (2, 1). Is y increasing or decreasing there?" Board: dy/dx = 2² − 1 = 4 − 1 = 3; 3 > 0: increasing.
> d y d x equals x squared minus y. Find d y d x at the point two, one, and say whether y is increasing or decreasing there. [pause]
> Plug in both coordinates: two squared minus one, which is three. [pause]
> The derivative is positive, so the solution through that point is increasing there. [long pause]

## Example 3: Cooling coffee
**On screen:** "Coffee cools according to dT/dt = −0.1(T − 70), with T in °F and t in minutes. Find dT/dt when T = 130 and interpret it." Board: dT/dt = −0.1(130 − 70) = −0.1(60) = −6; when the coffee is 130°F, its temperature is decreasing at 6°F per minute.
> Coffee cools according to d T d t equals negative point one times T minus seventy, with T in degrees Fahrenheit and t in minutes. Find the rate when T is one thirty, and interpret it. [pause]
> One thirty minus seventy is sixty. Negative point one times sixty is negative six. [pause]
> So when the coffee is one hundred thirty degrees, its temperature is decreasing at six degrees per minute. [long pause]
