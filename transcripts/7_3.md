# 7.3 Sketching Slope Fields

## A picture of a differential equation
**On screen:** Axes. For dy/dx = x − y, a short segment appears at one point, (1, −1), with slope 2. Then segments fill in at every grid point, one row at a time, until the whole slope field is drawn. Finally two solution curves are traced, each following the segments like a leaf on a current.
> A differential equation like d y d x equals x minus y gives a slope at every point. At one, negative one, the slope is one minus negative one: two. Draw a tiny segment with that slope. [pause]
> Do that at lots of points, and you get a slope field: a picture of the differential equation. [pause]
> Every solution curve has to follow the segments, like a leaf floating on a current. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 7.3 · Sketching slope fields." Unit 7 · Differential Equations.

## Patterns to look for
**On screen:** Three small slope fields side by side. "dy/dx = x": slopes are equal down every vertical column. "dy/dx = y": slopes are equal along every horizontal row. "dy/dx = x − y": slopes are zero along the line y = x, highlighted.
> Some patterns save time. If d y d x depends only on x, then every point in the same vertical column gets the same slope. [pause]
> If it depends only on y, every point in the same horizontal row gets the same slope. [pause]
> And wherever the right side equals zero, the segments are flat. For x minus y, that's along the whole line y equals x. [long pause]

## Sketching nine segments
**On screen:** The problem: "Sketch the slope field for dy/dx = x − y at the nine points with x, y ∈ {−1, 0, 1}." On the right, a 3-by-3 grid of dots. Board: a table of slopes, one row per x: x = −1: y = −1: 0, y = 0: −1, y = 1: −2; x = 0: 1, 0, −1; x = 1: 2, 1, 0. Each segment is drawn at its dot as its slope is computed.
> Sketch the slope field for d y d x equals x minus y at the nine points where x and y are each negative one, zero, or one. [try it]
> Work one column at a time. At x equals negative one, the slopes are negative one minus y: zero, negative one, and negative two as y goes up. [pause]
> At x equals zero, the slopes are negative y: one, zero, and negative one.
> At x equals one: two, one, and zero. [pause]
> Draw each segment at its point. Zero is flat, one is a forty-five degree rise, and negative two is steep downhill. The three flat segments line up along y equals x. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Slope field: a short segment of slope dy/dx at each point." "Depends only on x: same slope down columns. Only on y: same slope along rows." "Right side zero: flat segments." "Solution curves follow the segments."
> A slope field draws the slope from the differential equation at each point. Look for columns, rows, and flat lines, and remember that solution curves follow the segments. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Slopes from y alone
**On screen:** "Sketch the slope field for dy/dx = y/2 at the points with x ∈ {−1, 0, 1} and y ∈ {−2, 0, 2}." Board: depends only on y; y = −2: −1; y = 0: 0; y = 2: 1. The grid fills row by row: steep-ish downward segments along the bottom row, flat along the middle, upward along the top.
> Sketch the slope field for d y d x equals y over two at the points where x is negative one, zero, or one and y is negative two, zero, or two. [pause]
> The slope depends only on y, so each row has one slope. [pause]
> When y is negative two, the slope is negative one. When y is zero, it's zero. When y is two, it's one.
> So the bottom row slants down, the middle row is flat, and the top row slants up, the same all the way across. [long pause]

## Example 2: Which equation?
**On screen:** "Which differential equation has this slope field: dy/dx = x, dy/dx = y, or dy/dx = −x/y?" The field shown: segments that circle the origin, flat on the y-axis and vertical on the x-axis. Board: on the y-axis (x = 0) the slopes are 0: rules out dy/dx = y; slopes change along rows, so not dy/dx = x alone; −x/y: 0 at x = 0, undefined (vertical) at y = 0: matches.
> Which differential equation has this slope field: d y d x equals x, d y d x equals y, or d y d x equals negative x over y? [pause]
> Look at the y axis, where x is zero. The segments there are flat. That rules out y, which would give a nonzero slope at most of those points. [pause]
> The slopes change as you move along a row, so it isn't x alone either.
> Negative x over y is zero when x is zero, and it has no value, a vertical segment, when y is zero. That matches the picture: the segments circle the origin. [long pause]
