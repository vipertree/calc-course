## Equilibrium solutions
**On screen:** "If dy/dx = 0 whenever y = c, then y = c is a solution: an equilibrium solution." For y(2 − y): y = 0 and y = 2 drawn as horizontal lines. Check: y = 2 constant, so dy/dx = 0, and 2(2 − 2) = 0. Then arrows: nearby solutions approach y = 2 from both sides, and move away from y = 0.
> Look at the flat rows. If d y d x is zero every time y is two, then the constant function y equals two is itself a solution: its derivative is zero, and so is the right side. [pause]
> Constant solutions like this are called equilibrium solutions. Here there are two: y equals zero and y equals two. [pause]
> Solutions nearby head toward y equals two from both sides, but move away from y equals zero. So for the solution through zero, one, the limit as x goes to infinity is two. [long pause]

## Concavity from the equation
**On screen:** The problem: "dy/dx = y(2 − y). (a) Find d²y/dx² in terms of y. (b) Is the solution through (0, 1.5) concave up or concave down there?" Board: dy/dx = 2y − y²; d²y/dx² = (2 − 2y) dy/dx (chain rule); = (2 − 2y) · y(2 − y); at y = 1.5: (2 − 3)(1.5)(0.5) = −0.75 < 0: concave down.
> For d y d x equals y times two minus y, find the second derivative, and decide whether the solution through zero, one point five is concave up or down there. [pause]
> Multiply out: d y d x is two y minus y squared. [pause]
> Differentiate with respect to x. Because y is a function of x, each y term picks up a factor of d y d x: the second derivative is two minus two y, times d y d x.
> Substitute d y d x: two minus two y, times y times two minus y. [pause]
> At y equals one point five: two minus three is negative one, times one point five, times point five. Negative point seven five. Concave down. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Sketch through a point: follow the segments both ways." "Equilibrium: y = c with dy/dx = 0 for all x." "Long run: read where solutions level off." "Concavity: d²y/dx², differentiating implicitly and substituting dy/dx."
> From a slope field you can sketch particular solutions, spot equilibrium solutions, and read long-run behavior. For concavity, differentiate the equation, remembering that y depends on x. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Equilibrium solutions
**On screen:** "Find the equilibrium solutions of dy/dt = y² − 4." Board: y² − 4 = 0; (y − 2)(y + 2) = 0; y = 2 and y = −2; check: a constant has derivative 0, and 2² − 4 = 0.
> Find the equilibrium solutions of d y d t equals y squared minus four. [pause]
> Set the right side equal to zero: y squared minus four is zero. [pause]
> Factor: y minus two, times y plus two. So y is two or negative two.
> The equilibrium solutions are the constant functions y equals two and y equals negative two. [long pause]

## Example 2: A second derivative in x and y
**On screen:** "dy/dx = x − y. Find d²y/dx² in terms of x and y, and decide the concavity of the solution through (0, 1) at that point." Board: d²y/dx² = 1 − dy/dx; = 1 − (x − y) = 1 − x + y; at (0, 1): 1 − 0 + 1 = 2 > 0: concave up.
> d y d x equals x minus y. Find the second derivative in terms of x and y, and decide the concavity of the solution through zero, one. [pause]
> Differentiate: the derivative of x is one, and the derivative of y is d y d x. So the second derivative is one minus d y d x. [pause]
> Substitute: one minus x minus y, which is one minus x plus y.
> At zero, one: one minus zero plus one is two. Positive, so concave up. [long pause]
