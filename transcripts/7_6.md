# 7.6 Finding General Solutions Using Separation of Variables

## Stop and ponder
**On screen:** "dy/dx = xy" at the top. Under it, "∫ xy dx ?" with a question mark, and "xy is a function of x and y". It is crossed out. Then a plan: "(a function of y) dy = (a function of x) dx", with "then integrate both sides".
> Here's a differential equation: d y d x equals x times y. How do we find y? [pause]
> Stop and think about it first. We can't just integrate x y with respect to x, because it's a function of x and y. And y depends on x in a way we don't know yet. [pause]
> But if we can break it into two separate pieces, a function of y with d y on one side and a function of x with d x on the other, then we might be able to integrate each side and solve. [long pause]

## Sorting the variables
**On screen:** "dy/dx = xy" at the top. Under it, "÷ y" and "· dx" on both sides, then "dy/y = x dx", with "all the y's with dy" under the left side and "all the x's with dx" under the right, well apart. Then "∫ dy/y = ∫ x dx". Then the left side is rewritten as "∫ (1/y) dy": the 1/y is labeled "integrand" and the dy "with respect to y".
> Divide both sides by y, and multiply both sides by d x. Now every y is on the left with d y, and every x is on the right with d x. [pause]
> Once they're separated, integrate both sides. [pause]
> We usually write the left side as d y over y. What it means is the integral of one over y, d y. One over y is the integrand, and we're integrating with respect to y. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 7.6 · Finding general solutions using separation of variables." Unit 7 · Differential Equations.

## The method
**On screen:** The original problem stays in a box at the top left: "dy/dx = xy". Steps under it, each applied to the problem: "1. Separate: dy/y = x dx." "2. Integrate both sides: ∫ (1/y) dy = ∫ x dx, so ln|y| = x²/2 + C."
> Here are the steps, on the same equation: d y d x equals x y. Step one: separate. All the y's and d y on one side, all the x's and d x on the other. [pause]
> Step two: integrate both sides. The integral of one over y, d y, is ln of absolute y. The integral of x, d x, is x squared over two. [long pause]

## Only one constant
**On screen:** The problem stays in its box. "3. One constant." Then "ln|y| + D = x²/2 + C", with the D and the C in two colors: "each side gets a constant". Then "subtract D from both sides": "ln|y| = x²/2 + C − D". The "C − D" is boxed: "just a new constant". Then "ln|y| = x²/2 + C": "one constant, on one side".
> Step three: you only need one constant of integration. [pause]
> Both integrals are indefinite, so really each side gets a constant. Call one D and the other C: ln of absolute y plus D equals x squared over two plus C. [pause]
> Subtract D from both sides. Now the right side ends in C minus D. [pause]
> But C minus D is just some new constant. So we don't bother with two. We put one constant on one side, usually the x side, and call it C. [long pause]

## Solve for y
**On screen:** The problem stays in its box. "4. Solve for y: |y| = e^{x²/2 + C} = e^C e^{x²/2}, so y = Ae^{x²/2}." A note: "A = ±e^C, any constant". A small graph shows the family of solutions.
> Step four: solve for y if you can. Exponentiate: absolute y is e to the x squared over two plus C, which is e to the C times e to the x squared over two. [pause]
> e to the C is just a positive constant, and the plus or minus from the absolute value can join it. Call the whole thing A. The general solution is y equals A e to the x squared over two. [long pause]

## Another one
**On screen:** The problem: "Find the general solution of dy/dx = x²/y." Board: y dy = x² dx; ∫ y dy = ∫ x² dx; y²/2 = x³/3 + C; y² = (2/3)x³ + 2C = (2/3)x³ + K; y = ±√((2/3)x³ + K).
> Find the general solution of d y d x equals x squared over y. [try it]
> Multiply both sides by y and by d x: y d y equals x squared d x. [pause]
> Integrate both sides: the integral of y, d y, equals the integral of x squared, d x. That's y squared over two equals x cubed over three, plus C.
> Multiply by two: y squared equals two thirds x cubed plus two C. Two C is just another constant; call it K. [pause]
> Take the square root, keeping both signs: y equals plus or minus the square root of two thirds x cubed plus K. An initial condition will pick the sign later. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Separate: y's with dy, x's with dx." "Integrate both sides; one + C." "Solve for y: constants combine (A = ±e^C, K = 2C)." "A general solution is a family."
> To separate variables: put all the y's with d y and all the x's with d x, integrate both sides with one constant, and solve for y. The result is a whole family of solutions. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A trig factor
**On screen:** "Find the general solution of dy/dx = y cos x." Board: dy/y = cos x dx; ∫ (1/y) dy = ∫ cos x dx; ln|y| = sin x + C; |y| = e^C e^{sin x}; y = Ae^{sin x}.
> Find the general solution of d y d x equals y cosine x. [pause]
> Separate: d y over y equals cosine x d x. [pause]
> Integrate both sides: the integral of one over y, d y, equals the integral of cosine x, d x. So ln of absolute y equals sine x plus C.
> Exponentiate: absolute y is e to the C times e to the sine x. [pause]
> So y equals A e to the sine x, where A is any constant. [long pause]

## Example 2: Newton's law of cooling
**On screen:** "Find the general solution of dT/dt = k(T − 70)." Board: dT/(T − 70) = k dt; ∫ 1/(T − 70) dT = ∫ k dt; ln|T − 70| = kt + C; |T − 70| = e^C e^{kt}; T − 70 = Ae^{kt}; T = 70 + Ae^{kt}.
> Find the general solution of d T d t equals k times T minus seventy. [pause]
> Separate: d T over T minus seventy equals k d t. [pause]
> Integrate both sides. On the left, one over T minus seventy is the integrand, with respect to T. So ln of absolute T minus seventy equals k t plus C.
> Exponentiate and combine the constants: T minus seventy equals A e to the k t. [pause]
> So T equals seventy plus A e to the k t. Every cooling curve approaches seventy. [long pause]

## Example 3: An exponential mix
**On screen:** "Find the general solution of dy/dx = e^{x − y}." Board: e^{x − y} = eˣ e^{−y}; eʸ dy = eˣ dx; ∫ eʸ dy = ∫ eˣ dx; eʸ = eˣ + C; y = ln(eˣ + C).
> Find the general solution of d y d x equals e to the x minus y. [pause]
> Split the exponent: e to the x minus y is e to the x times e to the negative y. [pause]
> Multiply both sides by e to the y and by d x: e to the y d y equals e to the x d x.
> Integrate both sides: the integral of e to the y, d y, is e to the y, and the integral of e to the x, d x, is e to the x. So e to the y equals e to the x plus C. [pause]
> Take the natural log: y equals ln of e to the x plus C. [long pause]
