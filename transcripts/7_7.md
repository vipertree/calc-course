# 7.7 Finding Particular Solutions Using Initial Conditions and Separation of Variables

## One curve from a family
**On screen:** The family y = ±√(x² + K) drawn for several K, filling the plane like nested hyperbolas. A dot at (1, 2). One curve, y = √(x² + 3), lights up through the dot; the rest fade.
> Separation of variables gives a whole family of solutions. A real problem usually wants just one of them. [pause]
> An initial condition, like y equals two when x equals one, picks the one curve in the family that passes through that point. [pause]
> That one curve is called the particular solution. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 7.7 · Finding particular solutions using initial conditions and separation of variables." Unit 7 · Differential Equations.

## Find C right away
**On screen:** The problem: "Find the particular solution of dy/dx = x/y with y(1) = 2." Board: y dy = x dx; y²/2 = x²/2 + C; substitute (1, 2) now: 4/2 = 1/2 + C, C = 3/2; y²/2 = x²/2 + 3/2, y² = x² + 3; y = +√(x² + 3), positive because y(1) = 2 > 0. A boxed tip: "Substitute the point as soon as you've integrated."
> Find the particular solution of d y d x equals x over y with y of one equal to two. [pause]
> Separate and integrate: y squared over two equals x squared over two, plus C. [pause]
> Now substitute the point right away, before any more algebra. Two squared over two is two, and one squared over two is one half. So C is three halves.
> Multiply through by two: y squared equals x squared plus three. [pause]
> The square root could be positive or negative, but y of one is two, which is positive. So y equals the positive square root of x squared plus three. [pause]
> Finding C as soon as you've integrated is the safest habit, and it's how the AP exam awards the points. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Separate, integrate, then substitute the initial condition to find C." "Then solve for y, choosing the sign that fits the point." "Watch the domain: the solution must be defined at and around the starting point."
> Separate, integrate, and substitute the initial condition to find C before solving for y. Choose the sign that matches the point, and make sure the solution is defined around it. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Exponential growth
**On screen:** "Find the particular solution of dy/dx = 2xy with y(0) = 3." Board: dy/y = 2x dx; ln|y| = x² + C; (0, 3): ln 3 = 0 + C, C = ln 3; ln|y| = x² + ln 3; |y| = e^{x²} · e^{ln 3} = 3e^{x²}; y = 3e^{x²} (y > 0 near the point).
> Find the particular solution of d y d x equals two x y with y of zero equal to three. [pause]
> Separate: d y over y equals two x d x. Integrate: ln of absolute y equals x squared plus C. [pause]
> Substitute zero, three: ln three equals zero plus C, so C is ln three.
> Exponentiate: absolute y is e to the x squared times e to the ln three, which is three e to the x squared. [pause]
> y is positive at the starting point, so y equals three e to the x squared. [long pause]

## Example 2: A cooling cup
**On screen:** "Coffee cools by dT/dt = −0.1(T − 70), with T(0) = 190. Find T(t), then T(10)." Board: dT/(T − 70) = −0.1 dt; ln|T − 70| = −0.1t + C; (0, 190): ln 120 = C; T − 70 = 120e^{−0.1t}; T = 70 + 120e^{−0.1t}; T(10) = 70 + 120e^{−1} ≈ 114.1°F.
> Coffee cools by d T d t equals negative point one times T minus seventy, starting at one ninety. Find T of t, then T of ten. [pause]
> Separate and integrate: ln of absolute T minus seventy equals negative point one t plus C. [pause]
> At t equals zero, T is one ninety, so ln one twenty equals C.
> Exponentiate: T minus seventy equals one twenty e to the negative point one t. So T equals seventy plus one twenty e to the negative point one t. [pause]
> At ten minutes: seventy plus one twenty over e, about one fourteen point one degrees. [long pause]

## Example 3: A domain
**On screen:** "Find the particular solution of dy/dx = xy² with y(0) = 1, and give its domain." Board: dy/y² = x dx; −1/y = x²/2 + C; (0, 1): −1 = C; −1/y = x²/2 − 1 = (x² − 2)/2; y = 2/(2 − x²); undefined at x = ±√2; domain containing 0: −√2 < x < √2.
> Find the particular solution of d y d x equals x y squared with y of zero equal to one, and give its domain. [pause]
> Separate: d y over y squared equals x d x. Integrate: negative one over y equals x squared over two, plus C. [pause]
> At zero, one: negative one equals C. So negative one over y equals x squared over two minus one, which is x squared minus two, over two.
> Flip and change sign: y equals two over two minus x squared. [pause]
> That's undefined at plus or minus root two. The solution through x equals zero lives on the interval between them: from negative root two to root two. [long pause]
