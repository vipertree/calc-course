# 7.9 Logistic Models with Differential Equations

## Growth that levels off
**On screen:** An S-shaped curve: a population that starts small, grows faster and faster, then slows and levels off under a dashed line labeled "carrying capacity L". Beside it, "dP/dt = kP(1 − P/L)". A note: "BC only".
> Exponential growth can't go on forever. Food, space, or some other limit catches up. This topic is for BC. [pause]
> A logistic model grows almost exponentially at first, then slows, and levels off at a maximum population the environment can support, called the carrying capacity, L. [pause]
> The differential equation is d P d t equals k P times one minus P over L. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 7.9 · Logistic models with differential equations." Unit 7 · Differential Equations.

## Reading the equation
**On screen:** "dP/dt = kP(1 − P/L)". Three labeled cases with arrows on a vertical P line: "P small: 1 − P/L ≈ 1, so dP/dt ≈ kP (nearly exponential)"; "0 < P < L: dP/dt > 0 (growing)"; "P > L: dP/dt < 0 (shrinking toward L)". Equilibria marked at P = 0 and P = L. Then "lim_{t→∞} P(t) = L for any P(0) > 0".
> When P is small, one minus P over L is close to one, so the growth is nearly exponential. [pause]
> Between zero and L, both factors are positive, so the population grows. Above L, one minus P over L is negative, so it shrinks back toward L. [pause]
> So P equals zero and P equals L are equilibrium solutions, and any positive starting population heads toward L. The limit as t goes to infinity is the carrying capacity. [long pause]

## Fastest growth at half the capacity
**On screen:** A graph with P on the horizontal axis and dP/dt on the vertical: the downward parabola dP/dt = kP − (k/L)P², zero at P = 0 and P = L, with its vertex at P = L/2 marked "fastest growth". Then, on the S-curve, the point where P = L/2 is marked as the inflection point.
> How fast is it growing? Treat d P d t as a function of P: k P minus k over L times P squared. [pause]
> That's a downward parabola with zeros at zero and L. Its highest point is halfway between, at P equals L over two. [pause]
> So the population grows fastest when it reaches half the carrying capacity. That's also where the S curve changes from concave up to concave down. [long pause]

## Using the model
**On screen:** The problem: "dP/dt = 0.4P(1 − P/1000), P(0) = 100. (a) Find lim_{t→∞} P(t). (b) For what P is the population growing fastest? (c) Is the graph of P concave up or concave down when P = 300?" Board: (a) L = 1000, and P(0) > 0: limit 1000; (b) P = L/2 = 500; (c) 300 < 500: dP/dt still increasing, concave up.
> d P d t equals point four P times one minus P over a thousand, with P of zero equal to a hundred. Answer the three questions shown. [try it]
> The carrying capacity is a thousand, and the population starts positive, so the limit as t goes to infinity is a thousand. [pause]
> It grows fastest at half the carrying capacity: five hundred.
> At three hundred, the population hasn't reached five hundred yet, so its growth rate is still increasing. The graph is concave up there. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "dP/dt = kP(1 − P/L): L is the carrying capacity." "P(0) > 0: lim P = L." "Fastest growth (inflection point) at P = L/2." "Solution: P = L/(1 + Ae^{−kt})."
> In a logistic model, L is the carrying capacity: the limit of every positive solution. Growth is fastest at half of L, where the graph has its inflection point. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Find L first
**On screen:** "dP/dt = 0.002P(500 − P). Find the carrying capacity and the population at which it grows fastest." Board: 0.002P(500 − P) = 0.002 · 500 · P(1 − P/500) = P(1 − P/500); k = 1, L = 500; fastest at P = 250.
> d P d t equals point zero zero two P times five hundred minus P. Find the carrying capacity and the population where growth is fastest. [pause]
> Rewrite it in the standard form. Factor five hundred out of the parentheses: point zero zero two times five hundred is one, so it's P times one minus P over five hundred. [pause]
> So k is one and the carrying capacity is five hundred.
> The growth is fastest at half of that: two hundred fifty. [long pause]

## Example 2: The solution formula
**On screen:** "The solution of dP/dt = kP(1 − P/L) is P = L/(1 + Ae^{−kt}). For L = 1000, k = 0.4 and P(0) = 100, find A and P(5)." Board: P(0) = 1000/(1 + A) = 100, so 1 + A = 10, A = 9; P(5) = 1000/(1 + 9e^{−2}) ≈ 450.9.
> The solution of the logistic equation is P equals L over one plus A e to the negative k t. With L equal to a thousand, k equal to point four, and P of zero equal to a hundred, find A and P of five. [pause]
> At t equals zero, e to the zero is one, so a thousand over one plus A equals a hundred. [pause]
> So one plus A is ten, and A is nine.
> P of five is a thousand over one plus nine e to the negative two: about four fifty-one. [long pause]
