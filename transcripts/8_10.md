# 8.10 Volume with Disc Method: Revolving Around Other Axes

## A different axis
**On screen:** The region bounded by y = x² and y = 4, sketched twice side by side. The left copy turns and sweeps around the x-axis into a solid with a hole through the middle, shaped like a spool hollowed out from both ends ("a hole: next topic", under it). The right copy has the line y = 4 drawn dashed: it turns and sweeps around that line into a solid with no hole, centered on that line. Both turn slowly.
> The axis of revolution doesn't have to be the x-axis or the y-axis. It can be any horizontal or vertical line, such as y equals four. [pause]
> Spin this region around the x-axis and the solid has a hole through the middle. That's the next topic. [pause]
> Spin it around the line y equals four instead. The region runs along that axis the whole way, so there's no hole, and every slice is a solid disc. [pause]
> The only thing that changes is the radius. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 8.10 · Volume with disc method: revolving around other axes." Unit 8 · Applications of Integration.

## The radius is a distance to the axis
**On screen:** The region between y = x² and y = 4, sketched flat, with the axis y = 4 drawn dashed. At one x, a segment runs from the curve up to the axis: "R". On the board beside the graph (never on it): "R = 4 − x²". At the matching point on the left, a gray segment from the x-axis up to the curve; the board adds "R = x²" struck through, with "x² is the distance down to the x-axis" under it. The sketch turns, the region sweeps around y = 4, and the solid fades to a ghost around one disc at x, centered on the axis, with its radius R drawn on its face; then it turns back to the sketch. Board: "R = (bigger) − (smaller)", "axis above the curve: R = k − f(x)", "axis below the curve: R = f(x) − k".
> Here's the region between y equals x squared and y equals four, and the axis is the line y equals four. [pause]
> The radius of a slice is the distance from the axis to the curve. With the axis at four and the curve at x squared, that distance is four minus x squared. [pause]
> Not x squared. X squared is the distance from the curve down to the x-axis, which isn't the axis anymore. [pause]
> Spin the region around y equals four and look at the slice at x. It's a disc centered on the axis, and its radius is four minus x squared. [pause]
> Write the radius as the bigger value minus the smaller one, so it's positive. If the axis is above the curve, it's k minus f. If it's below, f minus k. [long pause]

## Spinning about y = 4
**On screen:** The problem: "The region bounded by y = x² and y = 4 is revolved about the line y = 4. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ₐᵇ [R(x)]² dx". The picture is sketched as the board fills in: the parabola, the line y = 4, the region between them shaded, the axis y = 4 dashed. Board: "x² = 4 at x = ±2". The sketch turns and the region sweeps around y = 4; the solid fades to a ghost with one disc at x and its radius R from the curve up to the axis; the picture turns back to the flat sketch with that one radius. Board: "one slice at x: a disc centered on y = 4"; "R(x) = 4 − x²"; "R² = (4 − x²)² = 16 − 8x² + x⁴"; "V = π∫₋₂² (16 − 8x² + x⁴) dx"; "= 2π[16x − 8x³/3 + x⁵/5]₀²"; "= 2π(32 − 64/3 + 32/5)"; "= 512π/15 ≈ 107.23".
> The region bounded by x squared and the line y equals four is spun around y equals four. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: the parabola, and the line y equals four across the top. They meet where x squared is four, at plus or minus two. The region is between them. [pause]
> Spin it around y equals four. We only need one slice, at x: a disc centered on the axis. [pause]
> Its radius runs from the curve up to the axis: four minus x squared. [pause]
> Square it: sixteen minus eight x squared plus x to the fourth. [pause]
> The region is symmetric, so integrate from zero to two and double. The antiderivative is sixteen x, minus eight x cubed over three, plus x to the fifth over five. [pause]
> At two that's thirty-two minus sixty-four thirds plus thirty-two fifths. Times two pi: five hundred twelve pi over fifteen, about one hundred seven point two three. [long pause]

## Vertical lines too
**On screen:** The region between the curve x = y² and the line x = 1, sketched flat, with the vertical axis x = 1 dashed. It turns and sweeps around x = 1; the solid fades to a ghost with one horizontal disc at height y, and its radius, from the curve over to the axis, drawn on it: "R". Back to the sketch. Board: "vertical axis: horizontal slices, integrate in y"; "R = (axis) − (curve) = 1 − y²".
> The same idea works for a vertical axis, such as x equals one. Here's the region between the curve x equals y squared and the line x equals one. [pause]
> Spin it around x equals one. Slices perpendicular to that axis are horizontal discs, so integrate in y. [pause]
> The radius is now a horizontal distance: from the curve x equals y squared over to the axis x equals one. That's one minus y squared. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "V = π∫ R² (d x or d y)"; "R = distance from the axis to the curve: bigger − smaller"; "axis y = k: R = |f(x) − k|"; "axis x = k: R = |g(y) − k|".
> For any horizontal or vertical axis, the disc method is still pi times the integral of R squared. The radius is the distance from the axis to the curve: the bigger value minus the smaller. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: About y = 2
**On screen:** "The region bounded by y = √x, y = 2, and the y-axis is revolved about the line y = 2. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ₐᵇ [R(x)]² dx". Sketch: y = √x, the line y = 2 (dashed: it is also the axis), the region between them and the y-axis shaded; it sweeps around y = 2, the solid fades to a ghost with one disc at x and its radius R, and the picture turns back. Board: √x = 2 at x = 4; one slice at x: a disc centered on y = 2; R = 2 − √x; R² = 4 − 4√x + x; V = π∫₀⁴ (4 − 4√x + x) dx; = π[4x − (8/3)x^{3/2} + x²/2]₀⁴; = π(16 − 64/3 + 8); = 8π/3 ≈ 8.38.
> The region bounded by root x, the line y equals two, and the y-axis is spun around y equals two. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: root x rises from the origin and reaches two at x equals four. The region is above the curve, below the line y equals two, to the right of the y-axis. [pause]
> Spin it around y equals two. One slice at x is a disc, and its radius runs from the curve up to the axis: two minus root x. [pause]
> Square it carefully: four minus four root x plus x. [pause]
> The antiderivative is four x, minus eight thirds x to the three halves, plus x squared over two. At four: sixteen minus sixty-four thirds plus eight. That's eight thirds, so the volume is eight pi over three. [long pause]

## Example 2: About x = 1
**On screen:** "The region bounded by x = y² and x = 1 is revolved about the line x = 1. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ᶜᵈ [R(y)]² dy". Sketch: the sideways parabola x = y², the line x = 1 (dashed: the axis), the region between them shaded; it sweeps around x = 1, the solid fades to a ghost with one horizontal disc at height y and its radius R, and the picture turns back. Board: y² = 1 at y = ±1; one slice at y: a horizontal disc; R = 1 − y²; R² = 1 − 2y² + y⁴; V = π∫₋₁¹ (1 − 2y² + y⁴) dy; = 2π(1 − ⅔ + ⅕); = 16π/15 ≈ 3.35.
> The region bounded by x equals y squared and the line x equals one is spun around x equals one. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: x equals y squared is a parabola opening to the right. It meets the line x equals one at y equals plus or minus one. [pause]
> Spin it around x equals one. One slice at y is a horizontal disc, and its radius is the horizontal distance from the curve to the axis: one minus y squared. [pause]
> Square it: one minus two y squared plus y to the fourth. [pause]
> Twice the integral from zero to one: two pi times one minus two thirds plus one fifth. That's sixteen pi over fifteen. [long pause]

## Example 3: A vertical axis on the right
**On screen:** "The region bounded by y = x², the x-axis, and x = 1 is revolved about the line x = 1. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ᶜᵈ [R(y)]² dy". Sketch: the parabola from the origin to (1, 1), the line x = 1 (dashed: the axis), the region under the curve shaded; it sweeps around x = 1, the solid fades to a ghost with one horizontal disc at height y and its radius R, and the picture turns back. Board: horizontal slices, 0 ≤ y ≤ 1; the curve is x = √y; R = 1 − √y; R² = 1 − 2√y + y; V = π∫₀¹ (1 − 2√y + y) dy; = π(1 − 4/3 + ½); = π/6 ≈ 0.52.
> The region under x squared from zero to one is spun around the line x equals one, its right edge. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: the parabola from the origin up to the point one, one, the line x equals one, and the region under the curve. [pause]
> The axis is vertical, so integrate in y, from zero to one. Write the curve as x equals root y. The slice at height y is a horizontal disc, and its radius runs from the curve over to the axis: one minus root y. [pause]
> Squared: one minus two root y plus y. [pause]
> Integrate: one minus four thirds plus one half, which is one sixth. The volume is pi over six. [long pause]
