# 8.10 Volume with Disc Method: Revolving Around Other Axes

## A different axis
**On screen:** The region bounded by y = x² and y = 4. First it spins around the x-axis (leaving a hole in the middle, marked "next topic"), then the picture resets and it spins around the line y = 4 instead, making a solid with no hole.
> The axis of revolution doesn't have to be the x-axis or the y-axis. It can be any horizontal or vertical line, like y equals four. [pause]
> When the region touches the axis all the way along, the slices are still solid discs. [pause]
> The only thing that changes is the radius. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 8.10 · Volume with disc method: revolving around other axes." Unit 8 · Applications of Integration.

## The radius is a distance to the axis
**On screen:** The region under y = 4, above y = x², with the axis y = 4 drawn as a thick dashed line. A radius segment at x runs from the curve up to the axis, labeled "R = 4 − x²". Beside it, a wrong label "R = x²" crossed out, with "that's the distance to the x-axis". Board: "R = (farther) − (nearer)", "axis above the curve: R = k − f(x)", "axis below the curve: R = f(x) − k".
> The radius is the distance from the axis to the curve. With the axis at y equals four and the curve at x squared, that distance is four minus x squared. [pause]
> Not x squared. X squared is the distance from the curve down to the x-axis, which isn't the axis anymore. [pause]
> Write the radius as the bigger value minus the smaller one, so it's positive. If the axis is above the curve, it's k minus f. If it's below, f minus k. [long pause]

## Spinning about y = 4
**On screen:** The problem: "The region bounded by y = x² and y = 4 is revolved about the line y = 4. Find the volume." Board, one step per line: x² = 4 at x = ±2; R(x) = 4 − x²; R² = (4 − x²)² = 16 − 8x² + x⁴; V = π∫₋₂² (16 − 8x² + x⁴) dx; = 2π[16x − 8x³/3 + x⁵/5]₀²; = 2π(32 − 64/3 + 32/5); = 512π/15 ≈ 107.23. The picture shows the region, the axis y = 4, a radius, and the solid hanging below the axis.
> The region bounded by x squared and the line y equals four is spun around y equals four. Find the volume. [pause]
> The parabola meets the line at plus or minus two. [pause]
> The radius runs from the curve up to the axis: four minus x squared. [pause]
> Square it: sixteen minus eight x squared plus x to the fourth. [pause]
> The region is symmetric, so integrate from zero to two and double. The antiderivative is sixteen x, minus eight x cubed over three, plus x to the fifth over five. [pause]
> At two that's thirty-two minus sixty-four thirds plus thirty-two fifths. Times two pi: five hundred twelve pi over fifteen, about one hundred seven point two three. [long pause]

## Vertical lines too
**On screen:** The region bounded by x = y² and x = 1, with the vertical axis x = 1 dashed. A horizontal radius at height y runs from the curve to the axis: "R = 1 − y²". The solid spins around x = 1. Board: "vertical axis: horizontal slices, integrate in y"; "R = (axis) − (curve) in x".
> The same idea works for a vertical axis, like x equals one. Slices perpendicular to it are horizontal, so integrate in y. [pause]
> The radius is now a horizontal distance: from the curve x equals y squared over to the axis x equals one. That's one minus y squared. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "V = π∫ R² (d x or d y)"; "R = distance from the axis to the curve: bigger − smaller"; "axis y = k: R = |f(x) − k|"; "axis x = k: R = |g(y) − k|".
> For any horizontal or vertical axis, the disc method is still pi times the integral of R squared. The radius is the distance from the axis to the curve: the bigger value minus the smaller. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: About y = 2
**On screen:** "The region bounded by y = √x, y = 2, and the y-axis is revolved about the line y = 2. Find the volume." Board: √x = 2 at x = 4; R = 2 − √x; R² = 4 − 4√x + x; V = π∫₀⁴ (4 − 4√x + x) dx; = π[4x − (8/3)x^{3/2} + x²/2]₀⁴; = π(16 − 64/3 + 8); = 8π/3 ≈ 8.38.
> The region bounded by root x, the line y equals two, and the y-axis is spun around y equals two. Find the volume. [pause]
> Root x reaches two at x equals four. The radius runs from the curve up to the axis: two minus root x. [pause]
> Square it carefully: four minus four root x plus x. [pause]
> The antiderivative is four x, minus eight thirds x to the three halves, plus x squared over two. At four: sixteen minus sixty-four thirds plus eight. That's eight thirds, so the volume is eight pi over three. [long pause]

## Example 2: About x = 1
**On screen:** "The region bounded by x = y² and x = 1 is revolved about the line x = 1. Find the volume." Board: y² = 1 at y = ±1; R = 1 − y²; R² = 1 − 2y² + y⁴; V = π∫₋₁¹ (1 − 2y² + y⁴) dy; = 2π(1 − ⅔ + ⅕); = 16π/15 ≈ 3.35.
> The region bounded by x equals y squared and the line x equals one is spun around x equals one. Find the volume. [pause]
> The curve meets the line at y equals plus or minus one. The radius is the horizontal distance from the curve to the axis: one minus y squared. [pause]
> Square it: one minus two y squared plus y to the fourth. [pause]
> Twice the integral from zero to one: two pi times one minus two thirds plus one fifth. That's sixteen pi over fifteen. [long pause]

## Example 3: A vertical axis on the right
**On screen:** "The region bounded by y = x², the x-axis, and x = 1 is revolved about the line x = 1. Find the volume." Board: horizontal slices, 0 ≤ y ≤ 1; the curve is x = √y; R = 1 − √y; R² = 1 − 2√y + y; V = π∫₀¹ (1 − 2√y + y) dy; = π(1 − 4/3 + ½); = π/6 ≈ 0.52.
> The region under x squared from zero to one is spun around the line x equals one, its right edge. Find the volume. [pause]
> The axis is vertical, so integrate in y, from zero to one. Write the curve as x equals root y. [pause]
> The radius runs from the curve over to the axis: one minus root y. Squared: one minus two root y plus y. [pause]
> Integrate: one minus four thirds plus one half, which is one sixth. The volume is pi over six. [long pause]
