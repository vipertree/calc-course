# 8.11 Volume with Washer Method: Revolving Around the x- or y-Axis

## A hole in the middle
**On screen:** The region between y = x (top) and y = x² (bottom) on [0, 1], with a gap between the region and the x-axis. It spins about the x-axis, and the solid has a hollow core. One slice is pulled out: a flat ring, like a hardware washer, next to a drawing of an actual metal washer.
> Spin this region around the x-axis. There's a gap between the region and the axis, so the solid comes out hollow in the middle. [pause]
> Slice it, and each slice is a ring with a hole: a washer, like the flat metal ring that goes under a bolt. [pause]
> The volume of a washer is the volume of a disc, minus the volume of the hole. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 8.11 · Volume with washer method: revolving around the x- or y-axis." Unit 8 · Applications of Integration.

## Outer radius and inner radius
**On screen:** The region with one vertical segment at x. Two radii from the axis: a long one to the top curve labeled "R(x), outer" and a short one to the bottom curve labeled "r(x), inner". The washer face: a big circle with a smaller circle cut out. Board: A(x) = πR² − πr² = π(R² − r²); V = π∫ₐᵇ (R(x)² − r(x)²) dx; boxed. Then a warning: "(R − r)² is not R² − r²" with numbers: R = 3, r = 2: (3 − 2)² = 1, but 9 − 4 = 5.
> At each x, measure two radii from the axis. The outer radius, capital R, reaches the curve farther from the axis. The inner radius, lowercase r, reaches the nearer curve. [pause]
> The washer's face is a big circle with a small circle removed: pi R squared minus pi r squared. [pause]
> So the volume is pi times the integral of R squared minus r squared. [pause]
> Square each radius separately. R minus r, squared, is a different number. With radii three and two, it gives one, but the washer's area uses nine minus four, five. [long pause]

## Washers between a line and a parabola
**On screen:** The problem: "The region between y = x and y = x² is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Board, one step per line: x = x² at x = 0, 1; on (0, 1), x > x², so R = x and r = x²; V = π∫₀¹ (x² − (x²)²) dx; = π∫₀¹ (x² − x⁴) dx; = π[x³/3 − x⁵/5]₀¹; = π(⅓ − ⅕); = 2π/15 ≈ 0.42. The picture shows the region, both radii, and the hollow solid.
> The region between y equals x and y equals x squared is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [try it]
> The curves meet at zero and one. Between them, x is bigger than x squared, so the line is farther from the axis. [pause]
> That makes the outer radius x and the inner radius x squared. [pause]
> The volume is pi times the integral of x squared minus x squared squared: x squared minus x to the fourth. [pause]
> The antiderivative is x cubed over three minus x to the fifth over five. From zero to one, that's one third minus one fifth, two fifteenths. [pause]
> The volume is two pi over fifteen, about point four two. [long pause]

## Around the y-axis
**On screen:** The same region, now spun about the y-axis. A horizontal slice at height y with two radii from the y-axis: to x = y (nearer, inner) and to x = √y (farther, outer). Board: in y, the line is x = y and the parabola is x = √y; R = √y, r = y; V = π∫₀¹ (y − y²) dy = π/6.
> Spin the same region about the y-axis. Now the washers are horizontal, so write both curves as x in terms of y: the line is x equals y, the parabola is x equals root y. [pause]
> At a height y between zero and one, root y is bigger than y, so the parabola is farther from the axis this time. Outer radius root y, inner radius y. [pause]
> Pi times the integral of y minus y squared: one half minus one third, so pi over six. Which curve is outer depends on the axis, so always check. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "V = π∫ (R² − r²)"; "R: axis to the farther curve; r: axis to the nearer curve"; "square each radius separately".
> When the region doesn't touch the axis, the slices are washers. The volume is pi times the integral of the outer radius squared minus the inner radius squared. Square each one separately. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Root and line about the x-axis
**On screen:** "The region between y = √x and y = x is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Board: √x = x at x = 0, 1; on (0, 1), √x > x: R = √x, r = x; V = π∫₀¹ (x − x²) dx; = π(½ − ⅓); = π/6 ≈ 0.52.
> The region between root x and the line y equals x is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> The curves meet at zero and one, and in between root x is bigger. Outer radius root x, inner radius x. [pause]
> Squared: x minus x squared. [pause]
> One half minus one third is one sixth. The volume is pi over six. [long pause]

## Example 2: A parabola and a line, about the x-axis
**On screen:** "The region between y = 4 − x² and y = 3 is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Board: 4 − x² = 3 at x = ±1; R = 4 − x², r = 3; R² − r² = (16 − 8x² + x⁴) − 9 = 7 − 8x² + x⁴; V = π∫₋₁¹ (7 − 8x² + x⁴) dx; = 2π(7 − 8/3 + ⅕); = 136π/15 ≈ 28.48.
> The region between four minus x squared and the line y equals three is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> They meet at plus or minus one. The parabola is on top, farther from the axis: outer radius four minus x squared, inner radius three. [pause]
> R squared minus r squared: sixteen minus eight x squared plus x to the fourth, minus nine. That's seven minus eight x squared plus x to the fourth. [pause]
> Twice the integral from zero to one: two pi times seven minus eight thirds plus one fifth. That's one hundred thirty-six pi over fifteen, about twenty-eight point four eight. [long pause]

## Example 3: About the y-axis
**On screen:** "The region between y = 2x and y = x² is revolved about the y-axis. Find the volume." Board: 2x = x² at x = 0, 2, so 0 ≤ y ≤ 4; in y: x = y/2 (line), x = √y (parabola); at y = 1: √1 = 1 > ½, so R = √y, r = y/2; V = π∫₀⁴ (y − y²/4) dy; = π(8 − 16/3); = 8π/3 ≈ 8.38.
> The region between y equals two x and y equals x squared is spun around the y-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> They meet at x equals zero and two, so y runs from zero to four. In terms of y, the line is x equals y over two and the parabola is x equals root y. [pause]
> Test y equals one: root one is one, and one half is smaller. So the parabola is outer: R is root y, r is y over two. [pause]
> Pi times the integral of y minus y squared over four, from zero to four: eight minus sixteen thirds, eight thirds. The volume is eight pi over three. [long pause]
