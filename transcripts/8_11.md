# 8.11 Volume with Washer Method: Revolving Around the x- or y-Axis

## A hollow solid
**On screen:** The line y = x and the parabola y = x² from 0 to 1, drawn flat the way they are on paper, and the region between them shaded. The picture turns so we see it at an angle, and the region swings slowly all the way around the x-axis; everything it passes through stays behind. The solid turns: from the outside ("from the outside") it is a cone with its point at the origin. Then it turns so we look in its open end ("looking in the open end"): the inside is a hollow bowl, its wall drawn darker with rings and lines running down into it, and the x-axis runs through the hollow.
> Here's the region between the line y equals x and the parabola y equals x squared, from zero to one. [pause]
> Spin it around the x-axis. There's a gap between the region and the axis, so the solid it sweeps out is hollow. [pause]
> From the outside, it looks like a cone, with its point at the origin. [pause]
> Look in the open end, and it's hollow inside: a bowl, carved out by the parabola. The x-axis runs right through the hollow. [pause]
> To find its volume, we'll slice it, the same as before. This time, each slice has a hole in it. [long pause]

## What a washer is

> A slice shaped like this is called a washer. [pause]
> Not that kind of washer. This one won't do your laundry. [pause]
> This kind: a flat metal ring that sits under the head of a screw. The screw goes through the hole in the middle. [pause]
> Screw it into a board and look from the side. The head presses on the washer, and the washer spreads that squeeze over a wider ring of wood. [pause]
> Take the screw out and stand the washer on its edge. [pause]
> Our slice has exactly this shape: a disc with a hole in the middle. [pause]

## Every slice is a washer
**On screen:** The slice at an angle; the solid returns as a see-through ghost around it, and two segments are drawn on the slice's face side by side, from the axis out to the outer edge, "R", and from the axis to the edge of the hole, "r". Board: "A(x) = πR² − πr²"; "one washer: π(R² − r²)Δx". The solid is rebuilt from 8 washers, then 16, then 40, then the smooth solid, and the board adds "V = π∫ₐᵇ ([R(x)]² − [r(x)]²) dx", boxed, with "R = distance from the axis to the outer edge" and "r = distance from the axis to the inner edge".
> A washer has two radii. The outer radius, capital R, runs from the axis out to the outer edge. The inner radius, lowercase r, runs from the axis to the edge of the hole. [pause]
> The face is a big circle with a small circle removed: pi R squared minus pi r squared. So one washer's volume is about pi times R squared minus r squared, times delta x. [pause]
> Build the solid out of washers: eight, then sixteen, then forty. Add up infinitely many thin washers, and the volume is pi times the integral of R squared minus r squared. [long pause]

## Square each radius separately
**On screen:** A washer with outer radius 3 and inner radius 2, seen at a slight angle, and below it a small disc of radius 1. Board: "R = 3, r = 2"; "π(3² − 2²) = 5π". Then "π(3 − 2)² = π", "a disc of radius 1", and "(R − r)² ≠ R² − r²".
> One warning. Square each radius on its own, then subtract. [pause]
> With an outer radius of three and an inner radius of two, the washer's face is pi times nine minus four: five pi. [pause]
> Subtracting first gives pi times three minus two, squared: pi. That's the area of a disc of radius one, much smaller than the washer. [long pause]

## Washers between a line and a parabola
**On screen:** The problem: "The region between y = x and y = x² is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Formula card in the corner: "V = π∫ₐᵇ ([R(x)]² − [r(x)]²) dx". The graph is sketched flat as the board fills in: the line and the parabola, the region between them shaded. Board: "sketch: the line, the parabola, the region between"; "x² = x at x = 0 and x = 1". The sketch turns and the region sweeps once around the x-axis into the hollow solid; the solid fades to a see-through ghost and one thin washer at x stands in it. Board: "one slice at x: a washer". On the washer's face, a segment from the axis up to the line, "R", then one from the axis up to the parabola, "r"; the picture turns back to the flat sketch with the region and both radii. Board: "From the axis up to the line, the outer edge:"; "R(x) = x"; "From the axis up to the parabola, the inner edge:"; "r(x) = x²"; "A(x) = π(x² − (x²)²) = π(x² − x⁴)"; "V = π∫₀¹ (x² − x⁴) dx"; "= π[x³/3 − x⁵/5]₀¹"; "= π(⅓ − ⅕)"; "= 2π/15 ≈ 0.42".
> The region between y equals x and y equals x squared is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it first. The line and the parabola both start at the origin, and they meet again where x squared equals x: at x equals one. In between, the line is on top. Shade the region between them. [pause]
> Spin it around the x-axis and it sweeps out this hollow solid. There's a gap between the region and the axis, so one slice at x is a washer. [pause]
> Its outer radius runs from the axis up to the line, the curve farther from the axis. The axis is the x-axis, so that distance is the line's height: x. [pause]
> Its inner radius runs from the axis up to the parabola: x squared. On paper, draw the region and both radii. [pause]
> The washer's area is pi times x squared, minus x squared squared: pi times x squared minus x to the fourth. [pause]
> Integrate from zero to one: x cubed over three minus x to the fifth over five. That's one third minus one fifth, two fifteenths, so the volume is two pi over fifteen, about point four two. [long pause]

## Around the y-axis
**On screen:** The same region, between the line and the parabola from 0 to 1, sketched flat and shaded. It turns and sweeps around the y-axis into a solid with a hollow running up its middle. Board: "horizontal slices: integrate in y"; the solid fades to a ghost and one horizontal washer at height y stands in it; "line: x = y, parabola: x = √y". Then "at y = ¼: the parabola is at ½, the line at ¼, so the parabola is farther out"; on the washer's face, R from the y-axis out to the parabola and r out to the line; "R(y) = √y, r(y) = y". Back to the flat sketch with both radii. Board: "V = π∫₀¹ (y − y²) dy = π/6".
> Now spin the same region, between the line and the parabola, around the y-axis instead. [pause] It sweeps out a solid with a hollow running up its middle, around the axis. [pause]
> Slices perpendicular to this axis are horizontal washers, stacked up the axis, so integrate in y, from zero to one. Write both curves as x in terms of y: the line is x equals y, and the parabola is x equals root y. [pause]
> The radii are now horizontal distances from the y-axis. At a height such as one quarter, the parabola is at one half and the line is at one quarter, so this time the parabola is farther out. Outer radius root y, inner radius y. [pause]
> Pi times the integral of y minus y squared, from zero to one: one half minus one third, so pi over six. Which curve is outer depends on the axis, so find both radii on the picture every time. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "V = π∫ (R² − r²)", boxed; "R: from the axis to the outer edge"; "r: from the axis to the edge of the hole"; "square each radius separately".
> When there's a gap between the region and the axis, the slices are washers. The volume is pi times the integral of the outer radius squared minus the inner radius squared, with both radii measured from the axis. Square each one separately. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Root and line about the x-axis
**On screen:** "The region between y = √x and y = x is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ₐᵇ ([R(x)]² − [r(x)]²) dx". The two curves are sketched and the region between them shaded; it sweeps around the x-axis, the solid fades to a ghost with one thin washer at x, its R (up to √x) and r (up to the line) are drawn on its face, and the picture turns back to the flat sketch. Board: sketch: y = √x, y = x, the region between; √x = x at x = 0 and x = 1; one slice at x: a washer; R(x) = √x; r(x) = x; R² − r² = x − x²; V = π∫₀¹ (x − x²) dx; = π(½ − ⅓); = π/6 ≈ 0.52.
> The region between root x and the line y equals x is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: root x and the line both start at the origin and meet again at x equals one. In between, root x is on top. [pause]
> Spin it around the x-axis. One slice at x is a washer. Its outer radius runs from the axis up to root x, the higher curve, and its inner radius runs up to the line: x. [pause]
> Square each one and subtract: x minus x squared. [pause]
> Integrate from zero to one: one half minus one third, one sixth. The volume is pi over six, about point five two. [long pause]

## Example 2: A napkin ring
**On screen:** "Rowan is turning a napkin ring on a lathe. Its shape is the region between y = 4 − x² and y = 3, revolved about the x-axis (in centimeters). How much wood is in the ring?" Formula card: "V = π∫ₐᵇ ([R(x)]² − [r(x)]²) dx". The parabola and the line y = 3 are sketched, the cap between them shaded; it sweeps around the x-axis into a thick ring, the solid fades to a ghost with one thin washer at x, its R (up to the parabola) and r (up to the line) are drawn on its face, and the picture turns back. Board: sketch: the parabola, the line y = 3, the region between; 4 − x² = 3 at x = ±1; one slice at x: a washer; R(x) = 4 − x²; r(x) = 3; R² − r² = (16 − 8x² + x⁴) − 9; = 7 − 8x² + x⁴; V = π∫₋₁¹ (7 − 8x² + x⁴) dx; = 2π(7 − 8/3 + ⅕); = 136π/15 ≈ 28.48 cm³.
> Rowan is turning a napkin ring on a lathe, and they want to know how much wood is in it. Its shape is the region between four minus x squared and the line y equals three, spun around the x-axis, in centimeters. [pause]
> Sketch it: the parabola peaks at four, and the line y equals three cuts across below the peak. They meet where four minus x squared is three: at plus or minus one. The region is the cap between them. [pause]
> Spin it around the x-axis. There's a gap of three between the region and the axis, so one slice at x is a washer, and that gap is the hole the napkin goes through. [pause]
> The outer radius runs from the axis up to the parabola: four minus x squared. The inner radius runs up to the line: three. [pause]
> Square each one and subtract: sixteen minus eight x squared plus x to the fourth, minus nine. That's seven minus eight x squared plus x to the fourth. [pause]
> The region is symmetric, so take twice the integral from zero to one: two pi times seven minus eight thirds plus one fifth. That's one hundred thirty-six pi over fifteen, about twenty-eight point four eight cubic centimeters of wood. [long pause]

## Example 3: About the y-axis
**On screen:** "The region between y = 2x and y = x² is revolved about the y-axis. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ᶜᵈ ([R(y)]² − [r(y)]²) dy". The line and the parabola are sketched, the region between them shaded; it sweeps around the y-axis, the solid fades to a ghost with one thin horizontal washer at height y, its R (out to the parabola) and r (out to the line) are drawn on its face, and the picture turns back. Board: sketch: the line, the parabola, the region between; 2x = x² at x = 0 and x = 2, so 0 ≤ y ≤ 4; horizontal washers: integrate in y; line: x = y/2, parabola: x = √y; at y = 1: √1 = 1 is farther out than ½; R(y) = √y, r(y) = y/2; V = π∫₀⁴ (y − y²/4) dy; = π(8 − 16/3); = 8π/3 ≈ 8.38.
> The region between y equals two x and y equals x squared is spun around the y-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: they meet where two x equals x squared, at x equals zero and two, so the region runs from y equals zero up to four. Between the crossings, the line is above the parabola. [pause]
> Spin it around the y-axis. The slices are horizontal washers, so integrate in y. Write each curve as x in terms of y: the line is x equals y over two, and the parabola is x equals root y. [pause]
> To see which is farther from the axis, test y equals one: root one is one, and one half is less. So the parabola is the outer edge: R is root y, and r is y over two. [pause]
> Pi times the integral of y minus y squared over four, from zero to four: eight minus sixteen thirds, eight thirds. The volume is eight pi over three, about eight point three eight. [long pause]
