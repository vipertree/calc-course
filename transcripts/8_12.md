# 8.12 Volume with Washer Method: Revolving Around Other Axes

## Moving the axis
**On screen:** The region between y = x and y = x² on [0, 1]. A dashed horizontal axis at y = −1 appears below it. The region spins about that line, making a ring-shaped solid with a wide hole. Then the axis jumps to y = 2, above the region, and the region spins again.
> Here's the region between y equals x and y equals x squared again. This time, spin it around the line y equals negative one. [pause]
> The region doesn't touch that line, so the slices are washers. [pause]
> Everything works as before. The only care needed is in measuring the two radii from the new axis. [long pause]

## Measuring from the axis
**On screen:** The region with the axis y = −1 dashed below it. At x, two vertical segments start at the axis: one up to the line y = x, labeled "R = x − (−1) = x + 1", and one up to the parabola, labeled "r = x² − (−1) = x² + 1". A gap of length 1 between the x-axis and the axis is marked. Board: "radius = (farther value) − (nearer value)", "axis below: curve − k", "axis above: k − curve".
> Every radius starts at the axis. From the line y equals negative one up to the curve y equals x, the distance is x minus negative one: x plus one. [pause]
> Up to the parabola, it's x squared plus one. The line is farther from the axis, so it gives the outer radius. [pause]
> Moving the axis down by one adds one to both radii. In general, each radius is the bigger value minus the smaller one: curve minus k when the axis is below, k minus curve when it's above. [long pause]

## About y = −1
**On screen:** The problem: "The region between y = x and y = x² is revolved about the line y = −1. Find the volume." Board, one step per line: R = x − (−1) = x + 1; r = x² − (−1) = x² + 1; R² = x² + 2x + 1; r² = x⁴ + 2x² + 1; R² − r² = 2x − x² − x⁴; V = π∫₀¹ (2x − x² − x⁴) dx; = π(1 − ⅓ − ⅕); = 7π/15 ≈ 1.47.
> The region between y equals x and y equals x squared is spun around the line y equals negative one. Find the volume. [pause]
> The outer radius goes from the axis up to the line: x plus one. The inner radius goes up to the parabola: x squared plus one. [pause]
> Square each one. X plus one squared is x squared plus two x plus one. X squared plus one squared is x to the fourth plus two x squared plus one. [pause]
> Subtract: the ones cancel, and you're left with two x minus x squared minus x to the fourth. [pause]
> Integrate from zero to one: one minus one third minus one fifth, which is seven fifteenths. The volume is seven pi over fifteen. [long pause]

## An axis above the region
**On screen:** The same region with the axis y = 2 above it. Two segments hang down from the axis: to the parabola (longer), "R = 2 − x²", and to the line (shorter), "r = 2 − x". A note: "now the parabola is farther". Board: V = π∫₀¹ ((2 − x²)² − (2 − x)²) dx = π∫₀¹ (x⁴ − 5x² + 4x) dx = π(⅕ − 5/3 + 2) = 8π/15.
> Put the axis at y equals two, above the region. The radii hang down from the axis. [pause]
> Now the parabola is the farther curve: outer radius two minus x squared, inner radius two minus x. The curves swapped roles because the axis moved to the other side. [pause]
> The integrand simplifies to x to the fourth minus five x squared plus four x, and the volume is eight pi over fifteen. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "V = π∫ (R² − r²)"; "measure both radii from the axis: bigger − smaller"; "which curve is outer depends on where the axis is"; a small sketch reminder: "draw the axis and both radii first".
> For washers about any horizontal or vertical line, measure both radii from the axis, as bigger minus smaller. Which curve is outer depends on where the axis is, so sketch the axis and both radii before you write the integral. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: About x = −1
**On screen:** "The region between y = x and y = x² is revolved about the line x = −1. Find the volume." Board: vertical axis: horizontal washers, 0 ≤ y ≤ 1; line x = y, parabola x = √y; √y is farther from x = −1; R = √y + 1, r = y + 1; R² − r² = (y + 2√y + 1) − (y² + 2y + 1) = 2√y − y − y²; V = π∫₀¹ (2√y − y − y²) dy; = π(4/3 − ½ − ⅓); = π/2.
> Spin the same region around the vertical line x equals negative one. Find the volume. [pause]
> The axis is vertical, so use horizontal washers and integrate in y. The line is x equals y, and the parabola is x equals root y. [pause]
> Root y is farther to the right, so it's farther from the axis. Outer radius root y plus one, inner radius y plus one. [pause]
> R squared minus r squared simplifies to two root y minus y minus y squared. Integrate: four thirds minus one half minus one third, one half. The volume is pi over two. [long pause]

## Example 2: About y = −2
**On screen:** "The region between y = x² and y = 4 is revolved about the line y = −2. Find the volume." Board: x² = 4 at x = ±2; R = 4 − (−2) = 6; r = x² + 2; R² − r² = 36 − (x⁴ + 4x² + 4) = 32 − 4x² − x⁴; V = π∫₋₂² (32 − 4x² − x⁴) dx; = 2π(64 − 32/3 − 32/5); = 1408π/15 ≈ 294.89.
> The region between x squared and the line y equals four is spun around y equals negative two. Find the volume. [pause]
> The outer radius goes from negative two up to four: six. The inner radius goes up to the parabola: x squared plus two. [pause]
> Thirty-six minus x squared plus two squared: thirty-two minus four x squared minus x to the fourth. [pause]
> Twice the integral from zero to two: two pi times sixty-four minus thirty-two thirds minus thirty-two fifths. That's one thousand four hundred eight pi over fifteen, about two hundred ninety-four point eight nine. [long pause]

## Example 3: About y = 3
**On screen:** "The region between y = √x and y = x/2 is revolved about the line y = 3. Find the volume." Board: the curves meet at x = 0, 4; the axis is above; x/2 is lower, so farther: R = 3 − x/2, r = 3 − √x; R² − r² = (9 − 3x + x²/4) − (9 − 6√x + x) = x²/4 − 4x + 6√x; V = π∫₀⁴ (x²/4 − 4x + 6√x) dx; = π(16/3 − 32 + 32); = 16π/3 ≈ 16.76.
> The region between root x and x over two is spun around the line y equals three. Find the volume. [pause]
> The curves meet at zero and four. The axis is above the region, so the lower curve, x over two, is farther from it. Outer radius three minus x over two, inner radius three minus root x. [pause]
> Square and subtract: x squared over four, minus four x, plus six root x. [pause]
> Integrate from zero to four: sixteen thirds minus thirty-two plus thirty-two. The volume is sixteen pi over three. [long pause]
