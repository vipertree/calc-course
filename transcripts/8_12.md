## About y = −1
**On screen:** The problem: "The region between y = x and y = x² is revolved about the line y = −1. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ₐᵇ ([R(x)]² − [r(x)]²) dx". The picture is sketched as the board fills in: the line, the parabola, the region between them shaded, the axis y = −1 dashed below. Board: "sketch: the line, the parabola, the region between; the axis y = −1"; "x² = x at x = 0 and x = 1". The sketch turns and the region sweeps around y = −1; the solid fades to a ghost with one thin washer at x centered on the axis. Board: "one slice at x: a washer centered on y = −1". R is drawn from the axis up to the line, then r up to the parabola, and the picture turns back to the flat sketch with both radii. Board: "Up to the line: 1 to the x-axis, then x more:"; "R(x) = x + 1"; "Up to the parabola:"; "r(x) = x² + 1"; "the axis is 1 lower: both radii are 1 longer"; "R² = x² + 2x + 1"; "r² = x⁴ + 2x² + 1"; "R² − r² = 2x − x² − x⁴"; "V = π∫₀¹ (2x − x² − x⁴) dx"; "= π(1 − ⅓ − ⅕)"; "= 7π/15 ≈ 1.47".
> The region between y equals x and y equals x squared is spun around the line y equals negative one. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: the line and the parabola meet at zero and one, with the line on top. Draw the axis, y equals negative one, below the region. [pause]
> Spin it around that axis, and it sweeps out a solid with a hole running all the way through. One slice at x is a washer centered on the axis. [pause]
> The outer radius runs from the axis up to the line, the curve farther from the axis. It's one unit from the axis up to the x-axis, then x more: x plus one. [pause]
> The inner radius runs from the axis up to the parabola: x squared plus one. Moving the axis down by one made both radii one longer. On paper, draw the region, the axis, and both radii. [pause]
> Square each one. X plus one, squared, is x squared plus two x plus one. X squared plus one, squared, is x to the fourth plus two x squared plus one. [pause]
> Subtract: the ones cancel, leaving two x minus x squared minus x to the fourth. [pause]
> Integrate from zero to one: one minus one third minus one fifth, seven fifteenths. The volume is seven pi over fifteen, about one point four seven. [long pause]

## An axis above the region
**On screen:** The same region with the axis y = 2 dashed above it. It turns and sweeps around y = 2; the solid fades to a ghost around one washer at x centered on that axis. Board: "the axis is above both curves, so each distance down is 2 minus the height". R hangs from the axis down to the parabola: "R(x) = 2 − x²"; r hangs down to the line: "r(x) = 2 − x", and "now the parabola is farther". Back to the flat sketch with both radii. Board: "V = π∫₀¹ ((2 − x²)² − (2 − x)²) dx"; "= π∫₀¹ (x⁴ − 5x² + 4x) dx = 8π/15".
> Now put the axis at y equals two, above the region, and spin it. The hole runs along that line. [pause]
> The radii hang down from the axis. The axis is above both curves, so each distance down is two minus the curve's height. [pause]
> Down to the parabola is two minus x squared. The parabola is lower, so it's farther from the axis: it gives the outer radius this time. [pause]
> Down to the line is two minus x, the inner radius. The curves swapped roles because the axis moved to the other side. [pause]
> The integrand simplifies to x to the fourth minus five x squared plus four x, and the volume is eight pi over fifteen. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "V = π∫ (R² − r²)", boxed; "both radii start at the axis"; "find each on the picture: from the axis to its curve"; "which curve is outer depends on where the axis is".
> For washers about any horizontal or vertical line, both radii start at the axis. Find each one on the picture, from the axis to its curve. Which curve is outer depends on where the axis is, so sketch the axis and both radii before you write the integral. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: About x = −1
**On screen:** "The region between y = x and y = x² is revolved about the line x = −1. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ᶜᵈ ([R(y)]² − [r(y)]²) dy". Sketch: the line, the parabola, the region between them shaded, the axis x = −1 dashed to the left. It sweeps around x = −1, the solid fades to a ghost with one thin horizontal washer at height y, its R (out to the parabola) and r (out to the line) drawn on its face, and the picture turns back. Board: sketch: the line, the parabola, the region; the axis x = −1; vertical axis: horizontal washers, 0 ≤ y ≤ 1; line: x = y, parabola: x = √y; the axis is 1 left of the y-axis, so each radius is 1 more than the curve's x; R(y) = √y + 1, r(y) = y + 1; R² − r² = (y + 2√y + 1) − (y² + 2y + 1); = 2√y − y − y²; V = π∫₀¹ (2√y − y − y²) dy; = π(4/3 − ½ − ⅓); = π/2 ≈ 1.57.
> Spin the region between y equals x and y equals x squared around the vertical line x equals negative one. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: the region between the line and the parabola, from the origin to the point one, one, and the axis, x equals negative one, off to its left. [pause]
> The axis is vertical, so the washers are horizontal and we integrate in y, from zero to one. In terms of y, the line is x equals y, and the parabola is x equals root y. [pause]
> The axis is one unit left of the y-axis, so each radius is one more than the curve's x. Root y is farther right, so it gives the outer radius: R is root y plus one, and r is y plus one. [pause]
> Square each one and subtract. The ones cancel, and y minus two y leaves negative y, so R squared minus r squared is two root y minus y minus y squared. [pause]
> Integrate: four thirds minus one half minus one third, which is one half. The volume is pi over two, about one point five seven. [long pause]

## Example 2: About y = −2
**On screen:** "The region between y = x² and y = 4 is revolved about the line y = −2. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ₐᵇ ([R(x)]² − [r(x)]²) dx". Sketch: the parabola, the line y = 4, the region between them shaded, the axis y = −2 dashed below. It sweeps around y = −2 into a wide drum with a hole, the solid fades to a ghost with one thin washer at x, its R (up to the line y = 4) and r (up to the parabola) drawn on its face, and the picture turns back. Board: sketch: the parabola, the line y = 4, the region; the axis y = −2; x² = 4 at x = ±2; one slice at x: a washer centered on y = −2; the axis is 2 below the x-axis, so each radius is the height plus 2; R(x) = 4 + 2 = 6, r(x) = x² + 2; R² − r² = 36 − (x⁴ + 4x² + 4); = 32 − 4x² − x⁴; V = π∫₋₂² (32 − 4x² − x⁴) dx; = 2π(64 − 32/3 − 32/5); = 1408π/15 ≈ 294.89.
> The region between y equals x squared and y equals four is spun around the line y equals negative two. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: the parabola and the line y equals four meet at plus or minus two, and the region is between them. The axis, y equals negative two, is below everything. [pause]
> Spin it around that axis, and it sweeps out a wide drum with a hole through the middle. One slice at x is a washer centered on the axis. [pause]
> The axis is two units below the x-axis, so each radius is the curve's height plus two. Up to the line y equals four, the farther one: six. Up to the parabola: x squared plus two. [pause]
> Thirty-six, minus x squared plus two, squared: thirty-two minus four x squared minus x to the fourth. [pause]
> Twice the integral from zero to two: two pi times sixty-four minus thirty-two thirds minus thirty-two fifths. That's one thousand four hundred eight pi over fifteen, about two hundred ninety-four point eight nine. [long pause]

## Example 3: About y = 3
**On screen:** "The region between y = √x and y = x/2 is revolved about the line y = 3. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ₐᵇ ([R(x)]² − [r(x)]²) dx". Sketch: the two curves, the region between them shaded, the axis y = 3 dashed above. It sweeps around y = 3, the solid fades to a ghost with one thin washer at x, its R (down to x/2) and r (down to √x) drawn on its face, and the picture turns back. Board: sketch: y = √x, y = x/2, the region; the axis y = 3; √x = x/2 at x = 0 and x = 4; one slice at x: a washer centered on y = 3; the axis is above both curves, so each distance down is 3 minus the height; R(x) = 3 − x/2, r(x) = 3 − √x; R² − r² = (9 − 3x + x²/4) − (9 − 6√x + x); = x²/4 − 4x + 6√x; V = π∫₀⁴ (x²/4 − 4x + 6√x) dx; = π(16/3 − 32 + 32); = 16π/3 ≈ 16.76.
> The region between y equals root x and y equals x over two is spun around the line y equals three. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: root x and x over two meet where root x equals x over two, at zero and four, and root x is on top. The axis, y equals three, is above the region. [pause]
> Spin it around y equals three, and the solid's hole runs along that line. One slice at x is a washer centered on the axis. [pause]
> The axis is above both curves, so each distance down is three minus the curve's height. The lower curve, x over two, is farther from the axis: R is three minus x over two, and r is three minus root x. [pause]
> Square each one and subtract: x squared over four, minus four x, plus six root x. [pause]
> Integrate from zero to four: sixteen thirds minus thirty-two plus thirty-two. The volume is sixteen pi over three, about sixteen point seven six. [long pause]
