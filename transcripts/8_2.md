# 8.2 Connecting Position, Velocity, and Acceleration of Functions Using Integrals

## Out and back
**On screen:** A car on a straight road with a mile marker at 0. It drives 30 miles east, then 10 miles back west. A trip odometer counts 30, then 40. A "position" arrow from 0 ends at 20. Labels: "displacement 20 miles", "distance 40 miles".
> A car drives thirty miles east, then turns around and drives ten miles back. [pause]
> Where did it end up? Twenty miles east of where it started. That's the displacement. [pause]
> How far did it drive? Forty miles. That's the total distance. Integrals can give us both, but they're different integrals. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 8.2 · Connecting position, velocity, and acceleration using integrals." Unit 8 · Applications of Integration.

## Displacement is the integral of velocity
**On screen:** A velocity graph v(t) on [a, b], positive then negative. Area above the axis shaded and marked "+", area below marked "−". Board: ∫ₐᵇ v(t) dt = s(b) − s(a); "displacement: net change in position". Plain words: "where you end up, relative to where you started".
> Velocity is the derivative of position. So by the Fundamental Theorem, the integral of velocity from a to b is s of b minus s of a. [pause]
> That's the displacement: the net change in position. [pause]
> On the graph, area above the axis counts as forward motion and area below counts as backward motion, and they cancel. Thirty forward, ten back: twenty. [long pause]

## Position from a starting point
**On screen:** Board: s(t) = s(0) + ∫₀ᵗ v(u) du; "where you start" under s(0), "how far you've moved since" under the integral. Then the same pattern with velocity: v(t) = v(0) + ∫₀ᵗ a(u) du.
> To find position, not just change in position, you need a starting point. [pause]
> Position at time t is where you started, plus the net change since: s of zero plus the integral from zero to t of v. [pause]
> The same pattern works one level down. Acceleration is the derivative of velocity, so velocity at time t is v of zero plus the integral of acceleration. [long pause]

## Total distance: integrate speed
**On screen:** The same velocity graph. The part below the axis flips up (absolute value) and turns the same color as the rest. Board: total distance = ∫ₐᵇ |v(t)| dt. Then a number line for v with the zero marked, signs + and −, and the split: ∫ over the + piece minus ∫ over the − piece.
> Total distance doesn't cancel. Backward motion still adds to the odometer. [pause]
> So integrate speed, the absolute value of velocity. Total distance is the integral of absolute v. [pause]
> Without a calculator, you can't integrate absolute value directly. Instead, find where v changes sign, split the interval there, and add the pieces as positive numbers. [pause]
> That means a sign chart for v, every time. [long pause]

## Displacement and distance
**On screen:** The problem: "A particle moves along a line with v(t) = t² − 6t + 8 for 0 ≤ t ≤ 5, and s(0) = 1. Find (a) the displacement, (b) the position at t = 5, (c) the total distance." Board, one step per line: (a) ∫₀⁵ (t² − 6t + 8) dt; = [t³/3 − 3t² + 8t]₀⁵; = 125/3 − 75 + 40; = 20/3; (b) s(5) = 1 + 20/3 = 23/3; (c) v = (t − 2)(t − 4); zeros t = 2, t = 4; sign chart; ∫₀² v dt = 20/3; ∫₂⁴ v dt = −4/3; ∫₄⁵ v dt = 4/3; distance = 20/3 + 4/3 + 4/3 = 28/3.
> A particle moves along a line with velocity t squared minus six t plus eight, from time zero to five, starting at position one. Find the displacement, the position at time five, and the total distance. [try it]
> Displacement first: the integral of v from zero to five. The antiderivative is t cubed over three, minus three t squared, plus eight t. [pause]
> At five: one twenty-five thirds, minus seventy-five, plus forty. That's twenty thirds. At zero it's zero. So the displacement is twenty thirds. [pause]
> Position at five is the start plus the displacement: one plus twenty thirds, twenty-three thirds. [pause]
> For total distance, find where v changes sign. Factor: t minus two, times t minus four. The zeros are two and four. [pause]
> Test a value in each piece. At one: one minus six plus eight, three, positive. At three: nine minus eighteen plus eight, negative one. At five: twenty-five minus thirty plus eight, three, positive. [pause]
> Now integrate each piece. From zero to two: twenty thirds. From two to four: negative four thirds. From four to five: four thirds. [pause]
> Add them as positive numbers: twenty thirds plus four thirds plus four thirds, twenty-eight thirds. More than the displacement, because the particle backed up for a while. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "displacement = ∫ v dt (can cancel)"; "total distance = ∫ |v| dt (never cancels): split where v changes sign"; "s(t) = s(0) + ∫₀ᵗ v"; "v(t) = v(0) + ∫₀ᵗ a".
> Integrate velocity for displacement, and absolute velocity for total distance, splitting where v changes sign. Position is the starting position plus the integral of velocity, and velocity is the starting velocity plus the integral of acceleration. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: From a velocity graph
**On screen:** A velocity graph on [0, 8] made of segments: from (0, 0) up to (2, 4), level to (4, 4), down through (5, 0) to (6, −2), level to (8, −2). The problem: "Find the displacement and the total distance on [0, 8]." Board: area above = ½(2)(4) + 2(4) + ½(1)(4) = 4 + 8 + 2 = 14; area below = ½(1)(2) + 2(2) = 1 + 4 = 5; displacement = 14 − 5 = 9; distance = 14 + 5 = 19.
> Here's a velocity graph made of line segments. Find the displacement and the total distance from zero to eight. [pause]
> Above the axis: a triangle of area four, a rectangle of area eight, and a triangle of area two. Fourteen. [pause]
> Below the axis: a triangle of area one and a rectangle of area four. Five. [pause]
> Displacement subtracts: fourteen minus five, nine. Total distance adds: nineteen. [long pause]

## Example 2: From acceleration
**On screen:** "A particle has a(t) = 6t and v(0) = −3. Find v(t), then the total distance on [0, 2]." Board: v(t) = −3 + ∫₀ᵗ 6u du; = 3t² − 3; zero at t = 1 (t = −1 is outside); sign chart: − on (0, 1), + on (1, 2); ∫₀¹ v = [t³ − 3t]₀¹ = −2; ∫₁² v = 2 − (−2) = 4; distance = 2 + 4 = 6.
> A particle has acceleration six t and starting velocity negative three. Find the velocity, then the total distance from zero to two. [pause]
> Velocity is the start plus the integral of acceleration: negative three plus three t squared. [pause]
> It's zero when t squared is one. t equals one is in the interval; negative one is not. Before one, v is negative; after one, positive. [pause]
> From zero to one, the integral is negative two. From one to two, it's four. Total distance: two plus four, six. [long pause]

## Example 3: Working backward
**On screen:** "A particle has v(t) = 3t² − 2, and s(2) = 5. Find s(0)." Board: s(2) = s(0) + ∫₀² (3t² − 2) dt; 5 = s(0) + [t³ − 2t]₀²; 5 = s(0) + 4; s(0) = 1.
> A particle has velocity three t squared minus two, and at time two it's at position five. Where was it at time zero? [pause]
> Position at two equals position at zero plus the integral from zero to two. [pause]
> The integral is eight minus four, four. So five equals s of zero plus four. [pause]
> It started at position one. [long pause]
