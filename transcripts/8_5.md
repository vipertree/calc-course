# 8.5 Finding the Area Between Curves Expressed as Functions of y

## Turning the slices sideways
**On screen:** A region whose left edge is a sideways parabola x = y² and whose right edge is a slanted line. Vertical slices are drawn first, and the top of each slice is sometimes the parabola and sometimes the line; they are marked with question marks. Then the slices rotate to horizontal and every one runs cleanly from the parabola on the left to the line on the right.
> Some regions are awkward to slice vertically. Here, a vertical slice sometimes starts on one curve and sometimes on another. [pause]
> Turn the slices sideways, and every slice runs from the same left curve to the same right curve. [pause]
> Horizontal slices have thickness d y, so we integrate with respect to y. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 8.5 · Finding the area between curves expressed as functions of y." Unit 8 · Applications of Integration.

## Right minus left
**On screen:** A region between x = f(y) (right) and x = g(y) (left) for c ≤ y ≤ d. One horizontal rectangle at height y, its length marked "f(y) − g(y)" and its thickness marked with a small double arrow "dy". Board: area = ∫꜀ᵈ (f(y) − g(y)) dy; boxed: "∫ (right − left) dy". A small note: "write each curve as x = (something in y)".
> A horizontal slice at height y reaches from the left curve to the right curve. Its length is right minus left, and its thickness is d y. [pause]
> So the area is the integral from c to d of right minus left, d y. The limits are y values now: the bottom and top of the region. [pause]
> To use this, each curve has to be written as x equals something in y. Solve for x if you need to. [long pause]

## Choosing dy
**On screen:** The region bounded by y = √x, y = x − 2, and y = 0. Left: vertical slices, with a dashed line at x = 2 where the bottom boundary switches from the x-axis to the line; board: ∫₀⁴ √x dx − ∫₂⁴ (x − 2) dx = 16/3 − 2 = 10/3. Right: horizontal slices from x = y² to x = y + 2; board: ∫₀² (y + 2 − y²) dy = 2 + 4 − 8/3 = 10/3. Both labeled "same area".
> Here's a region bounded by root x, the line y equals x minus two, and the x-axis. [pause]
> With vertical slices, the bottom switches from the x-axis to the line at x equals two, so you need two integrals: sixteen thirds minus two, ten thirds. [pause]
> With horizontal slices, solve each curve for x: x equals y squared on the left, x equals y plus two on the right. One integral from zero to two gives two plus four minus eight thirds: ten thirds again. [pause]
> Choose d y when the left and right boundaries stay the same but the top or bottom changes, or when the curves are already given as x in terms of y. [long pause]

## A parabola and a line, sideways
**On screen:** The problem: "Find the area of the region bounded by x = y² − 4 and x = 2y − 1." Board, one step per line: y² − 4 = 2y − 1; y² − 2y − 3 = 0; (y − 3)(y + 1) = 0; y = −1, y = 3; test y = 0: line −1, parabola −4, so the line is on the right; A = ∫₋₁³ ((2y − 1) − (y² − 4)) dy; = ∫₋₁³ (−y² + 2y + 3) dy; = [−y³/3 + y² + 3y]₋₁³; = (−9 + 9 + 9) − (1/3 + 1 − 3); = 9 − (−5/3); = 32/3. The graph shows the sideways parabola, the line, and a horizontal slice.
> Find the area of the region bounded by x equals y squared minus four and x equals two y minus one. [try it]
> Both curves are already x in terms of y, so use horizontal slices. Where do they meet? Set y squared minus four equal to two y minus one. [pause]
> That's y squared minus two y minus three equals zero, which factors as y minus three, times y plus one. So y is negative one or three. [pause]
> Which is on the right? Test y equals zero: the line gives negative one, the parabola gives negative four. Negative one is farther right, so the line is on the right. [pause]
> The area is the integral from negative one to three of the line minus the parabola. Simplify: negative y squared plus two y plus three. [pause]
> The antiderivative is negative y cubed over three, plus y squared, plus three y. [pause]
> At three: negative nine plus nine plus nine, which is nine. At negative one: one third plus one minus three, which is negative five thirds. [pause]
> Nine minus negative five thirds is thirty-two thirds. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "Area = ∫꜀ᵈ (right − left) dy"; "limits: y values"; "use dy when the left and right curves don't change".
> With horizontal slices, the area is the integral of right minus left, d y, with y limits. Use it when the left and right boundaries stay the same all the way up. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Against the y-axis
**On screen:** "Find the area between x = 4 − y² and the y-axis." Board: the y-axis is x = 0; 4 − y² = 0 at y = ±2; A = ∫₋₂² ((4 − y²) − 0) dy; = [4y − y³/3]₋₂²; = (8 − 8/3) − (−8 + 8/3); = 32/3.
> Find the area between x equals four minus y squared and the y-axis. [pause]
> The y-axis is the line x equals zero. The curve meets it where four minus y squared is zero: y is plus or minus two. [pause]
> The curve is on the right, so integrate four minus y squared from negative two to two. [pause]
> Four y minus y cubed over three: at two, sixteen thirds; at negative two, negative sixteen thirds. The area is thirty-two thirds. [long pause]

## Example 2: Solve for x first
**On screen:** "Find the area of the region bounded by y = ln x, the y-axis, y = 0, and y = 1." Board: y = ln x, so x = eʸ; left: x = 0, right: x = eʸ; A = ∫₀¹ eʸ dy; = [eʸ]₀¹; = e − 1 ≈ 1.72.
> Find the area of the region bounded by y equals ln x, the y-axis, and the lines y equals zero and y equals one. [pause]
> Solve for x: y equals ln x means x equals e to the y. [pause]
> Each horizontal slice runs from the y-axis to e to the y, from y equals zero to one. [pause]
> The integral of e to the y from zero to one is e minus one, about one point seven two. [long pause]

## Example 3: Two sideways parabolas
**On screen:** "Find the area between x = y² and x = 2 − y²." Board: y² = 2 − y²; 2y² = 2; y = ±1; test y = 0: 2 − y² gives 2, y² gives 0, so x = 2 − y² is on the right; A = ∫₋₁¹ (2 − 2y²) dy; = [2y − ⅔y³]₋₁¹; = 4/3 − (−4/3); = 8/3.
> Find the area between x equals y squared and x equals two minus y squared. [pause]
> Set them equal: two y squared equals two, so y is plus or minus one. [pause]
> At y equals zero, two minus y squared is two and y squared is zero. So two minus y squared is on the right. [pause]
> Integrate two minus two y squared from negative one to one: four thirds minus negative four thirds, eight thirds. [long pause]
