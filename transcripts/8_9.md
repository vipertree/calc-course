# 8.9 Volume with Disc Method: Revolving Around the x- or y-Axis

## Spinning a region
**On screen:** The graph of y = √x from 0 to 4, drawn flat the way it is on paper, and the region under it shaded. The picture turns so we see it at an angle. The region swings slowly all the way around the x-axis, swinging on the axis as a door swings on its hinge, and everything it passes through stays behind, so a solid builds up around the axis. The finished solid turns slowly.
> Here's a flat region: everything under the graph of root x, from zero to four. [pause]
> Spin it all the way around the x-axis. Everything the region passes through stays behind, and it sweeps out a solid. [pause]
> Solids made this way are called solids of revolution. [pause]
> To find their volume, we'll slice them, the same way we sliced solids in the last two topics. [long pause]

## One big solid
**On screen:** A new region: under a wavy curve y = f(x) from 0 to 4, drawn flat and shaded. The picture turns to an angled view. The region sweeps around the x-axis a quarter turn at a time, stopping each time, with "a quarter turn", "half", "all the way around" beside it; the part already swept stays. > Watch that again, slower, with a different region: the region under a curve, from zero to four. [pause]
> Turn it a quarter of the way around the x-axis. The part it has swept through stays. [pause]
> Now halfway around. [pause] And all the way around. [pause]
> Look at the whole thing as one solid. From the side, its outline is the curve, and the curve's mirror image below the axis. [pause]
> From the end, looking straight down the axis, it's round. Every point of the region traveled in a circle around the axis. [long pause]

## Every slice is a disc
**On screen:** The same solid at an angle. A ring is drawn around it at one x, and the solid is cut there: the two pieces slide apart, leaving a thin slice. The pieces fade, and the slice turns: face on, it is a circle; edge on, it is thin, with its thickness marked "Δx". Back at an angle, the solid returns as a see-through ghost around the slice, and a segment from the axis up to the curve at x is drawn on the slice's face: "R". Board: "A(x) = π[R(x)]²"; "volume of one disc ≈ π[R(x)]² Δx". The solid is rebuilt from 8 discs, then 16, then 40, then the smooth solid, and the board adds "V = π∫ₐᵇ [R(x)]² dx", boxed, with "R = distance from the axis to the curve".
> Now slice the solid perpendicular to the axis, at one value of x. [pause]
> Pull the two pieces apart and look at the slice on its own. [pause] Face on, it's a circle. [pause] Edge on, it's thin, with thickness delta x. It's a disc, shaped like a coin. [pause]
> The radius of the disc runs from the axis out to the curve. On the flat picture, that's the segment from the x-axis up to the curve at x. Call it R of x. [pause]
> The face of the disc has area pi R squared, so the disc's volume is about pi R squared delta x. [pause]
> Build the solid out of discs: eight, then sixteen, then forty. Add up infinitely many thin discs, and the volume is pi times the integral of R of x squared. [pause]
> Same method as the last two topics. The cross sections just happen to be circles. [long pause]

## Discs on a root
**On screen:** The problem: "The region under y = √x for 0 ≤ x ≤ 4 is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Formula card in the corner: "V = π∫ₐᵇ [R(x)]² dx". The graph is sketched flat as the board fills in: y = √x from the origin, the line x = 4, the region shaded. Board: "the region: under y = √x, from x = 0 to x = 4". The sketch turns to an angled view and the region sweeps once around the x-axis into a solid; the solid fades to a see-through ghost and one disc at x stands in it. Board: "one slice at x: a disc". A segment from the axis up to the curve is drawn on the disc's face, "R"; the picture turns back to the flat sketch, with the region and that one radius. Board: "R(x) = √x − 0 = √x"; "A(x) = π(√x)² = πx"; "V = π∫₀⁴ x dx"; "= π[x²/2]₀⁴"; "= 8π ≈ 25.13".
> The region under root x from zero to four is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it first. Root x starts at the origin and rises, bending over. The region is everything under it, down to the x-axis, out to x equals four. [pause]
> Spin the region around the x-axis and it sweeps out this solid. We only need one slice, at x. It's a disc. [pause]
> Its radius runs from the axis, zero, up to the curve, root x. So R is root x. On paper, draw the region and that one radius. [pause]
> The disc's area is pi times root x squared: pi x. [pause]
> Integrate from zero to four: pi times x squared over two, from zero to four. That's eight pi, about twenty-five point one three. [long pause]

## A check with a cone
**On screen:** The line y = x/2 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 6, sketched flat with the triangle under it shaded. It turns and sweeps around the x-axis into a cone lying on its side, height 6 along the axis and radius 3 at the open end. Board: V = π∫₀⁶ (x/2)² dx = π · 216/12 = 18π; geometry: ⅓πr²h = ⅓π(3²)(6) = 18π. A check mark.
> Here's a way to trust the formula. Spin the line y equals x over two, from zero to six, around the x-axis. That makes a cone of height six and radius three. [pause]
> The disc method gives pi times the integral of x squared over four: eighteen pi. [pause]
> Geometry says a cone has volume one third pi r squared h: one third pi times nine times six, eighteen pi. Same answer. [long pause]

## Around the y-axis
**On screen:** The region bounded by y = x², y = 4, and the y-axis, in the first quadrant, sketched flat and shaded. It turns and sweeps around the y-axis into a bowl-shaped solid. The solid fades to a ghost and one horizontal disc at height y stands in it; its radius, from the y-axis out to the curve, is drawn on the disc: "R". Back to the flat sketch with the radius drawn. Board: "horizontal slices: integrate in y"; "x = √y, R(y) = √y"; "V = π∫₀⁴ (√y)² dy = π∫₀⁴ y dy = 8π". Last: "slice perpendicular to the axis; integrate along it".
> Now spin around the y-axis instead. Here's the region bounded by y equals x squared, the line y equals four, and the y-axis. [pause]
> Spin it around the y-axis and it makes a bowl shape. Slices perpendicular to this axis are horizontal discs, stacked up the axis, so we integrate with respect to y. [pause]
> The radius of the slice at height y runs from the y-axis out to the curve, so it's an x distance, written in terms of y. The curve is y equals x squared, so x is root y. [pause]
> The volume is pi times the integral of root y squared from zero to four: pi times the integral of y, eight pi. [pause]
> The rule: the slices are perpendicular to the axis of revolution, and the variable is the one along that axis. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "V = π∫ R² dx (axis horizontal)" and "V = π∫ R² dy (axis vertical)"; "R = distance from the axis to the curve"; "slice perpendicular to the axis".
> Revolving a region makes discs. The volume is pi times the integral of the radius squared, where the radius is the distance from the axis to the curve. Integrate along the axis: d x for a horizontal axis, d y for a vertical one. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A parabola about the x-axis
**On screen:** "The region under y = x² for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2 is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ₐᵇ [R(x)]² dx". The parabola and the line x = 2 are sketched, the region shaded; it sweeps around the x-axis, the solid fades to a ghost with one disc at x and its radius R, and the picture turns back to the flat sketch. Board: the region: under y = x², from 0 to 2; one slice at x: a disc; R = x²; V = π∫₀² (x²)² dx; = π∫₀² x⁴ dx; = π[x⁵/5]₀²; = 32π/5 ≈ 20.11.
> The region under x squared from zero to two is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: the parabola from the origin up to the point two, four, and the region under it. [pause]
> Spin it around the x-axis and it sweeps out a solid. We only need one slice, at x. It's a disc, and its radius runs from the axis, zero, up to the curve, x squared. [pause]
> Square the radius: x to the fourth. [pause]
> Pi times x to the fifth over five, from zero to two: thirty-two pi over five, about twenty point one one. [long pause]

## Example 2: An exponential about the x-axis
**On screen:** "The region under y = eˣ for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1 is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ₐᵇ [R(x)]² dx". The curve and the lines x = 0 and x = 1 are sketched, the region shaded; it sweeps around the x-axis, the solid fades to a ghost with one disc at x and its radius R, and the picture turns back. Board: the region: under y = eˣ, from 0 to 1; one slice at x: a disc; R = eˣ, R² = e²ˣ; V = π∫₀¹ e²ˣ dx; = π[½e²ˣ]₀¹; = (π/2)(e² − 1) ≈ 10.04.
> The region under e to the x from zero to one is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: e to the x crosses the y-axis at one and climbs to e at x equals one. The region is under it, between zero and one. [pause]
> Spin it around the x-axis and it sweeps out a solid that flares out to the right. We only need one slice, at x. It's a disc, and its radius runs from the axis up to the curve, e to the x. Squared, that's e to the two x. [pause]
> Its antiderivative is one half e to the two x. From zero to one: pi over two times e squared minus one, about ten point zero four. [long pause]

## Example 3: A cubic about the y-axis
**On screen:** "The region bounded by y = x³, y = 8, and the y-axis is revolved about the y-axis. Find the volume." Formula card: "V = π∫ᶜᵈ [R(y)]² dy". The cubic and the line y = 8 are sketched, the region between them and the y-axis shaded; it sweeps around the y-axis, the solid fades to a ghost with one horizontal disc at height y and its radius R, and the picture turns back. Board: horizontal discs, integrate in y from 0 to 8; x = y^{1/3}, so R = y^{1/3}; V = π∫₀⁸ y^{2/3} dy; = π[⅗y^{5/3}]₀⁸; = π · ⅗ · 32; = 96π/5 ≈ 60.32.
> The region bounded by x cubed, the line y equals eight, and the y-axis is spun around the y-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> Sketch it: x cubed rises from the origin and reaches eight at x equals two. The region is between the curve and the y-axis, below the line y equals eight. [pause]
> The axis is vertical, so the discs are horizontal and we integrate in y, from zero to eight. The radius of the slice at height y runs from the y-axis to the curve. Solve for x: x is the cube root of y. That's the radius. [pause]
> Squared, it's y to the two thirds. Its antiderivative is three fifths y to the five thirds. [pause]
> Eight to the five thirds is thirty-two. So the volume is pi times three fifths times thirty-two: ninety-six pi over five, about sixty point three two. [long pause]
