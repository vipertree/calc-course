# 8.9 Volume with Disc Method: Revolving Around the x- or y-Axis

## Spinning a region
**On screen:** The region under y = √x from 0 to 4, lying flat. It spins around the x-axis; a ghosted copy sweeps around and leaves behind a solid shaped like the nose of a bullet. A potter's wheel turning a lump of clay into a vase appears beside it for a moment.
> Take a flat region and spin it around a line, like clay on a potter's wheel. It sweeps out a solid. [pause]
> Solids made this way are called solids of revolution. [pause]
> And you already know how to find their volume: slice them, and the slices are circles. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 8.9 · Volume with disc method: revolving around the x- or y-axis." Unit 8 · Applications of Integration.

## Every slice is a disc
**On screen:** The bullet-shaped solid with one slice at x pulled out: a thin disc. On the flat region, the vertical segment from the axis up to the curve is marked "radius R(x)", and it sweeps around to make the disc's face. Board: A(x) = π[R(x)]²; V = π∫ₐᵇ [R(x)]² dx; boxed, with the plain-words version "the radius is the distance from the axis to the curve".
> Slice the solid perpendicular to the axis. Each slice is a thin disc, like a coin. [pause]
> The radius of the disc is the distance from the axis out to the curve: on this picture, the segment from the x-axis up to the curve. Call it R of x. [pause]
> The disc's face has area pi R squared. So by the cross-section method, the volume is pi times the integral of R of x squared. [pause]
> Same method as last topic. The cross sections just happen to be circles. [long pause]

## Discs on a root
**On screen:** The problem: "The region under y = √x for 0 ≤ x ≤ 4 is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Board, one step per line: R(x) = √x − 0 = √x; A(x) = π(√x)² = πx; V = π∫₀⁴ x dx; = π[x²/2]₀⁴; = 8π ≈ 25.13. Picture: the region, a radius segment, and the solid.
> The region under root x from zero to four is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> The radius runs from the axis, zero, up to the curve, root x. So R is root x. [pause]
> The disc's area is pi times root x squared: pi x. [pause]
> Integrate from zero to four: pi times x squared over two, eight pi, about twenty-five point one three. [long pause]

## A check with a cone
**On screen:** The line y = x/2 for 0 ≤ x ≤ 6 revolved about the x-axis, making a cone with height 6 and base radius 3. Board: V = π∫₀⁶ (x/2)² dx = π∫₀⁶ x²/4 dx = π · 216/12 = 18π; geometry: ⅓πr²h = ⅓π(3²)(6) = 18π. A check mark.
> Here's a way to trust the formula. Spin the line y equals x over two, from zero to six, around the x-axis. That makes a cone of height six and radius three. [pause]
> The disc method gives pi times the integral of x squared over four: eighteen pi. [pause]
> Geometry says a cone has volume one third pi r squared h: one third pi times nine times six, eighteen pi. Same answer. [long pause]

## Around the y-axis
**On screen:** The region bounded by y = x², y = 4, and the y-axis, in the first quadrant. It spins about the y-axis to make a bowl-shaped solid. A horizontal slice at height y is a disc; its radius runs from the y-axis out to the curve. Board: slices are horizontal, so integrate in y; x = √y, so R(y) = √y; V = π∫₀⁴ (√y)² dy = π∫₀⁴ y dy = 8π.
> Now spin around the y-axis instead. The discs are horizontal, stacked up the axis, so integrate with respect to y. [pause]
> The radius runs from the y-axis out to the curve, so it's an x distance, written in terms of y. Here the curve is y equals x squared, so x is root y. [pause]
> The volume is pi times the integral of root y squared from zero to four: pi times the integral of y, eight pi. [pause]
> The rule: the slices are perpendicular to the axis of revolution, and the variable is the one along that axis. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "V = π∫ R² dx (axis horizontal)" and "V = π∫ R² dy (axis vertical)"; "R = distance from the axis to the curve"; "slice perpendicular to the axis".
> Revolving a region makes discs. The volume is pi times the integral of the radius squared, where the radius is the distance from the axis to the curve. Integrate along the axis: d x for a horizontal axis, d y for a vertical one. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A parabola about the x-axis
**On screen:** "The region under y = x² for 0 ≤ x ≤ 2 is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Board: R = x²; V = π∫₀² (x²)² dx; = π∫₀² x⁴ dx; = π[x⁵/5]₀²; = 32π/5 ≈ 20.11.
> The region under x squared from zero to two is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> The radius is x squared, so its square is x to the fourth. [pause]
> Pi times x to the fifth over five, from zero to two: thirty-two pi over five, about twenty point one one. [long pause]

## Example 2: An exponential about the x-axis
**On screen:** "The region under y = eˣ for 0 ≤ x ≤ 1 is revolved about the x-axis. Find the volume." Board: R = eˣ; R² = e²ˣ; V = π∫₀¹ e²ˣ dx; = π[½e²ˣ]₀¹; = (π/2)(e² − 1) ≈ 10.04.
> The region under e to the x from zero to one is spun around the x-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> The radius is e to the x. Squared, that's e to the two x. [pause]
> Its antiderivative is one half e to the two x. From zero to one: pi over two times e squared minus one, about ten point zero four. [long pause]

## Example 3: A cubic about the y-axis
**On screen:** "The region bounded by y = x³, y = 8, and the y-axis is revolved about the y-axis. Find the volume." Board: horizontal discs, integrate in y from 0 to 8; x = y^{1/3}, so R = y^{1/3}; V = π∫₀⁸ y^{2/3} dy; = π[⅗y^{5/3}]₀⁸; = π · ⅗ · 32; = 96π/5 ≈ 60.32.
> The region bounded by x cubed, the line y equals eight, and the y-axis is spun around the y-axis. Find the volume. [pause]
> The axis is vertical, so the discs are horizontal and we integrate in y, from zero to eight. Solve for x: x is the cube root of y. That's the radius. [pause]
> Squared, it's y to the two thirds. Its antiderivative is three fifths y to the five thirds. [pause]
> Eight to the five thirds is thirty-two. So the volume is pi times three fifths times thirty-two: ninety-six pi over five, about sixty point three two. [long pause]
