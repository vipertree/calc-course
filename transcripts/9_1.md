# 9.1 Defining and Differentiating Parametric Equations

## A path with a clock
**On screen:** A ladybug walking across a grid, leaving a curved trail that crosses itself. A clock in the corner ticks t = 0, 1, 2, 3, and at each tick the bug's position is stamped on the trail: (x(t), y(t)). Labels: "x depends on t", "y depends on t".
> A ladybug walks across a table. To describe its path, you could try to write y as a function of x. But the path loops back across itself, so it fails the vertical line test. [pause]
> Instead, give each coordinate its own formula in terms of time: x of t and y of t. [pause]
> Those two equations together are called parametric equations, and t is the parameter. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 9.1 · Defining and differentiating parametric equations." Unit 9 · Parametric Equations, Polar Coordinates, and Vector-Valued Functions. A small "BC" tag.

## Plotting a parametric curve
**On screen:** A table with columns t, x = t² − 1, y = t³ − 3t for t = −2, −1, 0, 1, 2: (3, −2), (0, 2), (−1, 0), (0, −2), (3, 2). The points are plotted in order and joined with arrows showing the direction of increasing t, tracing a curve that turns back on itself.
> Here's a curve: x equals t squared minus one, y equals t cubed minus three t. Make a table: for each t, compute x and y. [pause]
> Plot the points in order of t and connect them. The arrows show the direction the point moves as t increases. That direction is called the orientation. [pause]
> Notice the curve passes through x equals zero twice, at different times. As a function of x, it would fail the vertical line test, but as parametric equations it's no problem. [long pause]

## The slope of a parametric curve
**On screen:** Board: the chain rule dy/dt = (dy/dx)(dx/dt); divide by dx/dt; boxed: dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt), provided dx/dt ≠ 0. A small picture: a short step along the curve with horizontal part dx and vertical part dy, both happening in the same tiny time dt.
> How steep is the curve at a point? We want d y d x, but x and y are both given in terms of t. [pause]
> The chain rule says d y d t equals d y d x times d x d t. Divide both sides by d x d t. [pause]
> So the slope is d y d t over d x d t. In a tiny time d t, the point moves d x sideways and d y up, and the slope is rise over run. [long pause]

## A tangent line
**On screen:** The problem: "For x = t² − 1, y = t³ − 3t, find dy/dx and the equation of the tangent line at t = 2." Board, one step per line: dx/dt = 2t; dy/dt = 3t² − 3; dy/dx = (3t² − 3)/(2t); at t = 2: dy/dx = (12 − 3)/4 = 9/4; point: x(2) = 3, y(2) = 8 − 6 = 2; y − 2 = (9/4)(x − 3). The tangent line is drawn on the curve at (3, 2).
> For this curve, find d y d x and the tangent line at t equals two. [try it]
> First the two derivatives with respect to t. D x d t is two t. D y d t is three t squared minus three. [pause]
> Divide: d y d x is three t squared minus three, over two t. [pause]
> At t equals two: twelve minus three is nine, over four. The slope is nine fourths. [pause]
> For the point, plug t equals two into x and y. X is four minus one, three. Y is eight minus six, two. [pause]
> Point-slope form: y minus two equals nine fourths times x minus three. [long pause]

## Horizontal and vertical tangents
**On screen:** The same curve. Board: "horizontal tangent: dy/dt = 0 and dx/dt ≠ 0"; "vertical tangent: dx/dt = 0 and dy/dt ≠ 0"; "both 0: look closer". For this curve: 3t² − 3 = 0 at t = ±1, points (0, −2) and (0, 2), with horizontal segments drawn; 2t = 0 at t = 0, point (−1, 0), with a vertical segment drawn.
> A horizontal tangent has slope zero, so the top of the fraction is zero: d y d t equals zero, while d x d t isn't. [pause]
> A vertical tangent means the bottom is zero: d x d t equals zero, while d y d t isn't. If both are zero at once, you need to look more closely. [pause]
> On our curve, three t squared minus three is zero at t equals plus or minus one: horizontal tangents at zero, negative two and at zero, two. And two t is zero at t equals zero: a vertical tangent at negative one, zero. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "x = x(t), y = y(t): t is the parameter"; "dy/dx = (dy/dt)/(dx/dt)"; "horizontal: dy/dt = 0; vertical: dx/dt = 0".
> Parametric equations give x and y separately in terms of a parameter. The slope is d y d t over d x d t. Horizontal tangents come from d y d t equals zero, and vertical tangents from d x d t equals zero. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Around a circle
**On screen:** "For x = cos t, y = sin t, find dy/dx and the slope at t = π/4." Board: dx/dt = −sin t; dy/dt = cos t; dy/dx = cos t/(−sin t) = −cot t; at t = π/4: −cot(π/4) = −1. The unit circle with the tangent line at (√2/2, √2/2).
> For x equals cosine t and y equals sine t, find d y d x and the slope at t equals pi over four. [pause]
> D x d t is negative sine t, and d y d t is cosine t. [pause]
> Divide: negative cotangent t. At pi over four, cotangent is one, so the slope is negative one. [pause]
> These equations trace the unit circle, and at forty-five degrees the tangent line does slope down at forty-five degrees. [long pause]

## Example 2: Finding the tangents
**On screen:** "Find the points where x = t² − 2t, y = t³ − 12t has horizontal or vertical tangents." Board: dy/dt = 3t² − 12 = 0 at t = ±2, where dx/dt = 2t − 2 ≠ 0; t = 2: (0, −16); t = −2: (8, 16): horizontal; dx/dt = 2t − 2 = 0 at t = 1, where dy/dt = −9 ≠ 0; t = 1: (−1, −11): vertical.
> Find where x equals t squared minus two t, y equals t cubed minus twelve t, has horizontal or vertical tangents. [pause]
> D y d t is three t squared minus twelve, zero at t equals plus or minus two. D x d t isn't zero there, so these are horizontal tangents: at zero, negative sixteen, and at eight, sixteen. [pause]
> D x d t is two t minus two, zero at t equals one. D y d t is negative nine there, not zero. [pause]
> So there's a vertical tangent at negative one, negative eleven. [long pause]

## Example 3: Eliminating the parameter
**On screen:** "Write x = 2t + 1, y = t² as an equation in x and y, and check the slope both ways." Board: t = (x − 1)/2; y = ((x − 1)/2)² = (x − 1)²/4; dy/dx = (x − 1)/2; parametric: dy/dx = (2t)/2 = t = (x − 1)/2. Same.
> Sometimes you can eliminate the parameter. For x equals two t plus one and y equals t squared, solve the first equation for t: t is x minus one, over two. [pause]
> Substitute: y equals x minus one squared, over four. A parabola. [pause]
> Its derivative is x minus one over two. The parametric formula gives two t over two, which is t, and t is x minus one over two. The two methods agree. [long pause]
