# 9.2 Second Derivatives of Parametric Equations

## Bowl or hill?
**On screen:** The parametric curve x = t² − 1, y = t³ − 3t from 9.1. A point slides along it; where the curve bends like a bowl it glows one color, where it bends like a hill another. A question: "concave up or concave down?"
> Last time we found the slope of a parametric curve. Now we want to know which way it bends: like a bowl, concave up, or like a hill, concave down. [pause]
> That's the second derivative, d two y d x squared. [pause]
> There's one trap here that catches almost everyone, so let's go slowly. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 9.2 · Second derivatives of parametric equations." Unit 9 · Parametric Equations, Polar Coordinates, and Vector-Valued Functions. A small "BC" tag.

## Differentiate the slope, then divide again
**On screen:** Board: "d²y/dx² = d/dx (dy/dx)". Then: "dy/dx is a function of t, so use the same rule as for y:" d/dx(anything) = [d/dt(anything)] / (dx/dt). Boxed: d²y/dx² = [d/dt (dy/dx)] / (dx/dt). Then a warning in red: "not (d²y/dt²)/(d²x/dt²)", crossed out.
> The second derivative is the derivative of the slope with respect to x. [pause]
> But the slope, d y d x, is written in terms of t. So differentiate it the same way we differentiated y: take its derivative with respect to t, then divide by d x d t. [pause]
> So d two y d x squared equals d d t of d y d x, all over d x d t. [pause]
> It is not the second derivative of y over the second derivative of x. That shortcut looks natural, and it's wrong. [long pause]

## Concavity at a point
**On screen:** The problem: "For x = t² − 1, y = t³ − 3t, find d²y/dx² and decide the concavity at t = 2." Board, one step per line: dy/dx = (3t² − 3)/(2t) = (3/2)t − (3/2)t⁻¹; d/dt(dy/dx) = 3/2 + (3/2)t⁻²; d²y/dx² = [3/2 + 3/(2t²)] / (2t); = (3t² + 3)/(4t³); at t = 2: (12 + 3)/32 = 15/32 > 0: concave up. Side note in red: the wrong shortcut gives (6t)/2 = 3t = 6.
> For the curve x equals t squared minus one, y equals t cubed minus three t, find the second derivative and the concavity at t equals two. [pause]
> From last time, d y d x is three t squared minus three over two t. Split it to make differentiating easy: three halves t, minus three halves t to the negative one. [pause]
> Its derivative with respect to t: three halves, plus three halves t to the negative two. [pause]
> Now divide by d x d t, which is two t. [pause]
> Multiply top and bottom by two t squared to clean it up: three t squared plus three, over four t cubed. [pause]
> At t equals two: twelve plus three is fifteen, over thirty-two. Positive, so the curve is concave up there. [pause]
> The wrong shortcut would give six t over two, which is six. A completely different number. [long pause]

## Where is it concave up?
**On screen:** The sign of (3t² + 3)/(4t³): the top is always positive, so the sign follows t³. A number line in t with 0 marked: − for t < 0, + for t > 0. The curve with the t > 0 half highlighted as a bowl and the t < 0 half as a hill.
> The numerator, three t squared plus three, is always positive. So the sign of the second derivative is the sign of t cubed, which is the sign of t. [pause]
> For t greater than zero the curve is concave up; for t less than zero, concave down. The number line is in t, because t is the variable everything depends on. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "d²y/dx² = [d/dt (dy/dx)] / (dx/dt)"; "not d²y/dt² ÷ d²x/dt²"; "sign of d²y/dx²: concavity".
> To get the second derivative, differentiate the slope with respect to t, then divide by d x d t again. Never divide the second derivatives of y and x. The sign tells you the concavity. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: The circle again
**On screen:** "For x = cos t, y = sin t, find d²y/dx² at t = π/4." Board: dy/dx = −cot t; d/dt(−cot t) = csc²t; d²y/dx² = csc²t / (−sin t) = −csc³t; at t = π/4: −(√2)³ = −2√2 ≈ −2.83 < 0: concave down. The point (√2/2, √2/2) on the top of the circle.
> For the unit circle, x equals cosine t and y equals sine t, find the second derivative at t equals pi over four. [pause]
> The slope was negative cotangent t. Its derivative is cosecant squared t. [pause]
> Divide by d x d t, negative sine t: negative cosecant cubed t. [pause]
> At pi over four, cosecant is root two, so the second derivative is negative two root two. Concave down, as it should be on the top half of a circle. [long pause]

## Example 2: Always concave up
**On screen:** "For x = eᵗ, y = e⁻ᵗ, find d²y/dx²." Board: dy/dx = −e⁻ᵗ/eᵗ = −e⁻²ᵗ; d/dt(−e⁻²ᵗ) = 2e⁻²ᵗ; d²y/dx² = 2e⁻²ᵗ/eᵗ = 2e⁻³ᵗ > 0 for all t: concave up everywhere. (This is y = 1/x for x > 0.)
> For x equals e to the t and y equals e to the negative t, find the second derivative. [pause]
> The slope is negative e to the negative t over e to the t: negative e to the negative two t. [pause]
> Its derivative is two e to the negative two t. Divide by e to the t: two e to the negative three t. [pause]
> That's always positive, so the curve is concave up everywhere. In fact this curve is y equals one over x, for positive x. [long pause]

## Example 3: An interval of concavity
**On screen:** "For x = t + 1, y = t³ − 3t², for which t is the curve concave up?" Board: dy/dx = (3t² − 6t)/1 = 3t² − 6t; d/dt(dy/dx) = 6t − 6; d²y/dx² = (6t − 6)/1 = 6t − 6; positive when t > 1. A number line in t: − then + at 1.
> For x equals t plus one and y equals t cubed minus three t squared, when is the curve concave up? [pause]
> D x d t is one, so the slope is just three t squared minus six t. [pause]
> Its derivative is six t minus six, and dividing by one changes nothing. [pause]
> Six t minus six is positive when t is greater than one. Concave up for t greater than one. [long pause]
