# 9.4 Defining and Differentiating Vector-Valued Functions

## An arrow that follows the bug
**On screen:** The ladybug moving along its path. An arrow from the origin to the bug stretches and swings as the bug moves: the position vector. A second, shorter arrow rides on the bug, pointing the way it is going: the velocity vector.
> Here's the ladybug again. Draw an arrow from the origin to where it is. As it walks, the arrow stretches and swings to follow it. [pause]
> That arrow is the position vector, written r of t, with components x of t and y of t. [pause]
> Its derivative is another arrow, riding on the bug and pointing the way it's moving: the velocity. [long pause]

## Title
**On screen:** (No narration: the card shows with the intro music.) Title card: "Topic 9.4 · Defining and differentiating vector-valued functions." Unit 9 · Parametric Equations, Polar Coordinates, and Vector-Valued Functions. A small "BC" tag.

## Vectors made of functions
**On screen:** Board: r(t) = ⟨x(t), y(t)⟩; "the same information as the parametric equations x = x(t), y = y(t)". Then: r′(t) = ⟨x′(t), y′(t)⟩, labeled "velocity v(t)"; r″(t) = ⟨x″(t), y″(t)⟩, labeled "acceleration a(t)". Plain words: "differentiate each component".
> A vector-valued function packages two functions into one vector: r of t equals the vector with components x of t and y of t. It holds exactly the same information as the parametric equations. [pause]
> To differentiate it, differentiate each component. The derivative, r prime, is the velocity vector. [pause]
> Differentiate again for the acceleration vector, r double prime. [long pause]

## What the velocity vector means
**On screen:** The path with the velocity vector drawn at one point, tangent to the curve. Labels: "direction: the way the point is moving (tangent to the path)", "length: the speed, |v(t)| = √(x′² + y′²)". Then "slope of the path = y′/x′" connecting to 9.1.
> The velocity vector points along the path, in the direction of motion. It's tangent to the curve. [pause]
> Its length is the speed: the square root of x prime squared plus y prime squared, the same integrand as arc length in 9.3. [pause]
> And the ratio of its components, y prime over x prime, is the slope d y d x from 9.1. Everything we've done fits together. [long pause]

## Velocity and acceleration at a time
**On screen:** The problem: "r(t) = ⟨t², t³ − 3t⟩. Find the velocity, acceleration, and speed at t = 2." Board, one step per line: v(t) = r′(t) = ⟨2t, 3t² − 3⟩; v(2) = ⟨4, 9⟩; a(t) = r″(t) = ⟨2, 6t⟩; a(2) = ⟨2, 12⟩; speed = |v(2)| = √(4² + 9²) = √97 ≈ 9.85. The curve with v(2) drawn as an arrow at (4, 2).
> The position vector is t squared, t cubed minus three t. Find the velocity, acceleration, and speed at t equals two. [pause]
> Differentiate each component: the velocity is two t, three t squared minus three. [pause]
> At t equals two: four, nine. The point is moving right four units per unit of time and up nine. [pause]
> Differentiate again: the acceleration is two, six t. At t equals two: two, twelve. [pause]
> The speed is the length of the velocity: root of sixteen plus eighty-one, root ninety-seven, about nine point eight five. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** "r(t) = ⟨x(t), y(t)⟩"; "v = r′ = ⟨x′, y′⟩: tangent, length = speed"; "a = r″ = ⟨x″, y″⟩"; "differentiate componentwise".
> A vector-valued function has a component for each coordinate. Differentiate componentwise: r prime is the velocity, tangent to the path with length equal to the speed, and r double prime is the acceleration. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: Around the circle
**On screen:** "r(t) = ⟨cos t, sin t⟩. Find v(t) and a(t), and describe their directions." Board: v(t) = ⟨−sin t, cos t⟩; r · v = −cos t sin t + sin t cos t = 0, so v is perpendicular to r (tangent to the circle); |v| = 1; a(t) = ⟨−cos t, −sin t⟩ = −r(t): points to the center. The circle with r, v, a drawn at one point.
> Position is cosine t, sine t: the unit circle. Find the velocity and acceleration, and describe their directions. [pause]
> The velocity is negative sine t, cosine t. It's perpendicular to the position vector, so it's tangent to the circle, and its length is one: constant speed. [pause]
> The acceleration is negative cosine t, negative sine t, which is negative r. It points straight back to the center. [pause]
> Even at constant speed, the direction keeps changing, so there is acceleration. [long pause]

## Example 2: Mixed components
**On screen:** "r(t) = ⟨e²ᵗ, ln(t + 1)⟩. Find v(0) and a(0)." Board: v(t) = ⟨2e²ᵗ, 1/(t + 1)⟩; v(0) = ⟨2, 1⟩; a(t) = ⟨4e²ᵗ, −1/(t + 1)²⟩; a(0) = ⟨4, −1⟩.
> Position is e to the two t, ln of t plus one. Find the velocity and acceleration at zero. [pause]
> The velocity is two e to the two t, one over t plus one. At zero: two, one. [pause]
> The acceleration is four e to the two t, negative one over t plus one squared. At zero: four, negative one. [long pause]

## Example 3: Which way is it moving?
**On screen:** "r(t) = ⟨t² − 4t, t³⟩. At t = 1, is the particle moving left or right? Up or down?" Board: v(t) = ⟨2t − 4, 3t²⟩; v(1) = ⟨−2, 3⟩; x′ < 0: moving left; y′ > 0: moving up.
> Position is t squared minus four t, t cubed. At t equals one, which way is the particle moving? [pause]
> The velocity is two t minus four, three t squared. At one: negative two, three. [pause]
> The x component is negative, so it's moving left. The y component is positive, so it's moving up. [long pause]
