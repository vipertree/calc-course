## The toolkit
**On screen:** A two-column chart built row by row: "position at time b | x(b) = x(a) + ∫ₐᵇ x′(t) dt, same for y"; "velocity vector | ⟨x′(t), y′(t)⟩"; "speed | √(x′² + y′²)"; "distance traveled | ∫ₐᵇ √(x′² + y′²) dt"; "slope of the path | dy/dx = y′/x′"; "moving left / right / up / down | sign of x′ or y′"; "at rest | x′ = 0 and y′ = 0".
> To find position at a later time, start from the known position and add the integral of each velocity component. Do x and y separately. [pause]
> The velocity vector is x prime, y prime, and its length is the speed. [pause]
> Distance traveled is the integral of the speed. It's never the same as the change in position unless the path is a straight line traveled one way. [pause]
> The slope of the path is y prime over x prime. And the signs of x prime and y prime tell you which way the object is moving. It's at rest only when both are zero. [long pause]

## A full motion problem
**On screen:** The problem: "A particle moves in the plane with x′(t) = 2t − 4 and y′(t) = 3t². At t = 0 it is at (1, 0). (a) Find the speed at t = 1. (b) For which t > 0 is it moving left? (c) Find its position at t = 2. (d) Write an integral for the distance traveled from t = 0 to t = 2." Board, one step per line: (a) x′(1) = −2, y′(1) = 3; speed = √((−2)² + 3²) = √13 ≈ 3.61; (b) moving left when x′(t) < 0: 2t − 4 < 0, so 0 < t < 2; (c) x(2) = 1 + ∫₀² (2t − 4) dt = 1 + (4 − 8) = −3; y(2) = 0 + ∫₀² 3t² dt = 8; position (−3, 8); (d) ∫₀² √((2t − 4)² + 9t⁴) dt ≈ 10.468.
> Here's a full problem. The velocity components are two t minus four and three t squared, and the particle starts at one, zero. [try it]
> Speed at t equals one: the components are negative two and three. The speed is root of four plus nine, root thirteen, about three point six one. [pause]
> Moving left means x prime is negative: two t minus four less than zero, so t between zero and two. [pause]
> Position at t equals two: for x, start at one and add the integral of two t minus four from zero to two. That's four minus eight, negative four, so x is negative three. [pause]
> For y, start at zero and add the integral of three t squared: eight. The particle is at negative three, eight. [pause]
> Distance traveled is the integral of speed: the square root of two t minus four squared plus nine t to the fourth, from zero to two. A calculator gives about ten point four seven. [pause]
> Compare that with the straight-line distance between the start and end points: much less. The path curves. [long pause]

## Close
**On screen:** The toolkit chart, compressed: "position: start + ∫ velocity (each component)"; "speed: √(x′² + y′²)"; "distance: ∫ speed"; "slope: y′/x′"; "direction: signs of x′, y′".
> In plane motion, work with each component separately for position, combine them with Pythagoras for speed, integrate speed for distance, and divide for slope. [long pause]

# Worked examples

## Example 1: A calculator problem
**On screen:** "A drone has x′(t) = cos(t²) and y′(t) = e^{t/2}, and is at (3, 1) at t = 0. Find (a) its position at t = 2, (b) its speed at t = 2, (c) the distance it travels for 0 ≤ t ≤ 2." Board: (a) x(2) = 3 + ∫₀² cos(t²) dt ≈ 3.461; y(2) = 1 + ∫₀² e^{t/2} dt ≈ 4.437; (b) speed = √(cos²(4) + e²) ≈ 2.796; (c) ∫₀² √(cos²(t²) + eᵗ) dt ≈ 3.828.
> A drone has velocity components cosine of t squared and e to the t over two, and it starts at three, one. Find its position and speed at t equals two, and the distance it travels. [pause]
> Position: three plus the integral of cosine t squared from zero to two, about three point four six one. And one plus the integral of e to the t over two, about four point four three seven. [pause]
> Speed at two: the square root of cosine of four, squared, plus e squared. About two point seven nine six. [pause]
> Distance: the integral of the speed from zero to two, about three point eight two eight. [long pause]

## Example 2: Slope and tangent line
**On screen:** "A particle has x′(t) = t + 1, y′(t) = t² − 2, and is at (4, 5) at t = 1. Find the slope of its path and the tangent line there." Board: dy/dx = y′/x′ = (1 − 2)/(1 + 1) = −½; y − 5 = −½(x − 4).
> A particle has velocity components t plus one and t squared minus two, and it's at four, five when t is one. Find the slope of its path and the tangent line there. [pause]
> The slope is y prime over x prime: at t equals one, negative one over two. [pause]
> Tangent line: y minus five equals negative one half times x minus four. [long pause]

## Example 3: At rest?
**On screen:** "A particle has x′(t) = t² − 4 and y′(t) = t − 2 for t ≥ 0. When is it at rest? When is it moving straight up or down?" Board: at rest: t² − 4 = 0 and t − 2 = 0, both only at t = 2; vertical motion: x′ = 0 with y′ ≠ 0: t² − 4 = 0 at t = 2, but y′(2) = 0 too, so never.
> A particle has velocity components t squared minus four and t minus two. When is it at rest, and when is it moving straight up or down? [pause]
> At rest means both components are zero. T squared minus four is zero at two, and t minus two is zero at two. So it's at rest at t equals two. [pause]
> Straight up or down means x prime is zero but y prime isn't. The only time x prime is zero is t equals two, and y prime is zero there too. So it never moves straight up or down. [long pause]
